Things we do together

Events were just the example. Together is the whole shelf — everything women do alongside each other: go out, read, play, take something on, watch, wind down. Built so it feels alive even in a small, anonymous community, and warm rather than a scoreboard. This is the deep brainstorm, the pillars I picked, and what's now built into the redesign demo — grounded in cited research on what actually makes people show up together.

The one-line thesis

Belonging comes from doing the same small thing at the same rhythm — not from another feed. So Together is a set of shared activities (rituals, read-along, challenges, a daily game, watch-along, live circles, events), each async-first (works without a crowd online), each showing a warm "the room did X" aggregate, and never a leaderboard. One hub, one calm front door, nothing that ever reads "0 online."

1 · The evidence (verified sources)

What actually makes women show up together

The engineSynchrony & "collective effervescence"

The deepest belonging lever is doing the same small thing at the same rhythm — shared synchronous experience produces spontaneous emotional synchrony and "a sense of emotional unity." Bonding tracks shared emotional intensity, not co-presence — which is why a shared ritual bonds even async.

pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC7423264 · bpspsychub (BJSP 12723) · Royal Society Open Sci 240048
The Wordle lessonOne shared thing · no scoreboard

Wordle works because it's one puzzle a day, same for everyone, spoiler-safe to share, and has NO leaderboard — simultaneous yet comparison-free. That's the template: a shared daily/weekly artefact + no ranking = belonging without pressure.

cnbc.com (Wordle psychology, ABC of needs)
What kills itLeaderboards & flake-shame

Competition demotivates the ~80% who know they won't win, and comparison "works" short-term but makes people "quit things that make them feel chronically guilty." So: no public scoreboards, and a streak-freeze so a missed day never surfaces shame.

commoninja.com · medium/practical-motivation-science · streaksapp.com
The "I'm in"A gentle commitment device

A public "I'm in" is an identity claim that's motivating — but for career/identity goals, over-broadcasting backfires, so keep it soft, specific, to the small group, changeable, penalty-free. Written commitments beat verbal.

finestreak.com/blog/commitment-devices-that-work · nature s41598-024-74878-4 (SDT relatedness)
Async is fineSmall community, no guaranteed crowd

A small anonymous community can't promise people are online now — so default async-first: a shared artefact (one chapter/prompt/challenge), threads that stay warm for latecomers, and aggregate "we" signals. Live formats become fixed-slot rituals (the parkrun formula — you manufacture simultaneity with a standing appointment).

bookclubs.com · teleparty.com · p2pu.org · cmgr.live
2 · The set I picked

Seven pillars of Together

PillarWhy it's in · how it runs
Weekly ritualsDeepest belonging, lowest build, pure async — the heartbeat. Close-the-week + collective-kindness; everyone answers in their own time, then sees the room's aggregate.
Read-alongProven async structure (a thread per chapter-range, spoiler-safe), spans life beyond health. Reuses the book club.
Together challengesA collective goal ("the room did X"), no rank, streak-freeze, soft "I'm in". Whole-life: read 20 mins, one creative thing, text a friend, no-spend week.
Daily gameThe Wordle formula — one shared prompt, no leaderboard. Whole-life prompts (style, would-you-rather), never a cycle quiz.
Watch-alongGossip/joy/entertainment. An async per-episode reaction thread (+ an optional live slot) — never needs a crowd online. (Not a synced watch-party — too heavy.)
Live circlesFixed-slot presence = manufactured simultaneity: co-working/body-doubling, gentle movement, wind-down. Reuses the talk-rooms audio (gated).
Events / meetupsThe 7th pillar — real events women attend together, safe in-person design + ticket links (its own doc: feature #2a).

Whole-life, not clinical: gentle-movement is the only health-tinted pillar; the rest span relationships, career, hobbies, creativity, joy and entertainment.

4 · The hard rule

Never a ghost town

The research is blunt: "you cannot fix a structural collapse with better marketing… digital mansions too big to clean and too empty to feel cozy." Silence is a structure problem. So Together follows the anti-ghost-town rules:

  • Start tight, not sprawling — a handful of strong pillars, not 30 empty rooms.
  • An aggregate front door — "this week, together" + the real pool aggregate greet you, never an empty grid.
  • Seed relentlessly — editorial + Jess prompts so no pillar ever shows zero.
  • Async warmth as the floor — always-on threads + "we" signals so a 2am visitor still feels the room inhabited.
  • Fixed-slot rituals as the heartbeat — a predictable weekly appointment gives a reliable pulse.
  • Never show emptiness as a number — no "0 online", no "1 going" — fuzzy warmth only.
