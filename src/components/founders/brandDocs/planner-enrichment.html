2 · The gaps

What's missing for a real planner

Honest gaps the scan surfaced — concepts a whole-life women's planner wants, that the app has no data or surface for yet:

  • no effort/energy on tasks PersonalTasks/PlannerItems have no estimated duration or energy-cost — so the planner can't weigh a day honestly.
  • no invisible labour Every task is a flat "do" — nothing captures who notices / who owns / who tracks it (the mental load).
  • no household layer Tasks are self-only; no caregiving / family / shared coordination (school run, parent's appointment).
  • no life-admin lane No category for renewals / forms / bills / appointments biased toward doing less.
  • no protected time No "rest block", "do-not-schedule" window, or boundary the plan must respect.
  • recovery not used Sleep + HRV + readiness exist in WearableSync but nothing reads them to suggest a lighter day.
  • no symptom→capacity link Severe cramps don't automatically soften the day's load.
  • rest is leftover No way to schedule rest/recovery as a first-class, protected booking.
3 · The research (cited)

What a women's planner should do

Nine areas, each with the finding, the design implication, and a source. (Two are flagged as popular-practice, not RCT-grade — design with, don't over-claim.)

01Cyclical, energy-aware planning

A 372-woman workplace survey found markedly worse self-rated concentration, energy (81% hit) and mood in the bleed/pre-bleed phases, and better in late-follicular/early-luteal (p≤0.001) — real lived experience to plan with, framed as energy not deficit. Hill's four "seasons" (winter/spring/summer/autumn) is the usable frame. → Suggest a phase-tinted capacity; tag tasks by energy demand; always overridable.

Sources: PMC12398178 (workplace survey) · PMC4241821 (cognition) · Maisie Hill, Period Power flag: popular-practice

02The mental load / invisible labour

Daminger (ASR 2019, 70 interviews) splits cognitive labour into anticipate · identify · decide · monitor — women carry disproportionate anticipation + monitoring, the never-finished parts. Rodsky's Fair Play: hand over Conception→Planning→Execution in full, or the load stays. → Let tasks hold "who notices/owns/tracks"; support full hand-off. The biggest differentiator.

Sources: Daminger, ASR 2019 · Rodsky, Fair Play

03Caregiving & family logistics

Women are the default household coordinators; 65.6% of "sandwich generation" caregivers are women, and half of those mothers have left a job over caregiving demands. The coordination is the labour. → A shared household/coordination layer; caregiving logistics as first-class; the season lowers pressure.

Sources: PMC12288679 (sandwich caregiving) · Caring Across (2019)

04Life admin

Emens (Columbia Law) names "life admin" — bills, forms, renewals, logistics — invisible, uncredited, expanding, landing on women. Her prescription: do less, do better, redistribute. → An admin lane biased toward reduction: "delete / delegate / automate?", not just "when?"

Source: Elizabeth Emens, Life Admin

05Executive-function-friendly

ADHD is widely late-diagnosed in women; core issues are time-blindness and task initiation. Reliable counters: externalising tasks/time and body-doubling. Same support helps luteal/PMDD fog and early postpartum. → Visible durations + gentle countdowns; a "2-minute first step"; a focus/body-double mode; low-capacity-day presets that shrink the plan, never guilt.

Sources: ADDA — time blindness · FLOWN — body-doubling

06Time-blocking vs intention/values

Newport's time-blocking protects deep work but always needs rescheduling; Burkeman's Four Thousand Weeks argues the real skill is deciding what to neglect (finitude). → Lead with intention/most-important-things; offer soft, re-flowable blocks underneath; make "what am I deliberately not doing?" a feature.

Sources: Cal Newport, Time-Block Planner · Burkeman, Four Thousand Weeks

07Energy management, not time

Schwartz & McCarthy (HBR): energy is the real currency, from four sources — body, emotions, mind, spirit — running on ~90-min ultradian rhythms; push past the dip and output degrades. → Tag tasks by energy type; deep work in ~90-min blocks with recovery built in; phase-energy × ultradian-energy = the right task for this hour, this week.

Source: HBR — Manage Your Energy, Not Your Time

08Seasons of life

Women's seasons collide — peak caregiving often overlaps perimenopause. Practitioners frame these as transitions to flex with, favouring small sustainable adjustments. → The planner's baseline ambition should shift with the season; a new-baby / perimenopause / heavy-caregiving mode lowers the default load and reframes success as sustaining.

Sources: Next Avenue — menopause × caregiving · Psychology Today

09Rest & recovery as planned

The Nagoskis' Burnout: burnout is hitting stressors without completing the stress cycle — and completion needs action (movement, sleep, laughter, crying, affection, creativity, connection), not insight. "Human Giver Syndrome" deprioritises women's rest. → Make rest + stress-cycle completers first-class, protected, plannable blocks — not leftovers.

Sources: Nagoski, Burnout · Brené Brown × Nagoski

Top 8 — what we should do that a generic planner doesn't

1Plan to the cycle's seasons, not a flat week (overridable). 2Capture invisible labour, not just visible tasks. 3Be a household coordination layer. 4Treat life-admin as reduction. 5Be executive-function-friendly by default. 6Lead with intention, blocks second; doing-less is a feature. 7Schedule energy, not just hours. 8Make rest a protected booking and flex the bar by life-season.

4 · Proposal

"Reserves" — the Capacity rework

The current "Capacity" board does one thing (a load meter + energy curve). You wanted it to hold more — so make it the board about everything you're working with today, with its own slide-within-card deck.

The name (it should feel broader + warmer)

Reserves ★ recommended Bandwidth Your weather Energy & limits Keep "Capacity"

Why "Reserves": garden-true (the soil you draw on), broader than "capacity", and kinder than a productivity word. "Your weather" is the playful alternate (a day has its weather). Your call — I'd lead with Reserves.

▸ slide within the card — 4 lenses
Lens 1Energy

The phase-season × ultradian curve (today's bright window + dips). "Plant the bold thing in your peak; save admin for the dip." rides existing data

Lens 2Load

What you've planned vs your phase-set ceiling (the meter + 100% line + Ease-it), plus the finitude prompt: "What are you deliberately not doing today?" rides existing

Lens 3Recovery

Last night's sleep + readiness/HRV (from WearableSync) and a gentle "rest debt" read → a one-tap plan a rest block. Rest is a booking, not a leftover. part-new (reads existing wearable/sleep; rest-block needs a small add)

Lens 4Boundaries

Protected/no-plan windows the day must respect, and a low-capacity-day preset that shrinks the plan (for a heavy bleed, a fog day, a hard week) — never guilt. new (protected-time)

6 · Proposal

New elements the research surfaced

★ "The load" — a new board (the differentiator)

The single biggest thing FemWell can do that no generic planner does: hold the invisible work, not just the visible. A distinct board with two lenses — no overlap with the day's task list.

▸ slide within the card — 2 lenses
Lens 1Invisible labour

Capture the mental load — each item can hold who notices · who owns · who tracks, with a "hand it over fully" action (not just "assign the chore"). Surfaces the anticipation/monitoring women carry. needs new fields

Lens 2Life admin

A reduction-biased lane for renewals · forms · bills · appointments, each with the prompt "delete · delegate · automate · or do?" A planner that helps you do less admin. needs new field/category

Smaller folds (each distinct, no duplication)

  • Intentions gains a "This season" lens. Today's intentions stay; a second lens holds what matters this season + "what I'm deliberately not doing" (finitude). rides Intention
  • Energy-type tag on blocks. Each block is already a "kind" (focus/task/life/move/rest) — map it to an energy demand so the day auto-suggests the right task for the phase-week + ultradian peak. small field on PlannerItems
  • Season-of-life mode. A planner setting (new-baby · perimenopause · heavy-caregiving · steady) that lowers the default load and shifts the tone — success becomes sustaining. rides life_stage + a pref
  • EF-friendly day defaults. Visible durations + gentle countdowns, soft re-flow of missed blocks (never a scoreboard), the "2-minute first step". mostly UI

8 · Recommendation

What I'd fold in first

If we want the biggest lift for the least risk, in order:

  • 1. Reserves rework (rename + the 4-lens deck) — mostly rides existing data, instantly richer, and answers your "hold more" ask directly.
  • 2. Rituals → 4-lens deck — adds rest + focus, the soulful/whole-life depth, almost all UI.
  • 3. Intentions "This season" + energy-type tags — tiny, high-meaning.
  • 4. "The load" board — the true differentiator, but the one with real schema cost; preview it seeded, decide the data model deliberately before going live.
  • 5. Season-of-life mode — the frame that makes the whole planner kinder; layer it once the boards exist.

All of it fits the sliding-card + slide-within-card language you liked — depth without crowding. Nothing is built yet — this is the preview to react to before we fold it into /PlannerNewDemo.
