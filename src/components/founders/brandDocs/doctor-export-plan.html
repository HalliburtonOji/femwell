2 · The frontier (cited, 2023–26)

The deeper evidence

Each row flagged evidence / popular-practice / myth/risk. Women-specific.

01The premise is now state-endorsed — women are "gaslit"

The UK government formally accepts women are "let down" and "gaslit"; 84% of women report feeling ignored by a healthcare professional. → Design for the woman who expects not to be believed; the export is leverage, not a courtesy. evidence

Women's Health Strategy (GOV.UK)

02Endometriosis takes ~8y 10m to diagnose — and pain is under-treated

UK endo diagnosis averages ~8 years 10 months (worsening); women wait longer and get less analgesia for equal pain. → A longitudinal pain-and-impact diary is the direct counter to "it's probably nothing". evidence

Endo delay (York 2024) · Gender pain gap (2024)

03Patient data helps as corroboration — but GPs must "recontextualise" it

PGHD improves communication by giving visual evidence for verbal accounts; but GPs treat it as subjective evidence and must do "recontextualisation work" before acting. → The export must do that work FOR the GP — summarise, structure, never dump raw logs. evidence

PGHD communication review · "Data as symptom" (2023)

04Data overload is the core clinician objection — brevity is a safety feature

One patient can generate thousands of unstructured points; GPs reject data dumps. The UK appointment is ~9–11 min — assume ≤2 minutes to read. → The one-page principle is dictated by the real consultation budget. evidence + risk

Data overload · GP consultation length (BJGP)

05Question-prompt lists raise quality WITHOUT lengthening the visit

RCT meta-analysis: prompt lists increase questions asked + shared decision-making, time-neutral. The NHS-endorsed "Ask 3 Questions" (options / benefits-harms / likelihood) is the canonical set; BRAN (Benefits/Risks/Alternatives/Nothing) is the NHS framework for treatment decisions. → Ship Ask-3 as the default prep header; BRAN as a second mode for HRT/surgery decisions. evidence

Prompt-list meta-analysis (2025) · NHS Ask 3 Questions

06Prospective diaries beat recall — by 30–40%

Migraine patients underestimate frequency by 30–40% from memory; a headache diary + interview gives an adequate diagnosis in 97.7% of cases. → A timeline assembled from logged data is MORE accurate than what she can say in the room — lead with it. evidence

Headache-diary review (NICE/NCBI)

07Each condition has a specific data need — map to it

Menopause (NICE NG23, 2024): clinical diagnosis in women 45+, no blood test — foreground vasomotor symptoms + menstrual-change timeline (and don't push FSH). HMB (NG88): defined by impact on QoL — capture life-impact (days lost, flooding), not just flow. PCOS (2023 guideline): Rotterdam 2-of-3, AMH can replace ultrasound — surface cycle-irregularity + androgenic-symptom history. → Condition templates mapped to the exact criteria the GP applies. evidence

NICE NG23 · PBAC/HMB · PCOS 2023

08Validated free PROMs give instant credibility

The Greene Climacteric Scale (21-item, 4-domain, free) is a recognised menopause measure; PBAC / HMB-VAS for bleeding; FUNCAP27 for function (Visible uses it). → Embed a recognised instrument per template — the GP recognises it, so the export reads as clinical, not bespoke. evidence

Greene scale · HMB-VAS

09A red-flag net is ethically essential — and a trust differentiator

Some signs need urgent referral, not a diary — e.g. any postmenopausal bleeding → suspected-cancer pathway. → A safety surfacer that flags red flags and routes to NHS 2-week-wait guidance protects the woman, and signals the tool is responsible. evidence + safety

CRUK 2-week-wait · Macmillan referral guidance

10It's HER data — Art. 20 framing is legally accurate

UK GDPR Art. 20 gives her the right to her data in a portable format; but the NHS App / GP Connect are clinician-to-clinician — third-party apps can't write into the GP record. → Frame the export as "your right to your data"; plan around PDF + the woman as courier, never promise write-back. evidence

ICO data portability · GP Connect

11Competitive teardown — where rivals stop

Flo: one-tap 6-month PDF, generic + cycle-only. Clue: "Connect" shares with trusted people, not a doctor PDF — a clinician-grade export isn't even table stakes in trackers. Migraine Buddy: the two-tier model (diagnostic diary + impact report) worth copying. Visible: embeds the validated FUNCAP27 — proof the "PROM-inside-the-export" model works. Bearable: markets itself explicitly as an anti-medical-gaslighting tool — the philosophical sibling, but generic, not women's/NICE-templated. market

5 · The privacy tension

Secure-share vs the on-device promise

The good news: almost none of +1/+2 needs a new function. Templates, PROM scoring, the timeline, red-flag rules, language toggle, two-tier and most-bothersome are all content + client-side, rendered into the existing on-device pdfmake report. Post-appointment capture is a small additive entity (entities are fine).

  • The one tension — secure-link / QR sharing. A "send my GP a link" feature would mean hosting the PDF on a server — which breaks the on-device, never-uploaded promise that is core to the brand (special-category data, §10). A QR can't hold a whole PDF either.
  • Recommendation: don't build server-side sharing. Keep the on-device PDF + print + copy, and lean into the "your right to your data" (Art. 20) framing — it's legally accurate and more trustworthy than a cloud link. If a same-room digital handoff is ever wanted, explore a local AirDrop/Share-Sheet (device-native, still no server) rather than a hosted link.
  • No new base44 function is required for the whole plan — the Jess brief already rides the existing agent. The only new data object is the post-appointment record (additive entity).

Flag: there is no gated function here — the only decision is the deliberate choice to keep sharing on-device and not add cloud delivery, which I recommend.
