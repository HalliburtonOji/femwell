FemWell · Lifestyle · component #5

The Daily Story — honest, evergreen, immersive

Built + live: one gating contract (the stale/blank bug fixed), an honest chapter-a-day on the finished 30-chapter series, and the real immersive reader. Plus the parked options for a truly fresh daily source. 2026-07-16.

The situation

"The Long Room" (series_key: the_long_room) is 30 real ~500-word chapters, days 1–30 — a genuinely finished story. It ended ~37 days ago and nothing refills the entity. So the app had two bad options and was doing both: pretending a 37-day-old chapter was "Today's chapter", or showing nothing at all. We now do the honest third thing.

1 · The bug — five surfaces, five different questions fixed

The audit found three readers. There were five — and the live Today page wasn't the one named (it's TodayClipboardDemo, not TodayDemo6).

The fix: ONE contract — src/components/lifestyle/dailyStory.js. Every surface now calls chapterForDay() and gets the same chapter, framed honestly. Never stale-as-today; never blank. The reader keeps its own whole-series load on purpose — that is the straight-through mode.

2 · The evergreen model — honest, not a trick

Deterministic by calendar day: ((daysSinceEpoch − anchor) mod 30) → every woman is on the same chapter today, it advances one a day, and it cycles. No randomness, no drift across devices or reloads.

Framed truthfully — the sheet says, in as many words: “A chapter a day from ‘The Long Room’ — a finished story you can also read straight through.” The eyebrow reads “Chapter 7 of 30”, never "fresh today" unless it genuinely is.

Graceful upgrade built in: a chapter genuinely published for today always wins over the evergreen pick. So if the series is ever refilled (any option below), this module needs no change — it simply stops falling back. The evergreen is a floor, not a ceiling.

The kind return model (no streak-shame)

  • Where she's up to — "You've read 9 of 30 so far." A count, never a streak, never a scold for a gap.
  • Resume — the reader persists position + bookmarks per-book (fw_reader_pos_daily_the_long_room), so it reopens where she left off.
  • A gentle tease — "Tomorrow: chapter 8." An invitation, not a countdown.
  • Read means one thing app-wide — the same fw_read_chapter_<id> key the Today reel already used, so the surfaces agree.
  • Mark as read → the garden — via the existing recordProgress() (component #3's contract). Reaching a chapter in the immersive reader records it too.

The immersive read

"Read straight through" opens the real DailyStoryReader full-screen at today's chapter — the measured pagination + page-flip + movie mode + themes + bookmarks (components #3/#4's craft), reading through the shared ReadingColumn. Reused, not duplicated.

3 · PARKED — a truly fresh daily source needs Halli

Not built. The evergreen makes the page honest and never-empty today; these are the routes to genuinely new writing.

(a) Author / seed more chapters no backend

Write (or commission) a second series and seed DailyStory rows with real published_dates. Zero code changes — the gate already prefers a genuinely-fresh chapter. Cheapest, highest quality, entirely a content decision. Mx Storyteller is the named owner for long-form fiction.

(b) A generator + cron NEW function + AI cost + sign-off

A nightly job writing tomorrow's chapter. Costs: a new function (the 50-fn cap is already blown — ~118 dirs), recurring AI spend, plus a cron/notify path that's on the parked list. Quality risk: serialized fiction that stays coherent over 30+ chapters is exactly what LLMs drift on — and a bad chapter is worse than a re-read. Note: a fiction pipeline already exists (generateFemwellFiction, generateNextChapter, fictionWorker) but it writes LifestyleItems FICTION with chapters_json — not DailyStory. Reusing it beats writing a new one.

(c) Repoint at StoryPack/StoryItem doesn't fit — checked

I checked the schemas rather than assume. StoryPack is per-user (user_id, day_key) and StoryItem carries pack_id, slot_index, cta_label — that's the Today story-slots subsystem (Instagram-style daily cards), not serialized fiction. Repointing would mean rebuilding it into something it isn't. Recommend dropping (c).

(c′) The better third option: point the Daily Story at the existing FemWell fiction in LifestyleItems (FEMWELL_FICTION_*, real chapters_json) — already generated, already rendered by FictionReader. A chapter-a-day could draw from that library with no new function. Worth a look before (b).

Recommendation: (a) for quality, (c′) for breadth — both avoid a new function. Hold (b) unless you want AI-written serial fiction as a product bet.
