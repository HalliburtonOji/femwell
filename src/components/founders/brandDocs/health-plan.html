Letters, properly

You love the health letters — and you're right to. They're not off-brand as an idea; they're off-brand in their rendering. This is the plan to keep the beloved letters and give them the elite card language — plus the research on what the Health room, the clinical corner of the app, should actually hold. The one-line thesis

The letter is the content; the sliding card is the vessel. Keep the warm "Dear you" voice — render it as FwFloraHero + SummaryCard + a ClipboardSlider of StackedCard letter-boards with oxblood headings and living flora. Then grow Health from seven editorial letters into the credible clinical room that ties cycle → Pulse → Doctor Export.

The headline moves

  • Keep the letters, change the vessel. The "Please Write to Me" letter format is an evidence-based UK clinical movement — the fix is the elite card language, not a rewrite.
  • Grow the hub to the real territory. Mirror the Renewed Women's Health Strategy taxonomy + the under-served conditions (endo · PCOS · fibroids · PMDD · thyroid) + the one gap no rival covers: heart health.
  • Close the loop: Health (understand) → Pulse (the trend) → Doctor Export (the one-pager you hand over) — with a letter's "add to my GP prep" feeding it. And privacy as a headline feature.

2 · The evidence (cited)

The letters are right — keep them

Before redrawing them, the case for keeping the letter format: it's a named UK clinical best-practice, not a gimmick.

01"Please Write to Me" is an official UK movement

The Academy of Medical Royal Colleges guidance (refreshed Feb 2026) tells clinicians to write letters directly to the patient, in the second person, plain English, no jargon — approved by the Plain English Campaign. → FemWell's "Dear you" letters can honestly cite "in the spirit of the Royal Colleges' Please Write to Me". evidence

AoMRC — Please Write to Me (2026)

02Plain letters aid recall + ownership

Patients report forgetting what the doctor said by the time they get home; a letter you can re-read (and show another clinician) supports informed, shared decisions. → Frame each letter as a keepsake you can re-read and bring to an appointment. evidence

GMC summary

03The literacy case is large and quantified

43% of working-age adults in England can't understand textual health info (61% with numbers); UK average reading age ~9–11. → Write letter bodies at reading-age 9–11; a warm low-reading-age letter beats clinical bullet points for most women. evidence

NHS HEE — health literacy

04Narrative helps people understand a diagnosis

Narrative medicine / bibliotherapy — reading a "someone like you" account — increases knowledge and meaning, and builds trust. → Let a letter optionally carry one short, anonymised "someone like you" line, not only facts. evidence

Narrative-based medicine (PMC)

So the redesign is a rendering job, not a rewrite: keep the voice + the "Dear you" + the plain-English discipline; change the vessel to the elite card language (§3). One caution from the evidence: warmth must never bury a red flag — urgent things use the safe-by-design pattern in §5.

4 · The clinical territory (cited)

What the Health hub should hold

Today's 7 letters are a strong spine. The research says where to deepen — anchored to the Renewed Women's Health Strategy for England (April 2026) and the women's-health-gap evidence.

01The gap is being dismissed, not just under-informed

The UK has the G20's largest female health gap; 80% of women feel they haven't been listened to. → Make "being heard / being prepared" the hub's emotional spine — the "second opinion in your pocket" letter that validates + arms her with NICE-aligned questions. evidence

Nuffield Trust

02Mirror the Strategy's taxonomy

The 2026 Strategy names: menstrual & gynae conditions, fertility & loss, maternity, menopause, mental health, cancers — and adds a menopause question to the NHS Health Check (40–74). → Use this as the hub's section spine — the canonical UK list + a Doctor-Export credibility anchor. evidence

GOV.UK — Renewed Strategy 2026

03First-class rooms for the under-served conditions

Endometriosis now averages 9 years 4 months to diagnose (~11 for diverse ethnic communities); fibroids hit Black women younger and harder; 62% were unsatisfied with treatment info. → Give endo · PCOS · fibroids · PMDD · adenomyosis · thyroid their own letter-boards, ethnicity-aware — exactly what the system under-teaches. evidence

Endometriosis UK · RCOG — fibroids

04The standout gap no rival covers: heart health

Coronary heart disease kills roughly twice as many UK women as breast cancer, yet women are more often misdiagnosed and present atypically (jaw/back pain, nausea, breathlessness). → A heart-health letter is a genuine differentiator — almost every women's app omits CVD. evidence

BHF — heart attacks in women

The fuller spine (11 stages): menstrual · contraception · fertility/TTC · pregnancy & postpartum · peri/menopause — plus an under-served-conditions room, pelvic health/continence, bone health, heart health, breast & cervical awareness, mental health, sexual health.

5 · Safe by design

Screening reminders & red-flags

A gentle prevention layer is high-value and low-risk — it signposts, never diagnoses.

CRUK — cervical 5-yearly · NHS Health Check

Red-flags — calm "worth a check", never interpretation

The MHRA regulates on intended purpose: "input symptom → NHS resource" is fine; "input symptom → what it might mean" is a medical device. So every red flag is signposting: postmenopausal bleeding, a new breast lump/change, severe/sudden pelvic pain, persistent bloating, unexplained intermenstrual/postcoital bleeding → a calm "this is always worth getting checked — here's how" card + one-tap "add to GP prep". Never diagnose, never give a probability, never imply we replace care. Tone: "most causes are not serious, but this one should always be checked." safety rail

Macmillan — referral pathways · MHRA / SaMD guidance

6 · The differentiated loop

Health → Pulse → Doctor Export

The value over "just tracking" is the closed loop — and it's the thing the research says clinicians actually want.

StepWhat it does
Health (understand)The letters explain; "add to my GP prep" + red-flag cards collect what matters.
Pulse (the trend)Symptom/cycle patterns over time (already elite) — surfaces exactly the under-diagnosed-condition signals.
Doctor Export (the one-pager)A structured, dated, editable, plain-English summary (frequency · severity · duration · cycle-day) — clinician-readable, answering the "patient data isn't reliable" objection. Beats Balance's Health Report by spanning all 11 stages.

Privacy as a headline feature

Post-Meta/Flo (a 2025 jury found Meta illegally collected Flo's reproductive data), period-app privacy is a live fault line. → Lead with UK-GDPR: anonymous-safe by default, no third-party ad SDKs on health data, export/delete in plain sight — and say so in the Health room footer. differentiator

7 · Honest build reality

What this takes

  • Rule-safe (no new function): the whole redesign is a re-render of existing content into SliderKit + FwFloraHero — reuses the components already live on 5 pages. The new letters (heart, endo, etc.) are editorial content. Screening tracker + "your results over time" + GP-prep are small additive entities (e.g. ScreeningRecord, GpPrepItem) — additive, no new function.
  • Reuses real data already wired: SymptomLogs, DailyCheckins, HydrationLog, HabitLogs, cycle/phase — Health already reads these; the loop just routes them to Pulse + Doctor Export.
  • Clinical-safety pass required: two figures flagged by research need a human/clinical check before shipping copy (the postmenopausal-bleeding-to-cancer rate vs NICE NG12; the exact Strategy menopause-Health-Check wording). And all red-flag copy should get a §5 safe-by-design review.
  • Demo-first, like the others: build a /HealthLettersDemo (seeded) → FoundersOS → your approval → swap live with a one-line revert, exactly as Pulse/Nutrition/Garden shipped.
8 · Recommendation

  • 1. Re-render the 7 letters in the elite card language (FwFloraHero + SummaryCard + ClipboardSlider of StackedCard letter-boards, oxblood, flora) — the core of what you asked for, all reskin + reuse, zero rule-risk.
