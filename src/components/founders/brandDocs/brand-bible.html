One · The soul

Your life, in bloom.

A woman’s life is a garden, not a chart.

It has seasons — the cycle’s inner winter, spring, summer and autumn. It has long arcs — from the teen daisy to the elder magnolia. It has many beds, not one — career, friendship, love, money, rest, joy, identity. And it is tended, not optimised.

FemWell is the garden that remembers what bloomed where. Every flower means something true; a butterfly arrives only when you genuinely return or change; and the garden is unmistakably yours. The app doesn’t track you — it grows with you.

The meaning — flowers as a language

The flora is not decoration; it is a symbol system. Each flower is chosen for documented meaning (Victorian floriography + folk-herbalism + myth) and means the same thing everywhere it appears — garden, cycle, journal, chapters. A sunflower is always radiance; a forget-me-not is always memory; a butterfly always means you changed and came back. Because every surface draws from one dictionary, a returning woman reads the app fluently.

The positioning — the warm lane no one else owns

Femtech runs from “reflexive pink” straight into clinical neutrality (Clue, Elvie: teal, geometric, data-first). FemWell runs the other way — into editorial warmth: cream, gold and crimson; a serif voice; a living botanical world. That warm-but-not-pink, beautiful-but-not-clinical middle is documented white space, and it is ours.

Health is one room, not the house. FemWell is a whole-life wellness app for women — relationships, career, money, friendship, joy and identity, with health as one room among many. Breadth of life carries the femininity, not pink.

The personality — a warm friend who knows things

Brand archetype: a Caregiver core, tinted by the Sage, with a touch of the Innocent’s joy. The Caregiver is the warm, nurturing spine; the Sage keeps it from going saccharine (the app genuinely knows things — cycle science, real meaning); the Innocent keeps it lighthearted by default. Every copy, design and feature decision answers to one question:

“A warm friend who knows things and keeps it joyful.”

Two · Voice & tone

Speak like a smart friend.

Warm, plain, a little witty. UK English (NHS, £, en-GB). Never clinical, never cold, never a scoreboard, never guilt. Lighthearted by default; let life-stage gently tint the tone, never dominate it. No emoji, ever.

Do
“Rough night? Let’s keep today gentle. Here are three small things that tend to help.”
Warm, specific, optional. A friend, not a chart.
Don’t
“You missed your hydration goal 3 days in a row. Streak lost.”
Scoreboard, guilt, clinical. Off-brand.
Do — empty state
“Nothing here yet — and that’s fine. Plant the first line whenever you like.”
Don’t
“No data available.”

The garden has weather, not streaks

We never punish a gap. A hard week is “a frost the garden came through,” not a broken streak. Engagement language is seasonal and forgiving — the hellebore blooms through the cold.

The rules that end the “fonts are a mess” problem

  • Ephesis (script) is for short page titles only — never all-caps, never long; the script tangles.
  • Cormorant (serif) carries every heading and all reading body.
  • System sans is chrome only (eyebrows, captions, controls, nav, dates).
  • Never set fontFamily:'Inter' or 'Fraunces' expecting a sans — in this app they are remapped to Cormorant. Use the real system stack for sans.
  • Every text node maps to a role below. No free-floating font sizes; no new sizes; no half-pixels.

Phase hues (a separate semantic set): menstrual #BC2E27 · follicular #8FAF8F · ovulatory #D4AF37 · luteal #8E6E8E (plum). These read as the cycle’s seasons and never swap with chrome gold.

Accessibility — the hard rule

Warm palettes go muddy and fail contrast when text uses mid-tones. So:

Body & small text
ink, inkSoft or muted only (near-black on cream clears WCAG AA 4.5:1).
Never for body / small captions
gold, crimson, sage, blush — they risk failing AA on cream. Reserve them for large display, accents, borders & icons.

Anti-muddiness law: one clean light ground + ONE saturated accent (crimson) + ONE deep anchor (ink). Retire the legacy duplicates (#D4AF37 as chrome, #9B8B7A, #3A2C1A, #F4EDDB) — those mid-brown clutterers are what make warm palettes read cheap.

Five · The centerpiece

The flora & illustration system.

This is the brand. If one thing must be right, it is this: a rose must read as a rose.

The three heroes — recognisable at a glance

Each hero carries its own signature: the rose = layered reflexed guard petals coiling into a visible spiral heart; the sunflower = ray florets around a real phyllotaxis seed-disc; the hibiscus = five broad veined petals, a deep throat, and the projecting staminal column tipped with five stigma lobes.

Variety you can see — not one flower recoloured

The old failure was every flower sharing one radial silhouette, so only colour changed. The standard now is bespoke geometry per species — silhouette first, then colour. A peony is a full ruffled pompom; a dahlia is a sharp geometric star; a tulip is a closed goblet; a poppy is broad papery petals around a dark boss.

The craft standard (the “wow” bar)

  • Generous scale. A bloom is a centrepiece, not a tiny sticker. Small renders kill recognisability.
  • Bespoke silhouette per species — never one ring of identical petals for everything.
  • Tonal depth: multi-stop gradients running lit-tip → shadowed-throat; layered rings step in tone (outer in shadow, inner crown lightest); a soft throat occlusion seats the petals.
  • Fine edge linework separates petals — this is what reads as illustration rather than blob.
  • Pale blooms hold their edge: cream, blush and sky are deepened at the base so they don’t melt into the cream page.
  • One soft grounding shadow per bloom — the single permitted blur. Never blur petals.
  • Motion = breath, not bounce: a gentle scale-breath + multi-axis sway + dew shimmer, GPU-only, staggered, and always reduced-motion-gated.

Pollinators — earned, never ambient, and ON the plant

A creature visits a real moment, one at a time, then drifts off — it is the brand’s punctuation, not its wallpaper. Butterfly = transformation / return · moth = the night / rest / menstrual dark · dragonfly = insight (Pulse) · bee = community · ladybird = luck (Nutrition). When shown, the creature rests physically on a petal or leaf, never floating beside the flower.

Combinations — a bouquet makes a sentence

Pairing flowers compounds meaning (forget-me-not + rosemary = remembrance; sunflower + daisy = cheerful friendship). Surfaces may show a posy of different blooms, not one lone flower — variety made visible.

Per-user uniqueness — the flora fingerprint

A garden is a portrait, not a slot machine. Each woman’s flora is deterministically seeded from who she is and what she tends (profile + life-stage + phase + the life-areas she tends + what she’s earned) — so it’s the same on every device and recognisably hers, yet grows as she does. The permutation space is effectively unbounded (≈1.4 billion gardens), so no two repeat. Rarity is earned, never bought: a second species, then a rare bloom or creature visit, then the flowering tree and — across the community — the orchard.

Page character — one identity, different per page

Each surface gets a flora signature (a palette lean + a signature species + a creature) drawn from the one dictionary — Journal: gold + willow + moth · Community: sage + clover + bee · Nutrition: blush-green + herbs + ladybird · Cycle/Health: the phase hue + phase-flower + dragonfly · Garden: the full palette. Character changes the accent & signature only — never the bones.

Six · The card system

Cards are the language.

A card is never an empty or dumb container. Build every card from the shared family (brand/Card.jsx) — never hand-roll a <div> card. The shell is the Today “across your day” card, standardised: 365×488, a gradient + 4px accent left-rim, a four-corner sprig frame, radius 20.

Every card carries, at a glance

  • An icon disc + eyebrow (the type/section) and a meaning-bloom.
  • A hook (serif 20) — the one line that earns the card — and a supporting line.
  • An inline action: media plays in the card; you log / tick / answer in place.
  • A deep-link that opens the exact item full-screen — never a parent list to sift.

Typed variants: Article · Story · Video · Audio · Book · DailyStory · Horoscope · Summary · Recommendation · LogAction. Smart, signal-driven picks that change over time, with a warm fallback — never a blank box or a dead “coming soon.”

Eight · Components, motion & icons

The fittings.

The carved heart

The brand mark is the carved crimson heart — always #BC2E27, never recoloured, never outline-only, never the Lucide outline heart. Exactly one per surface — it is the single colour pop, in a primary header and the footer signature. Not scattered on every card.

Botanical motifs

A small disciplined library of hairline, stroke-only line-art (leaves, corners, dividers, flourishes). One motif per fold, low opacity, never behind reading text, never blurred. The premium-vs-clipart test, every time: growing/asymmetric? varied species? hairline stroke-only? one per fold?

Buttons, chips, sheets, nav

Buttons are accent-driven (solid crimson/section-accent primary, white text, sans 14/700; bordered secondary). Chips: radius 999, sans 13/600, accent-fill when selected. Bottom-sheets: paperHi, top-rounded, slide-up over a warm scrim. One unified floating glass capsule bottom nav at every viewport; Jess is a crimson cosmos bloom in the row. Icons are Lucide / inline SVG only — never emoji.

Nine · Before you build

The 60-second gate.

  1. Type — every node maps to a role. No free font sizes, nothing below 12px.
  2. Colour — only the tokens. One gold, one crimson, one cream. Body text near-black only.
  3. Heart — exactly one carved crimson heart on a primary header/signature.
  4. Motif — at most one hairline botanical per fold, never behind text.
  5. Flora — the canonical bloom; recognisable species, generous scale, breath-not-bounce, reduced-motion-gated.
  6. Cards — imported from brand/Card.jsx, never hand-rolled; never empty; inline action + exact-item deep-link.
  7. Page — flora hero → one summary card → rich content, all on PAPER_BG.
  8. Always — cream/ink world, Ephesis + Cormorant + system sans, Lucide/SVG icons, no emoji, UK English, no scoreboards, no guilt.

If a screen reads as plain, monochrome or sparse, it is off-brand — push it richer. Restraint here means cohesion and craft, not emptiness. FemWell is a flourishing garden, not a minimalist white app.
