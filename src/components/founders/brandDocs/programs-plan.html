2 · The frontier (cited, 2023–26)

The deeper evidence

Each row flagged evidence / popular-practice / myth/risk. Women-specific where it matters.

01Completion is the outcome — dose-response is real

More sessions completed → better outcomes across digital therapies. → The programme's job is to maximise sessions-done, not sessions-offered; everything below serves completion. evidence

Dose-response (PMC 2024)

02Guided beats unguided — roughly double the effect

Human/coach support ~doubles effect sizes (stress g=0.76 vs 0.27; depression 0.48 vs 0.23). Even a peer/buddy/light touch helps. → A check-in layer is the single highest-leverage upgrade — and Jess can be the light guide without a clinician on every journey. evidence

Guided vs unguided meta-analysis · Supportive Accountability (2025)

03The dropout is brutal — even for the gold standard

The "Law of Attrition" makes heavy early dropout the norm; NHS Couch-to-5K — free, branded, beautifully structured — saw only 27% complete, most quitting weeks 2–5. → Front-load support in the first fortnight; treat partial completion as normal and still valuable. evidence

Law of Attrition · Couch-to-5K completion

04Cohorts are the strongest completion lever there is

Peer-accountability cohorts hit 71–90% engagement vs 15–18% solo. → "Start-together" monthly intakes with a shared, anonymous-safe progress wall. evidence

Cohort engagement data

05Streaks past ~90 days become shame — the dark pattern to avoid

Streaks work via loss aversion but losing a long one drives outright abandonment; engagement-maximising mechanics risk retention over benefit. → FemWell's existing "no streaks to break" is correct; add streak-freezes/grace + recovery framing, never "you ruined it". risk

Streak abandonment

06Habits take ~2 months, not 21 days — graduate accordingly

Habit formation median 59–66 days (range 4–335); helped by self-chosen habits, stable cue, enjoyment, implementation plans. → The final session converts the programme into ONE user-chosen, cue-anchored habit on Today — bridging programme-end to lasting change. evidence

Habit-formation meta-analysis 2024

07The reliable mechanics: goal → self-monitor → feedback, tailored

The best-evidenced behaviour-change technique combo is goal-setting + self-monitoring + feedback together; success is targeted selection, not piling on mechanics (Michie BCT taxonomy underpins NICE). → Build every journey on goal→track→feedback; works for clinical AND whole-life programmes. evidence

BCT taxonomy

08Micro-sessions out-complete long modules

Bite-sized (3–7 min) content completes far better than 30-min lessons; reminders timed to behaviour (48h idle) beat fixed schedules, and 39% disable notifications when over-alerted. → Micro-session days + behaviourally-timed, restrained reminders. practice

Bite-sized completion

09The clinical spine is NICE-grade — credible programmes exist

Menopause: NICE NG23 (2024) recommends menopause-CBT for vasomotor/sleep/mood. Insomnia: digital CBT-I (Sleepio) is NICE-recommended, non-inferior to face-to-face. PCOS: 2023 guideline makes lifestyle first-line (body-neutral, no superior diet). Pelvic floor: Squeezy (+40% adherence). Postpartum/PMDD: iCBT evidenced. → Anchor the clinical journeys to these; frame honestly (coping skills, not cures). evidence

NG23 · Sleepio/CBT-I · PCOS 2023

10Don't over-claim cycle-synced programming

2023 reviews find no influence of menstrual phase on acute strength or training adaptation. → Offer cycle-aware comfort tinting (gentler days, optional) — never claim cycle-synced programming improves results. myth

Cycle-phase & training review

11Measure outcomes, not opens

Dose-response + the law of attrition both demand outcome measurement; it's also the £1M-sale story ("N women improved their sleep by X"). → A short validated pre/post check-in per journey (ISI sleep, GAD-7/EPDS mood, ICIQ pelvic). evidence

Outcome measurement

12Competitive teardown — the split

Sleepio / Squeezy / C25K / Balance: evidence-grade but single-domain. Flo / Headspace: whole-life-ish but un-coached, un-cohorted content feeds. Noom: has the coach+cohort engine — but weight-centric and pricey. → No one pairs NICE-grade clinical journeys with light coach + cohort accountability across the whole of a woman's life. That's the lane. market

5 · The gated calls

Push delivery & human coaching need your OK

Most of +1/+2 is rule-safe: the Jess guide rides the existing agent, cohorts/PROMs/graduation are additive entities + content. Two things need a decision:

  • 1 · Real reminder delivery (notifications). Today reminder_time is stored but there's no sign it fires. Actually delivering it (web-push / scheduled notification) needs notification infrastructure — likely a small service/function and OS permission. The no-infra fallback: in-app "due today" surfacing + a calendar/Today nudge (no push). gated infra
  • 2 · Human coaching. The evidence favours human support, but a human coach is a cost/ops decision, not code. The strong middle path: the Jess guide (rule-safe) delivers most of the "supportive accountability" benefit at zero marginal cost; reserve human coaching for a premium Pro tier if you want it. ops/cost

Flag: notification delivery is the one code-level gated item (with an in-app fallback). Human coaching is a business decision — recommend Jess-as-guide first. No new base44 function is required for cohorts, PROMs, graduation or content.

7 · Honest build reality

What's rule-safe vs what needs a call

  • Rule-safe (no new function): the Jess guide (existing agent), catch-up/rest mechanic + streak-freeze (state), habit graduation (writes an existing habit), pre/post PROMs (content + small entity), cohorts (a cohort entity + the Community path), whole-life programme content, adaptive pacing (rules). All ride the existing Programs/UserPrograms/ProgramDays/ProgramTasks engine.
  • Needs your OK (infra): real push-notification delivery (§5.1) — or ship the in-app/Today-nudge fallback with zero rule-risk.
  • Needs your OK (ops/cost): human coaching (§5.2) — recommend Jess-as-guide first.
  • Biggest real effort: authoring NICE-grade clinical content + the whole-life library — content, not engineering.
  • Avoid: long modules (use micro-sessions), shame streaks, cycle-synced performance claims.
