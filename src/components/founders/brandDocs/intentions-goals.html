One · The loop

One gentle circle.

The three features aren’t three things — they’re one daily-to-seasonal loop, and the garden is the shared ledger of all of it:

Goal (a perennial)→ Today’s intention→ Line of the day frames it→ You live the day→ Evening reflection waters it→ The garden grows→ A milestone blooms→ Seasonal review re-plants

A goal suggests today’s intention (“tend this by…”). The line of the day gives it soul. You carry it. The evening reflection waters it — and the plant in your garden grows a little. Over a season, milestones bloom; Pulse reflects the pattern; a seasonal review re-plants. No step nags. Every step is optional.

Two · No new nav tab

Baked into the pages you already have.

Each piece lives where its moment already is. One shared “Intentions & goals” door is added to the Jump-to switcher on the relevant pages — so it’s always reachable, but never a fifth nav icon.

Today
Set the morning intention (the day’s first card / the “start my day” curtain) · the line of the day (the almanac moment) · one contextual “tend a goal” nudge · the evening reflection.
Planner
The intention sits above the agenda as the day’s “why.” Goals break into tiny next-actions that drop into the day. Seasonal goals get a gentle week-by-week arc.
Garden
The living view of everything — intentions as sprouts, short-term goals as flowering plants, long-term goals as perennials/a young tree, milestones as buds opening. This is where progress is seen, as growth.
Profile
“What you’re growing” — the goal editor, framed by identity (“I’m becoming someone who…”) across whole-life domains. Set, edit, retire, review.
Journal
The evening reflection can become a pressed-flower entry. “Tell the garden” (v4 ritual) is a soft way to voice an intention or a hope.
Pulse
Gentle, no-guilt reflection of the pattern — “how your garden’s growing,” by domain and season. Never a scoreboard.

RULE 2 honoured: intentions/goals span all of life — career, money, friendship, love, creativity, joy, identity, home — not just health. Health is one bed in the garden, not the garden.

Three · Daily intentions

Plant the day.

Set — a 10-second morning ritual

On Today (or the “start my day” curtain): “What do you want to plant today?” — a few smart suggestions (tied to her phase, her nearest goal, yesterday) + free text. Choosing one plants a seed in the garden. Intentions are present & values-led (“be patient with myself,” “make the brave call”), not a to-do.

Set · suggestions (phase-tinted, whole-life)

“be brave about the work thing” · “go gently — it’s a rest week” · “actually reply to her” · “make something, badly, for fun” · “protect one hour for me”

Carry — it rides the day

The intention shows as a slim band on Today (“today: go gently”), tints the line of the day and nudges, and Jess knows it. It’s a quiet compass, not a task with a tick.

Reflect — water it at night (no guilt)

Evening (only if she planted one): “How did go gently grow today?” — three soft options, never a pass/fail:

Reflect · the three options

It bloomed — “Lovely. That one’s flowering in your garden now.”

It half-grew — “Half is growth. Plants don’t open in a day.”

It rested — “Some seeds wait for better weather. No ledger here. Plant it again tomorrow if it still matters.”

A kept intention adds a bloom to today’s row in the garden; a rested one is just unplanted soil — never red, never a broken streak. A week of intentions becomes a little flowering bed you can look back on.

Four · The line of the day

The garden’s voice, just for her.

Not a generic quote-of-the-day. A user-specific line assembled by the v4 omen/voice engine from her signals — her intention, her nearest goal, her phase & lifecycle stage, the date & season, a gentle omen — in the soulful “warm + funny + a little witch” voice. Always meaningful; graceful seasonal fallback; never hollow-cute.

“A snowdrop morning — and you said you’d be brave about the work thing. Good. They say the first bird of the day carries the news, so keep your phone close. The brave ones get the calls.”
“Rest week, and you planted ‘go gently.’ The garden agrees — even the bees are having a lie-in. Do less, on purpose.”
“Three weeks of tending that little courage-plant and it’s about to bud. Today’s a watering day, nothing more. Small and steady wins this one.”

Every goal lives in a life domain — career · money · relationships · friendship · health · creativity · learning · joy/adventure · home · identity — and each domain is a bed in the garden with its own signature flora (career = gladiolus, friendship = sunflower+daisy, identity = orchid, and so on from the v4 floriography map).

The shape of a goal (identity-led, not just outcome)

A goal card carries

The becoming — “I’m becoming someone who finishes what she starts.” (identity framing beats outcome framing for sticking power)

The why · the season (a gentle ~12 weeks) · the domain → flora · milestones (stepping-stones / buds) · the next tiny action (what drops into today).

Planting a goal — the WOOP ritual (the bit that actually makes goals stick)

Creating a goal is a 4-step planting: Wish → Outcome → Obstacle → Plan. Picture the win, name the real obstacle, and set an “if-then” plan (“if it’s a low-energy evening, then I’ll do the five-minute version”). The obstacle+plan step is the evidence-backed difference between a wish and a goal — and it’s a lovely “prepare the soil” moment.

Progress without guilt — identity votes, not streaks

We replace the streak counter entirely. Every tended action is “a vote for the woman you’re becoming” (the research’s stickiest, guilt-free mechanic) — and each vote grows the plant. Progress is how far it has grown, never a shaming % bar. A missed stretch is a dry spell the plant held; “never miss twice” becomes “water it again tomorrow.” Milestones bloom as you pass them. The plant rests, it never wilts.

Cyclical — the garden lifecycle is her cycle

The v4 lifecycle maps 1:1 onto the menstrual cycle, so seasonal goal-work and the flora speak the same language:

Cycle phaseLifecycleGoal-work (a gentle tint, never a rule)

Ovulatorybloompush & be bold — the big ask, the launch
Lutealseedorganise & harvest — tidy, refine, finish

A seasonal review ritual (a Sunday / new-moon / cycle-start “tend the garden”) looks back kindly and re-plants for the next season — a ~12-week arc as the spine, phase-tinted for effort. Reaching goals over years grows your orchard. (Cycle-syncing is offered as a gentle lean, not enforced — the productivity claims aren’t RCT-backed.)

Six · The evidence

Grounded, not guessed.

Every choice here is evidence-backed (full citations: workspace/GOALS_INTENTIONS_RESEARCH_2026-06-20.md). The eight buildable mechanisms and where each lands:

What the research says to AVOID (each flagged in the file): red streak counters & “you broke your streak” guilt (backfire); rigid SMART as the gate to creating a goal (a third of studies show specific≠better); a standalone Goals nav tab — the exact new prompt that kills adoption (vindicates baking it in); vanity metrics/leaderboards; over-clinical prescriptive framing; and treating cycle-syncing as a hard rule (its productivity claims are practitioner-led, not RCT-backed — gentle tint only). One honest caveat: WOOP’s evidence is strong but mostly from its creator with short follow-ups — fine to build on, just don’t over-claim it in user-facing copy.

Eight · Your call

The dials.

1 · Intention input
Free-text, suggestions, or both? My pick: both — a few smart phase/goal-tied suggestions + free text.
2 · Line of the day engine
Static template only, or Jess-enriched sometimes? My pick: static by default (instant, free, on-brand) + an optional “go deeper” Jess tap.
3 · Goals home
Profile-led (identity editor) or Garden-led (the living view)? My pick: both — Garden is the view, Profile is the editor, one “Goals” sheet reachable from either.
4 · How many active goals?
My pick: a gentle soft-cap (~5 perennials + a season’s annuals); a nudge to focus, never a hard block.
5 · Seasonal cadence
Cycle-synced (4 phases), calendar 12-week, or both? My pick: a gentle 12-week “season” as the spine, phase-tinted for effort. Her choice to lean more cyclical.
6 · Evening reflection
My pick: only when she planted an intention that day — never a nightly nag.
7 · Build order
My pick: (1) daily intention set+reflect on Today + the line of the day (rides v4 omen engine), then (2) goals in Garden+Profile, then (3) the seasonal review ritual.
