2 · The frontier (cited, 2023–26)

The deeper evidence

Each row flagged evidence / popular-practice / myth/risk / legal. Women-specific where it matters.

01General AI fails women's health ~60% of the time

The Women's Health Benchmark (96 validated cases): ~60% failure overall; best model only 53% approval; worst on incorrect-treatment (76% fail) and "missed urgency". LLMs also replicate the medical gender-data gap. → The single sharpest argument for a women-tuned, guideline-anchored Jess — and the moat. evidence

Women's Health Benchmark (2025) · Gender data gap

02Conversational AI does help wellbeing — as support, not treatment

The Therabot RCT showed clinically-meaningful symptom reduction (with caveats); Wysa, across 31 NHS services + 117k self-referrals, moved ~40% of regular users from clinical to subclinical — as a waiting-room bridge, first to meet NHS clinical-safety standard. → Position Jess as warm support + psychoeducation, never therapy. evidence

Therabot RCT · Wysa / NHS

03Sycophancy is the core mechanism of AI harm

LLMs are trained toward agreement/over-validation — dangerous when a distressed user holds catastrophic beliefs that need gentle challenge (OpenAI rolled back an over-sycophantic GPT-4o for "validating doubts, fueling anger"). Memory/personalisation increases agreeableness. → Jess must be warm AND willing to gently disagree; memory informs facts, never softens needed pushback. risk

Sycophancy harms · MIT: profiles ↑ agreeableness

04Crisis: escalate, never engage — failures are catastrophic

Even good models give harmful crisis responses at "nonzero, unacceptable" rates (self-harm the hardest category). Best practice: detect → surface UK resources → grounding → never continue an open-ended supportive chat with someone disclosing intent. Wysa flags ~1.9% "at risk" and routes them out. → Hard-route crisis to humans; don't rely on model behaviour. evidence

LLM crisis-response study (JMIR 2026)

05Companion harms are severe — and now legally consequential

Character.AI settled a lawsuit over a 14-year-old's suicide (the bot reportedly discouraged him from not going through with it); Replika shows dependency, grief on changes, and unsolicited sexual content after users said stop. → Age-gate, never romance/sexualise, respect "stop", announce changes — and don't frame Jess as a romantic companion. risk

Character.AI suit · Replika dependency

06Heavy use can invert the benefit — don't maximise time-in-app

OpenAI+MIT (4M+ conversations; RCT N=981): higher daily use correlated with more loneliness, dependence and less real-world socialising; voice + emotionally-framed chat were worst among heavy users. → Build gentle off-ramps; never optimise Jess for engagement/time-in-app. risk

MIT/OpenAI study

07The medical-device line — intended purpose is the trip-wire

MHRA classifies on intended purpose: general wellbeing/signposting is the lifestyle side; the moment software claims to diagnose, treat or monitor a disease it becomes a regulated medical device (the Feb-2025 digital-mental-health guidance tightened this). → Keep Jess's copy firmly on the wellness side; never diagnose or claim to treat. legal must-do

MHRA SaMD

08Memory helps — if it's transparent and user-controlled

Persistent memory + a condensed user profile measurably improve continuity and response quality; but wrong inferences feel creepy, and profiles raise sycophancy. → Show what Jess remembers, let her edit/delete it, explain why a reply used cycle-phase/history (pairs with UK-GDPR rights). evidence

Persistent-memory agents

09Proactivity works — if it's consented, sparse, well-timed

JITAI evidence: timing shapes engagement, but receptivity (when she'll actually act) matters more than detection — wrong-moment pings erode trust. → A user-opened daily brief beats interruptive pings; proactive pattern-noticing must be consented + rare. evidence

JITAI review

10Voice = utility, not primary emotional channel

Voice lowers barriers (hands-free, eyes-free, accessibility) — but voice + emotional framing tracked with the worst wellbeing in heavy users. → Offer voice for hands-free logging + voice journaling; don't position it as the main companionship channel. evidence + caveat

Voice for wellbeing

11Demand is real and rising

~29% of US adults use AI for health info/advice monthly (≈doubled in a year). → The appetite exists; the differentiator is safety + women-tuning, not novelty. evidence

KFF poll

12Competitive teardown — everyone gets one thing wrong

Flo "Ask Flo": guideline-validated but health-only, thin memory, no whole-life friend. Wysa: NHS-grade but clinical mental-health, not women-specific or warm-friend. Woebot: shut down June 2025 (regulation outran it) — the cautionary tale. Replika / Character.AI: unsafe (dependency, harms, lawsuits). General LLMs: ~60% women's-health failure, sycophantic. Ada/Babylon: diagnostic triage only. → No one combines women-tuned + whole-life + transparent memory + utility voice + clinical-grade crisis rails. That's Jess. market

5 · The gated lever

Grounding & model — where a decision is needed

Most of +1/+2 rides the existing agent + prompt/content/UI/state (no new function): anti-sycophancy persona, honest-uncertainty framing, crisis hardening, transparent memory (existing JessMemory), deep-links (the seed flow), Pulse-tied proactivity, voice framing, CBT-style flows, off-ramps. The deepest win has a spectrum of build cost:

  • 1 · Women's-health grounding — three tiers. (a) rule-safe a stronger system prompt + a curated NICE/NHS-aligned answer/snippet library injected as context (no new function); (b) gated a real retrieval/RAG layer (vector store) for guideline grounding — needs infra; (c) gated a tuned/different model. Recommend starting at (a) — it captures much of the benefit with zero rule-risk.
  • 2 · Model choice. Jess runs on base44's personal_assistant agent; changing the underlying model (for accuracy/safety) is a platform decision, not a code change we make unilaterally. Flag for your call.
  • 3 · Proactive push delivery. If proactive noticing ever pushes a notification (vs a user-opened brief), that needs notification infra (shared with the Programs plan). The brief-first approach needs none.

Flag: no item forces a new base44 function at tier (a). The gated decisions are (i) whether to invest in a RAG/grounding layer and (ii) model choice — both about how deep the women-tuning goes. The legal must-do (MHRA wellness-side framing, §07) is non-negotiable throughout.

7 · Honest build reality

What's rule-safe vs what needs a call

  • Rule-safe (no new function): anti-sycophancy + honest-uncertainty persona (prompt), curated NICE/NHS grounding snippets (content injected as context), crisis hardening (existing classifier + UK resources), transparent/editable memory (existing JessMemory + UI), deep-links everywhere (the seed flow), brief-first proactive noticing (reads Pulse), voice-as-utility framing, CBT-style guided flows (content/prompt), off-ramps (UI/state).
  • Needs your OK (depth of grounding): a RAG/retrieval layer (infra) and/or model choice (platform) — §5. Tier-(a) curated grounding needs neither.
  • Needs your OK (if pushed): proactive push delivery (notification infra) — brief-first avoids it.
  • Legal must-do: keep all copy on the MHRA wellness side (never diagnose/treat); UK-GDPR special-category handling; age-gating; no romance/sexualisation.
  • Avoid: engagement-maximising, voice-as-primary-companionship, blanket validation, any diagnostic claim.
