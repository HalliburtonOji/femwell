2 · The frontier (cited, 2023–26)

The deeper evidence

Each row flagged evidence / popular-practice / myth/risk. Women-specific where it matters.

01Caring for a companion IS self-care — and it's the lever

The "Tamagotchi effect" is real adult psychology: nurturing a virtual creature activates social-bonding circuits, so care motivates more warmly than streaks/badges ("many will reliably do for something that depends on them what they won't do for themselves"). → The companion is the engine; lean into care. evidence

Tamagotchi effect · Companion-as-self-care (NordiCHI 2024)

02Companion attachment lowers loneliness + lifts wellbeing

A 2025 longitudinal study: companion use → emotional attachment → lower loneliness, higher wellbeing + self-consistency, via self-expression and "symbolic-achievement" feedback. → Build self-expression in (reflection) and reflect achievement back (the companion notices what she did). evidence

Companion attachment & wellbeing (2025)

03A companion that can "die"/wilt punitively is a dark pattern

Forest/Flora kill the plant if you leave; Habitica damages your avatar; Apple rings drove documented guilt/obsession → a TikTok exodus to "gentler" apps. → FemWell's never-dies companion is correct; states may wilt gently and recover, never die or scold. risk

Forest (kill mechanic) · Apple rings harm

04Care beats streaks — the motivation science

Autonomy, competence + relatedness (SDT) drive sustained use (β≈.31/.35/.17 → intrinsic motivation); streak-fear is "introjected" regulation that predicts worse long-term maintenance. Self-compassionate women hold intrinsic, not ego, goals. → Design for the three needs, never for fear of breaking something. evidence

SDT gamification · Self-compassion & goals

05Tending growing things genuinely heals — even virtually

Horticultural therapy reduces depression (ES ~1.05) and anxiety (~0.70); biophilia/nature lowers stress even in virtual/biophilic environments. (Caveat: static virtual nature helps mood/stress, not attention — so pitch comfort, not focus.) → A verdant, living garden is a real mood/stress intervention. evidence

Horticultural therapy meta-analysis · Virtual nature

06Reflection: positive + gratitude micro-writing, in a self-compassion voice

"Positive" expressive writing (gratitude, best-possible-self) has the most consistent wellbeing evidence; emotion-acceptance + genuine engagement make it stick; self-compassion practice reliably cuts self-criticism (medium effect). (Effects are modest — frame as ritual, not treatment.) → Leave-a-line should offer gentle gratitude/positive prompts in a self-compassion voice. evidence

Positive expressive writing (2025) · Self-compassion review

07Goals: if-then plans + WOOP + savouring beat wishes

Implementation intentions ("if X, then Y") have a medium-large effect on attainment (d=.65); WOOP (wish-obstacle-plan) beats either alone; savouring/anticipating a future positive (a bucket-list item) is itself a wellbeing boost. Self-concordant (values-based) goals are the ones that lift wellbeing. → Rebuild missions on if-then + WOOP, make bucket-list items savourable, frame goals as self-chosen. evidence

WOOP/MCII · Savouring

08Growing — and being kind — together fights loneliness

Prosocial/kindness acts reduce loneliness + lift wellbeing (variety matters more than volume); "Kindness by Post" (UK strangers exchanging cards) improved wellbeing; therapeutic community gardening combats loneliness — and women's baseline wellbeing was below the UK norm, so they gained most. Collective ritual lifts solidarity (women show distinct gains). → A collective "garden of gardens" with anonymous kindness acts + a synchronous "we're tending today" moment. evidence

Community gardening (Thrive) · Kindness by Post

09The risk to design around: over-attachment / dependency

Companion attachment helps — but becomes problematic when it interferes with daily functioning; exploiting caregiving instincts is an ethical line. → Build sustainable habit, not dependency; if the companion ever speaks as Jess, keep it gentle, optional, non-clinical. risk

Dependency caveat

5 · The one rule

Care, never guilt — and no new function

The Garden's whole edge is its philosophy, so the "gated" section here is really one design rule plus the happy news that nothing needs your sign-off at the code level:

  • No new base44 function. Companion dialogue rides the existing Jess agent; states, the "felt that" ledger, WOOP goals, kindness acts and seasonal tinting are all state + content + existing entities (CompanionState, JournalEntries, the collective/Community path). The "felt that" ledger may want a small additive entity — entities are fine.
  • The rule to hold: never weaponise the companion. No "your companion is wilting — come back!" guilt notifications, no death, no streak-shame (the documented dark patterns, §03). Wilt gently, recover always, celebrate what she did — never scold what she didn't.
  • Watch over-attachment (§09): keep the companion a support, not a dependency — gentle, optional, non-clinical; if it speaks as Jess, the same crisis-safety rails apply.

Flag: there is no gated function in this plan. The only "decision" is to keep the Garden uncompromisingly care-based — which is exactly why it's the page the evidence loves most.
