What changes later?

One chosen voice would follow the companion you name through Jess, Read, Sky and the short listens. The words and scene can change; she should still sound like herself.

How would this feel across the app?

A short spoken invitation belongs beside the thing it actually opens. Read gets a tiny magazine dispatch and the complete article. Sky gets a small story, not a planet lecture. Books leads straight to the chapter. Listen keeps one short and your real full episode. Yours keeps the exact recording you saved. Jess talks when asked; she does not interrupt the page or another player.

Fiction stays labelled fiction. A public social trend would have its source and date, and say plainly when people are talking rather than proving a claim. Private Community posts and DMs would never become a story without the user's explicit action.

What about breathing, chairs and background sound?

Yes, but with taste. A chair shift or a bus brake can belong inside an authored scene. A cough should be rare and story-driven, not a random tick in every Jess reply. No perpetual café hiss behind serious advice. Speech first, occasional scene detail second, quiet when the user needs it. The full transcript would include meaningful sounds, and a voice-only mix would stay available.

What is still a decision?

These are ten real, once-generated ElevenLabs auditions of one identical script. They are not the final voice cast or a working account setting. We need your ears on the voices, then a full-length scene in the strongest candidates, a sound/no-sound comparison, and an iPhone test. The shared preference owner, interactive Jess voice cost/privacy, and public social source rules are staged architecture decisions. ElevenLabs' voice guide explains why the original voice and script matter more than extreme sliders; W3C explains the transcript requirement.
