Four+ calendars, three colour languages, two of them on retired tokens, none of them a real logging entry point. That's the "OLD" feeling you're describing.

Logging is welded to "today"

Every logger writes to the current date/time — there is no way to say "log this for Tuesday." Concretely:

  • SmartLoggerV4.jsx (the real production logger behind the FAB) hardcodes day_key: today() and logged_at: now().
  • UniversalLogger.jsx's DetailForm hardcodes todayISO for every entity write.
  • Nutrition UnifiedLogger.jsx hardcodes day_key = new Date()….
  • The global opener is openLogger(type) — it takes a type only. There is nowhere to pass a date or time.
The core engineering truthThis whole feature rests on one change: logging must accept a target date + time, threaded from openLogger(type, ctx) all the way to every create() call. Everything visual is downstream of that. It's very doable — the write sites are few and centralised — but it is the real work, so we plan it deliberately.

The "+" today

The FAB is rendered inside SmartLoggerV4 at position:fixed; bottom:88; right:18. On the Planner it becomes a dual stack (a small pen for the smart logger + a big espresso + for planner items). "Log a meal" pills and the nav's "Log today" all funnel into the same openLogger(). Good news: because it's all one dispatcher, we can swap the entry surface without rewriting the 50+ call sites.

2 · The idea in one screen

Before

  • Floating + on every page
  • Photo-bg calendar buried in Planner
  • Logging = always "now"
  • Calendar = a thing you look at

After

  • Calendar icon on every page
  • One cream calendar, everywhere
  • Logging = any day you choose
  • Calendar = the thing you log through

The mental model shifts from "add something (to now)" → "open my calendar, touch a day, tend to it." Warmer, more whole-life, and it makes back-filling and planning-ahead first-class instead of impossible.

6 · The date + time prefill model

The prefill rules must be consistent everywhere yet respect context. One rule table, three surfaces:

You open logging from…Date pre-fills toTime pre-fills toBoth editable?
General "Log for today" (any page)TodayNowYes
Tapping a day in the calendarThat dayNow (or blank for a past day)Yes
Planner day-view → tapping a free hourThat dayThat hourYes (date + time)
A page's own context (e.g. Nutrition "Log a meal")Today (or the page's active date if it has one)Inferred meal timeYes
The one rule Whoever opens the logger passes a context; the sheet always shows that context and always lets you change it. Signature becomes openLogger(type, { date, time, scope }) where scope is 'today' | 'day' | 'slot'. When date/time are omitted it falls back to today/now — so all 50+ existing call sites keep working untouched (they just get the old behaviour).

How editing feels

Inside the rebuilt sheet, a single date · time row sits at the top (below the title). It's pre-filled per the table. Tap the date → a mini month popover (the same FwCalendar in density:'sheet') — recursion done right, one component. Tap the time → a native time wheel. Change either; the pre-fill was just a helpful default, never a lock.

Planner time-slot in action

9 · Retiring the "+" FAB without breaking logging

Because everything funnels through the openLogger() singleton, the swap is surgical:

  1. Keep openLogger() + the sheet exactly as the dispatcher (extend its signature per §6). The 50+ call sites and every "Log a meal" pill keep working.
  2. Remove the FAB render blocks: in SmartLoggerV4 the !open && !hideFAB button, and in UniversalLogger the Planner dual-FAB stack. The sheet still opens on command — it just no longer paints a floating button.
  3. Add the <FwCalendarButton /> to the top bar in Layout.jsx. Its tap = open calendar; the calendar's "Log for today" / day-tap call openLogger(...) with context.
  4. Planner: the big espresso "+" (planner items) folds into the day-view's "tap a free hour" + the day sheet's Event chip — so the dedicated planner-add FAB isn't needed either.
  5. Ship behind a flag for one build so we can A/B the reachability before deleting FAB code for good.
Reachability gateThe FAB is currently the only obvious "add" affordance for many users. Before it goes, the calendar icon must be unmistakable and the "Log for today" button must be one tap from every page — or logging frequency drops. This is the one risk to watch; the flagged rollout de-risks it.

  • SmartLoggerV4 / DetailForm / nutrition UnifiedLogger: replace hardcoded today()/now() with ctx.date/ctx.time where present. ~a dozen create() sites, all centralised.

12 · One-flow: run / plan a whole day in one pass

Today, capturing a day means hopping modal-to-modal: log a meal, close, open symptoms, close, open meds… Each hop is friction and a reason to stop early. The fix: a single continuous flow that walks you through the whole day — or lets you plan a whole future day — in one pass, one save.

TappyA · Stepper / wizard (one section per screen)

+ Focused; each step uncluttered; feels like a gentle ritual.
– A tap (Next) between every section; N screens to review; back-and-forth to change an earlier answer. Slow for a quick daily sweep.

RecommendedB · One sectioned sheet (stacked, skippable, one save)

+ The whole day on one scroll — Meals · Water · Mood · Symptoms · Meds · Events. Each section is a compact inline capture with a Skip; a jump-to rail (the app's central-switcher pattern) pins the sections at the top; a running progress counter; and ONE save at the end. Fast for power users, jumpable, nothing to learn. Matches your "run through your day" phrasing exactly.
– Can feel long — mitigated by the jump rail, skippable sections, and each section staying to one compact row.

SlowerC · Chat-style (Jess asks section by section)

+ Warm, conversational, very on-brand (Jess's voice); naturally skippable.
– Slower turn-taking; harder to jump around or review/edit; more friction for a quick daily log.

Pick: B — the one-sheet "Run through the day" A single sheet with a jump-to rail + progress + a sticky Save the day. Entered from the day sheet's primary button — "Run through the day" (log) or "Plan the whole day" (plan) — with the individual type chips kept below for single quick logs. The LOG-vs-PLAN gate is respected: a future day shows only plannable sections (meals-as-menu, events, reminders, habits, meds); the retrospective sections (water, mood, symptoms) are hidden — you don't "run through" a headache you haven't had. A "focus mode" that turns B into a one-at-a-time stepper (option A) is an easy future toggle for anyone who prefers it.
✅ Built & live-verified in the demo

Open the demo → "Run through your whole day": sections capture, the jump rail lights a check per touched section, the progress reads "N/6 · N things", and one Save the day · N things drops the dots onto that day. Confirmed end-to-end.

13 · Smart picture → schedule

Snap or screenshot your work rota (or a list of upcoming events) → it reads the shifts, dates and times → you review and edit → then it becomes plan entries. A genuine time-saver for shift workers and busy planners.

✅ Stayed inside the function/key rule No new base44 function, no new key. This extends the existing analyzeMealPhoto function (the one meal-snap already uses) with a mode:"schedule" branch — same gpt-4o-mini multimodal model, same OpenAI key. The meal path is untouched (default mode). Deployed via base44 functions deploy analyzeMealPhoto.

The flow (review-before-save is non-negotiable)

  1. Pick — choose a photo from library (or "Try the sample rota" in the demo). Camera optional.
  2. Read — the image + today's date go to analyzeMealPhoto(mode:"schedule"); it returns structured entries (title, date, day-label, start/end, confidence) + an honest notes line. Weekday-only rota rows are resolved to the next upcoming date.
  3. Review / confirm — every parsed entry is shown for you to check & edit first: a per-row include tick, the date/time, a confidence tag, and a remove button. A row with no resolvable date is flagged "needs a date." Nothing is saved until you tap "Add N to your plan." Never a silent auto-commit.
  4. Commit — the included rows become plan entries on their dates (dots on the calendar).

Real rota shape — worked against Halli's actual "Shift Overview" (July 2026)

Built + verified against his real rota (date · day · start–end · location, with a done tick). The extractor + editable review list handle the tricky bits:

Each shift row (01 Wed 1–10pm · 03 Fri · 09 Thu · 11 Sat · 12 Sun …)Becomes an editable review row: a "Work shift" title, a date picker, start & end time pickers, plus a checkbox — untick or edit any of it before saving.
Overnight (3:00 PM – 12:00 AM crosses midnight)Detected (end ≤ start ⇒ end_next_day); the row shows an "OVERNIGHT · ends +1 day" badge and stores end 00:00 on the following day. Re-derived live if you edit the times.

14 · Photo → anything (intent-driven)

The photo logger must not be schedule-only. A picture can mean many things — a receipt ("what I had for lunch"), a rota ("my shifts"), a symptom, a moment. What it becomes should follow what the user means, not just what's in the pixels. Halli's example: a restaurant receipt is food I had, not a schedule.

How intent is captured — options

Guesses wrongA · Vision-only auto-classify

+ Zero typing.
– A receipt could be "food" or "an expense"; a whiteboard could be a plan or a note. Guessing without the user's words is where it feels dumb.

RecommendedB · Image + a one-line intent, together

+ After adding the picture, the user says it in their own words ("this is what I had for lunch"). The model reads image + intent and the user's words win when they conflict. Fast (one line, or a quick chip: Food I had · My schedule · A symptom · A moment), and it's what makes routing feel smart.
– One extra line of input — worth it for correct routing.

SlowerC · Ask a long questionnaire

– Too many taps for a quick log. We want 1–2 quick, skippable questions, not a form.

Pick: B — intent-led, vision-augmented Add photo → one intent line (free text + quick chips) → the model classifies into food / schedule / symptom / moment (with is_retrospective + a meal-type guess) → 1 smart follow-up → review → save. The rota→schedule parse becomes one outcome of this path, not the whole thing. Intent is the primary signal — so even if the vision call can't run, the flow still works from the user's words (and the demo proves this).

Smart follow-ups + how the "most likely" is highlighted

After classifying, the app asks one short multiple-choice question (skippable) — e.g. food → "Log it as" [Breakfast · Lunch · Dinner · Snack]. The most-probable answer is pre-selected and emphasised: it renders filled + bold with a "★ MOST LIKELY" tag, and it's already the chosen value so a user can just hit Continue. The guess comes from the model's meal_type_guess (or the time of day) for food, and keyword cues for symptoms. Every question also offers "Type your own" → a free-text reply, so the user is never boxed into the chips.

The LOG-vs-PLAN intent guard

When the entry's nature conflicts with the selected date's mode, the app confirms and redirects rather than doing the wrong thing:

  • Future date + retrospective thing (e.g. food you just ate while a future day is selected): "This looks like something you had — log it for today instead of planning it for [date]?" → [Log for today] (redirects to today, log mode) / [Keep for that day].
