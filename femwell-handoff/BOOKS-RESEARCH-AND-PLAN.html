FemWell · Books · deep pass 1 of 6 · research + plan, before build

A reading corner for when capacity comes and goes.

The research moved this section somewhere I didn't expect. The barrier to reading isn't motivation — it's capacity, and it collapses at exactly the life stages FemWell serves. That, not "more books", is the job.

A · THE RESEARCHWhat actually changes the design

Full cited file: mnt/femwell/research_books_section.md. Three existing FemWell research files already settle the serial-pacing and reader-craft half and are cited rather than re-derived (research_daily_story_ritual.md, research_reader_and_cards.md, research_reading_foundation.md).

  • 1 · The barrier is CAPACITY, not motivation. 7.3m UK adults say mental-health issues prevent them reading; 4.96m stopped after life events, ill health or bereavement — new parenthood named explicitly. Women are more likely to read to reduce stress. This spikes precisely at FemWell's life stages. source
  • 2 · Four books a month is ~16× the UK median. 40% of Britons read no books in a year; the median is 3. So the featured book is the product and the other three are scenery — the design must stop presenting four equals. Bedtime is the slot (57%). source
  • 3 · Our readers are the UK's biggest book-buying group — and not one genre. Women 13–44 are 35% of all UK purchases; romance leads under 45, historical fiction for women 45+, crime/thriller 45+. A romance-shaped shelf fails half our life stages. source
  • 4 · Escapism is up, self-help-shaped non-fiction is down. Fiction £1.1bn (+18%), audiobooks £268m (+31%), non-fiction −4%. This is not a self-improvement shelf. source
  • 5 · Ask which DOORWAY, not which genre. Nancy Pearl's appeal factors — character · setting · language · story — capture "what draws us in, rather than what the book is about". One question, once, no profiling. source
  • 6 · Spoiler-safety by reading POSITION is a shipped pattern. StoryGraph locks buddy-read comments until a reader reaches that point. We already store position. source
  • 7 · HARD LINE — no generative AI writing about her reading. Fable's AI recap (Jan 2025) told a reader to "surface for the occasional white author"; Fable pulled all genAI features. Recaps must come from authored text, never a model writing about her. source
  • 8 · Social reading is at a record high, and reading groups specifically lift CLASSIC fiction — which is exactly our stock. source
  • 9 · Anticipation only pays if the wait ends in a real read (delay raises enjoyment when consumption happens, lowers it when merely imagined), and the mature reveal shows the synopsis early, the object late (FairyLoot reveal the theme + synopsis so you can decide if it's for you). Our current "a new book arrives on the 27th" with nothing behind it is the wrong way round. source
  • 10 · Cream + 1.5 leading + left-align has a UK citation; "dyslexia fonts" are refuted (OpenDyslexic/Dyslexie show no benefit across three studies). Keep our cream reading world; don't add a dyslexia font. source

Where the evidence is weak — and where it contradicts us

“Paradox of choice” is contested (Scheibehenne 2010: ~zero mean effect across 50 experiments). So I will not justify a small set by choice overload. The defensible argument is preference uncertainty — she has no articulated book taste inside a wellness app — which is one of the moderators that does bite.

Spoilers: the 2011 "spoilers help" finding failed replication. Hide by default.

“Counting makes you read worse” is an opinion essay, not a study — used as corroboration only. And a claim that anonymity helps book discussion had no source, so it was dropped.

One place the research and your direction pull apart — your call

Our own serial research says a backlog should wait silently and that "Day N" labelling is "a streak in a hat". You asked for a gentle sense of how many are waiting ("3 waiting for you"). I've kept yours — it's your explicit direction and it reads as welcome, not debt — but I've paid for it by removing every other integer in the section: no books-read count, no percentages, no streak, no "Day N". The only progress artefact is a sentence she wrote herself. Say the word if you'd rather the backlog go silent too.

C · THE SECTION'S JOBWhat FemWell's reading corner uniquely is

Not a bookshop (we have no inventory), not Goodreads (we refuse to count her). Given finding 1, the distinctive job is: a reading corner built for a woman whose capacity comes and goes — one that costs nothing on a bad week, holds her place without comment, and still has something for ten minutes at bedtime. Escape first, improvement never.

D · THE DESIGNContent & arrangement, in order

Deliberate order: the thing she's mid-way through → the run → a way in when she's got nothing → this month's book → the room → the rest → hers. Everything reads her real state.

0

Section summary have

Stateful and kind: where she is in the serial, what's waiting, what's new this month. Never a count of books read.

1

Your next chapter have + recap, + read-time

The serial hero, her position (never the calendar's), ‹ › flip one at a time, "N waiting for you". New: a “Previously…” line when she's been away — drawn from the authored chapter text, never model-written about her (finding 7). New: "~7 min" read-time as permission, hidden past the threshold (existing research).

2

The run have

The compact strip scoped to this run's series — navigation, not a score. Two up-next rows, one straight-through link. Unchanged.

3

“Not much in the tank?” new — the differentiator

The research's biggest gap and genuinely unclaimed. A quiet row offering: the shortest chapter in the run · something you've read before (re-reading is restorative, not failure) · just a page (opens, reads one page, closes — and that counts) · and, when built, listen instead. Appears when she's been away, or always as a small doorway. This is the wellness job (finding 1) and nothing else in the market does it.

4

This month's book — singular changed

Not "4 books". One featured book is the product (finding 2): cover, why this one, in a sentence (authored), the doorway it suits, content notes if any, "~9 min a chapter", and Start reading in place. Plus “Not for me” as a first-class action — it swaps the book, the month's set quietly shrinks, no debt recorded. Life-stage aware, so it isn't romance-shaped for everyone (finding 3).

5

Read it with others new — surface what exists

The Book Club we already built, finally linked: the pick, "six weeks · no rush, no streak", her current checkpoint, and the conversation gated by her own reading position (finding 6) — a room that structurally cannot spoil. Any classic she opens also has its readers' corner. Anonymous, consent-gated, as Community already is. No member counts.

6

Also this month changed

The three alternates, small and quiet (scenery, not equals), each tagged by doorway rather than genre. The paced reveal is corrected to the mature form: the synopsis and why-this appear from day one; only the book itself arrives on the set day — and the reveal opens into readable text, or the anticipation backfires (finding 9).

7

Yours have + what you wrote

Continue-reading and saves behind one quiet link. New: the only progress artefact in the whole section — the reflections she wrote at chapter ends (BookReader already captures them). One sentence in her own words, instead of a number.

8

The doorway question new — asked once

"What draws you in — the people, the place, the words, or what happens next?" One tap, stored once, device-local. It orders the alternates and nothing else. No profile, no genre survey, no surveillance (finding 5).

Rules this section is held to

  • No integers beyond your "N waiting" — no books-read, no %, no streak, no "Day N".
  • No generative AI writing about her reading. Recaps and why-this are authored.
  • Never fake a shelf — with 0 FemWell fiction, the month's set is curated classics; thin months shrink honestly.
  • No new entity or function. The doorway token, "not for me", and the month set are device-local or an authored module (like the existing club seed).
  • Cream reading world kept, 1.5+ leading, left-aligned; no dyslexia font (refuted).
