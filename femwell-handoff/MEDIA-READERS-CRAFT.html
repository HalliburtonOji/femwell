Media & readers

Make the cards actually play, actually read, and actually look like us — and never feel empty.

Five gaps, one pass.

1 · Video & 2 · Audio

The cover is the poster. The flora is the visualiser.

Video, inline. The FloraCover is the poster — tap the bloom-play button and the real <video> takes its place and plays in the card (native controls, playsInline, never autoplay, reduced-motion safe). If it's an external platform (TikTok/YouTube), we keep the honest link-out action — no fake embed.

Audio, inline + alive. Real <audio>, plus the bit that makes it ours: a flora visualiser instead of a generic bar meter. A little row of stems topped with blooms — while it plays they sway and pulse, each stem on its own seeded phase so it breathes like a meadow, not a graphic equaliser. Palette: oxblood · gold · sage · cream. Reduced-motion → it simply rests, open and still.

Stems + blooms, swaying on their own phases — a meadow in the wind, not an equaliser. (Time-driven, so it works on any source and never fights CORS.)

3 · Books

Three taps become two.

Tap 1 the book cover card
Tap 2 — "Open reader" → you're already reading: a paged view, right there in the expand, with Next page.
Option "Open the full reader" — the whole experience (chapters, reflections, the club), one clear button.

The expand becomes the reading experience: opening pages fetched via the existing Gutenberg function, paginated to a comfortable page, with next/prev and a page count. It degrades honestly — if the text is slow or unavailable, the pane says so and the full reader is one tap away. Nothing is removed: the full reader keeps everything it has.

4 · The readers

They should look like the app they live in.

Three readers, three different palettes, none of them ours. The craft pass: cream paper (#ECE7DA/#F4EFE3), oxblood headings (#7A1A12), gold/sage flora accents, corner sprigs, Fraunces/Cormorant/Ephesis type — the same world as the cards that open them. Every mechanic stays: chapters, reflections, warnings, progress, the club, flip/movie mode, the cliffhanger countdown.

5 · The summary block

A card should never look empty.

New to the card set (§6.7.7): summary — one real, warm sentence of substance (a hook, a lede, or a true excerpt), not a label.

  • On the cover: under the title, so a card at rest already tells you something worth tapping.
  • In the expand: an "In short" block right under the title, so the page opens with substance instead of air.
  • Fallback ladder: summary → subtitle → excerpt → first line of body — a card is never blank, and never a lorem placeholder.

Joins the block list in §6.7.7 alongside player · excerpt · pull-quote · reading · ingredients · steps. Add a block, never a bespoke card.

The method (standing, from here)

Research each sub-piece separately, then add what's missing.

Halli's refinement, now the standard for every component pass: don't just implement the ask — brainstorm each sub-piece on its own, research what a genuinely great version looks like, and ADD what's needed. Below: five mini-brainstorms, each with what the research changed and what we're adding beyond the ask. Sources: mnt/femwell/research_inline_media_ux.md · mnt/femwell/research_reader_and_cards.md (26+ URLs, all fetched 16/07/2026).

Two claims we refused to make (the researcher dropped them rather than invent): there is no reading evidence for or against drop caps, and no real evidence that serif beats sans for long-form on screen. Fraunces/Cormorant are a brand decision — we won't cite fake science for them. Likewise "cream is kinder on the eyes" is anecdotal; the defensible argument for #ECE7DA is NN/g's positive-polarity evidence (and it strengthens as text gets smaller).

Mini-brainstorm 1 · Inline video

Tap-to-play was right. Captions were a Level A fail.

Validated: tap-to-play is correct for editorial ("users don't appreciate being surprised by video that begins playing without their consent" — NN/g). Muted autoplay is only defensible as a GIF-replacement, and iOS defeats it anyway (autoplay pauses when non-visible, and in Low Power Mode) — so poster + play affordance is the only consistent experience. playsinline is mandatory or iPhone forces fullscreen. Native controls beat a custom bar: free fullscreen, PiP, AirPlay, OS caption styling, keyboard.

What the research changed: two real defects. (1) We ship zero <track> — that's a WCAG SC 1.2.2 Level A failure, and for a wellness app captions aren't an a11y tax: she's watching in bed, on mute, beside a sleeping partner. (2) preload="metadata" is not the cheap option — browsers can't predict metadata location, so every card in a feed quietly bills her mobile data.

ADDING beyond the ask

  • Captions — <track default kind="captions" srclang="en"> via a new captionsSrc field (+ crossorigin when the VTT is remote).
  • Data discipline — preload="none" + loading="lazy". The FloraCover poster is generative SVG, so it costs no fetch at all.
  • Duration on the card face — NN/g: a video card must give the topic and the length. Ours didn't.
Mini-brainstorm 2 · Inline audio + the flora visualiser

The visualiser was right — for a bigger reason than taste.

The decisive finding. Never drive the visualiser from Web Audio's AnalyserNode on our media: for cross-origin audio a MediaElementAudioSourceNode outputs zeroes — the track goes silent. It's undetectable and irreversible ("it simply gets muted with no conservative way to revert it"). The failure mode is a sleep story that plays silently. Our time-driven CSS visualiser dodges a one-way trap. And at 5–9 stems, CSS transforms on a promoted layer beat canvas (canvas only wins "when you have hundreds of elements"). The ambient craft rule — "multiple asynchronous, very slow modulation sources… so it shifts and breathes" — is exactly our per-stem seeded phases. Gimmicky = beat-locked, uniform, fast. Tasteful = slow, unsynced, drifting.

What the research changed — and a discovery in our own codebase. The great player wants scrub, ±skip, speed, resume, lock-screen controls and background continue. We were building a dead-end <audio> in a card… while PodcastPlayerProvider already has all of it (Media Session, sleep timer, speed, resume-from-position via PodcastListens) and its MiniPlayer is already mounted globally in Layout. So the great version isn't to rebuild it — it's to route card audio into it. Reuse, don't duplicate.

ADDING beyond the ask

  • Card audio → the global player. Tap play on a card and the app's real player takes it: it keeps playing when she navigates or locks the phone, resumes where she left off, and gets speed + sleep timer for free.
  • The sleep timer FADES. A meditation cut off mid-breath is a jolt in the exact moment we promised calm. Spotify users are still asking for fade-out — so we ship it: volume ramps over the last ~20s and the flora folds closed as it fades. Called "End of story".
  • The visualiser goes where the audio actually lives — into the expanded player, not just the card.
  • A transcript, not captions. Audio-only is a different criterion (SC 1.2.1) — it wants a transcript. "Read it instead" is also just good: she may want to read the sleep story.
  • Reduced motion → the flora holds a still, open bloom. Stillness is brand-right; hiding it isn't.

Explicitly NOT building: an AnalyserNode visualiser (silences audio), a custom video control bar (loses fullscreen/PiP/AirPlay/captions), a canvas port of the flora (slower at our scale).

Mini-brainstorm 3 · The book 2-tap flow

Paginate for feel — never claim it aids comprehension.

The premise everyone repeats is refuted. A 2025 CHI study (n=100, real phones, ten short stories) found no significant difference between scrolling and pagination on comprehension (14.8 vs 14.7), duration, or workload. The "pagination aids comprehension" line is 10+ years old and desktop-based. But the qualitative data rescues it: "a sense of progression", "less intimidating", "more digestible". So we paginate — for feel, honestly — and the paper itself recommends offering both. Fiction-vs-article is genuinely unresolved; the defensible split is paginate the book & daily story, scroll the essays.

The finding that reframes progress: a percentage scores her; "6 min left" gives her permission to start. The enemy was never comprehension — it's not opening it.

ADDING beyond the ask

  • "~N min left" instead of a bare page count — permission, not scoring.
  • Silent auto-resume — she returns to the page she left, with no ceremony.
  • A real measure fix (a live bug we found). On a 390px phone the danger inverts: the risk isn't lines too long, it's too short — under ~45 characters. Our pane double-framed the text (a card inside the expand) down to ~41 CPL. Un-frame it and let the page breathe.
Mini-brainstorm 4 · In-app reader design

Beautiful is measure, leading and margins — not ornament.

The craft that actually carries a reader: a comfortable measure (~45–75 characters; our real risk at 390px is falling under it), generous margins, honest leading, and the oldest typographic tell of a real book — indent XOR space, never both (Butterick). Fiction gets a first-line indent and no paragraph gap; articles get spaced paragraphs and no indent. It's the cheapest "this is a real book" win available.

Honest limits: no evidence exists for or against drop caps, and serif-vs-sans for screen reading is null. Those stay brand calls under the bible, not science. The defensible case for cream #ECE7DA is positive polarity (dark-on-light), which strengthens as text gets smaller.

ADDING beyond the ask

  • Indent-XOR-space, split by type — the story/book reads like a book; essays read like essays.
  • Measure + leading held deliberately in the reading surfaces, not left to whatever the container gives.
  • Dark mode: offer, don't default (NN/g names book readers explicitly) — the story reader's Plum theme already is exactly this, now on-brand.
Mini-brainstorm 5 · Card summaries

Honesty doesn't cost taps. Vagueness does.

The finding that kills the "don't spoil it" instinct. Across 8,977 A/B tests and 35,910 headlines, concreteness→clicks is an inverted-U: too vague lowers clicks — "there is such a thing as omitting too much information." And 54.5% report decreased trust in habitual clickbait. For a warm UK product this settles it: write the real thing. Say what it is, and she'll tap it and trust you.

Per NN/g's "4 Ss", a summary that restates the title is worse than nothing — it's the empty space we were trying to fix, with words in it.

ADDING beyond the ask

  • Kill any summary that restates its title — a real guard in the component, not a copy note.
  • Read-time everywhere — the single most decision-useful bit of metadata, on every type.
  • Written to the clamp — a true 2-line snippet, not a truncated paragraph.

Deferred honestly to a later pass: "why this, for you" + scrutability. Research is strong (a one-sided, positive, personal reason beat two-sided on trust; and the thing everyone misses is shipping the reason as decoration rather than a control — let her correct it). We're not shipping it yet because getLifestyleFeed returns no reason field — inventing one would be a fake explanation, which is exactly the trust-breaker the research warns about. It needs a real signal first.
