One card, two shelves

The tap-to-expand cards, married to our StackedCard: a single clipboard card split top/bottom, each half a swipeable shelf that opens into the big detail card.

The idea

StackedCard × tap-to-expand.

Take the browse-then-dive interaction (from /ClipboardExpandDemo) and drop it into our StackedCard structure: one big clipboard card, split into a top and a bottom half by the subtle gold hairline. Each half is its own horizontal peek sub-slider — swipe sideways, edge-peek, pager dots, subtle ‹ › arrows. Tap any cover → the same full-screen detail card opens (identical expand in both halves).

Top = Articles (flora-cover reads → tap opens the article: cover · Fraunces title · a "listen to this read" inline player on narrated ones · detail rows · chips · Save + Read). Bottom = the Book club (flora-cover book cards → tap opens the book: cover · title + author · a paragraph hook · Save · Talk about it · Open reader, tied to our Library language).

Why it's good

Density without clutter.

  • Two shelves in one card — articles and books live together, each browsable, without a wall of stacked cards or a long scroll.
  • One learned gesture — swipe a row, tap a cover. It's the same in both halves and the same as the standalone carousel, so nothing new to learn.
  • The StackedCard rules hold — each half is a horizontal sub-slider (never a vertical whole-card swap), the gold hairline separates them, subtle arrows + dots, real card chrome (gradient paper, corner sprig, soft double shadow).
  • Cohesive — reuses the exact FloraCover + expand transition from the first demo (extracted into brand/expandCards.jsx so both demos share one source).

Where it fits

Any surface with two related content shelves: Lifestyle (Reads / Listens), Library (Articles / Books), Programs (Sessions / Series), Nutrition (Tonight / This week), Today's Discover (For you / Saved). It's the natural "board" for a two-lane content rail.
