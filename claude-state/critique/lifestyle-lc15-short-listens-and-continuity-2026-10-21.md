## Editorial brainstorm and listening model

**Sky — When the Moon clocked out.** A mythical visitor arrives at a recognisable late-night kitchen; her dry aside earns the punchline. This is fiction, not a prediction, true user event or astrological instruction. The finished Ideas edition has 66 seconds of actual voiced story and a complete one-tap transcript. A small Moon mark and Play occupy one line, not a second header or shelf. The recording starts only on tap. Its distinct `sky-short-listen:sky-listen-moon-kitchen:v1` Save key and preserved Ideas route reopen the same audio even from main Yours/Saved; the Bulletin beneath retains its own separate save.

**Read — Margin note.** An editor reads a short observation and one useful sourced fact from the *same* checked article; the tap opens that actual article. No summary masquerading as the full text. Named editor, original URL, dated recheck and transcript are prerequisites to publication, so this concept remains unbuilt.

**Good life — A scene and one doable thing.** A human everyday scene ends with one optional, topic-matched invitation. “Not today” never flips into a movement instruction. It must follow the chosen room/topic, rather than a phase label pretending to be a recommendation. The existing source/return and Planner action remain available; no new default notification.

Listen is the listening desk, not another duplicate catalogue: at most one short edition in the first view, then one direct full episode and optional Browse. The source room may show the same edition if it fits, never a second “daily” clip. No clip is generated for an individual listener; no audio bytes load until she taps Play; a saved ID points at a retained versioned asset. Offer text so the story works without sound. A 2-day edition cadence is an editorial capacity choice, not a scheduling job; ship only when enough finished editions make rotation meaningful. Do not loop two clips forever as if they were fresh.

**Cadence and editorial capacity.** Start with a deterministic two-day UK-local publishing slot shared by the app, not six independent daily clips or a call per listener. Have a genuinely finished backlog (proposed minimum 14 versioned editions for 28 days) and a named editorial owner before activating any rotation. Missing or withdrawn audio means an honest text choice and older kept editions, never a false “new today” label. Editing, voice selection and fact/rights review are human gates. The slot table and scheduler are **unbuilt**; this pilot is one edition, explicitly labelled Ideas.

**Voice/design alternatives:** one close, British storyteller with breath and tiny timing changes (recommended); a lightly mischievous theatre voice for Sky only; or two alternating readers keyed to genre, never to inferred user mood. Avoid music beds under speech: the image and words already carry the atmosphere. Compare voice once on a full script with Halli before cataloguing further audio. Keep spoken punctuation natural; title and transcription exact. No generated copy *about her*.

## iPhone-feel and every-tap design system, staged after the route repair

**One movement grammar.** The persistent bottom nav should acknowledge a touch immediately, move one calm active shape and let the destination take over without waiting for a decorative exit. Avoid the present 180 ms pill + 280 ms shrink + unrelated 120/220 ms page swap; those stacked cues make a simple tap feel laboured. Prototype two restrained variants in Ideas: a short gliding ink/floral underline with stable icon positions, or a quiet soft-focus halo that settles under the selected label. Compare real tap-to-content, not animation prettiness alone. Keep the selected label visible at rest, no bounce or petal shower. Reduced-motion turns the travel off but retains the selection cue.

**An opened surface is part of its room.** A Sky explainer can rise as one short sheet with its moon/botanical accent; a Read article can enter as a typographic folio; a Book reader keeps its own page; the grammar of Close, Back, safe-area clearance, focus return and background dimming is shared. The surface itself gets its own clean substrate. No old paper underneath, a second header pasted on top, nested popup, full-page detour for a two-line answer or delayed tap target. On any page switch, remember the outgoing position and focus, take a new destination to its beginning, and restore an exact history return. If a source intentionally has an anchor, that anchor wins over a late scroll timer.

**Click atlas for each next build:** record `origin section → named control → actual routed owner or overlay → expected job → loading/empty/error/success → close/back → restored source`. Capture the opened pixels at 360, 390 and 430 and act on every visible control at least once, including secondary menus/players/pickers and browser Back. Route and code identity matter: `/Profile` resolves to `ProfileClipboardDemo`, not the similarly named decoy. After each redesign, sample old saved/deep links and the full feature inventory, then let an independent critic repeat the riskiest journey. Start the next owner family with Profile/Menu and `PAPER_BG`, then Saved/reader/player returns; map consumers before any global primitive change. ## Source and cost gate

Repo finding: `elevenLabsTTS`, `voiceTTS`, `generateAudio`, `bulkGenerateVoiceAudio` presently call OpenAI despite their names. `guideActions` action `tts` alone calls ElevenLabs, truncates at 500 characters and returns base64 per request. Reusing that path on every Play would re-bill and scale with listeners. The already installed ElevenLabs account API responded as Creator with 0 of 364,137 character allowance at inspection; that is a point-in-time account signal, not a bill or future entitlement. Versioned MP3 in `public/` gives one generation per approved edition, while playback adds ordinary hosted bytes. Actual asset bytes, generation debit and network requests are measured after proof; Base44/CDN bandwidth billing and production audience remain unknown. No key in source/report, no frontend TTS endpoint and no new function name/schema.

The exact 66-second pilot asset is **1,054,554 bytes** (128 kbps MP3). One Play may transfer about a megabyte; 100,000 complete listens would be roughly 105 GB before CDN caching/compression/accounting, not a price or supported-user claim. ElevenLabs character debit was not reliably reflected in the immediate subscription response. The shared podcast player normally reads/writes `PodcastListens` around every five seconds, so LC15 explicitly bypasses that lookup and progress writes for this `save_progress:false` editorial short; full podcasts retain their place/resume behaviour. One explicit Keep uses existing `SavedItems` with owner acknowledgement and read-back, rather than a listener-by-listener TTS bill. Actual network bytes, egress price and authenticated write receipts remain unmeasured.
