## Focused research before implementation

- [Mailchimp's own voice and tone guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/) distinguishes a stable voice from tone that changes with the person's state; it prefers dry, inclusive wit, plain language and clarity over a forced joke. FemWell inference: let ordinary life take the joke, never the woman, a symptom or a failed action.
- [GOV.UK clear-language guidance](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/) recommends familiar short words, active voice, explained technical terms and short chunks. FemWell inference: a title and one useful line in the opening viewport; complete prose remains in the reader.
- [ONS user-needs content guide](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/content/writing-for-users/user-needs) says order content by the user's task and avoid repeated information. FemWell inference: the exact read, transport or time choice should come before the Glance/Jess context without deleting it.

## Voice standard for this pass

Write what the action does, then add at most one dry aside where the moment can hold it. Use UK English and everyday nouns. Avoid ‘journey’, ‘empower’, ‘curated for you’, vague ‘gentle’ padding and long reassurance on repeat. Funny lines are specific: tabs, lists, meetings, laundry, the optimism of ‘one page’. Error, consent, health, money and Save/Plan states stay literal. No template that inserts a joke into every room. Preserve complete source prose and author attribution.
