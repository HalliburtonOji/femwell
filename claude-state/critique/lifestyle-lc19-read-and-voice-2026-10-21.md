## Why the ten clips failed as an identity test

All ten used one 171-character script, one model, the same stability/similarity settings and almost no shift of situation. That isolates timbre, but it also squeezes the voices into a uniform “polished narrator” register. The problem is **performance and casting**, not just which preset sounds nicest. Do not sprinkle “um”, “right” or a cough at regular intervals; repeated imperfections become a synthetic fingerprint. A real person can restart a thought because she changes her mind, breathe before a difficult admission, laugh at her own terrible joke, or leave a silence that lets the scene land. None of that is a random sound effect.

The next audition has two lanes for each *candidate*: (1) the same 15-second factual line for comparison, and (2) 45–75 seconds **in character** from the actual section piece. A dry, quick voice must be able to stop before the joke; a slower voice must carry tension without sounding like sleep audio. We should deliberately test different timbres, pace, accent texture, age impression and emotional range from licensed source performances. Ten *names* do not prove ten distinct usable characters. An audition fails if a listener cannot tell two candidates apart with the labels hidden. Halli's judgement on an iPhone speaker and headphones is the final ear test.

## Recommended voice grammar across Lifestyle

**One audible doorway per section's real content.** Each room presents the piece's direct Read/Play and a small “Hear this” action. The section's daily voice speaks that edition; a short Jess-like companion thought may be the final beat **inside the same recording**, not a second Jess lecture underneath. The existing glance and interactive Jess button remain as capabilities while this composition is staged. The full conversational Jess uses the same section voice while she is in that room; outside Lifestyle it uses her chosen default. If a user changes the day's voice, the section's *approved* edition selects its matching pre-rendered asset. No extra autoplay, no competing players and no separate speech card to scroll past.

**Personalisation has three honest depths.** First, the app chooses an authored edition using explicit signals already read in that room: a named saved place, a followed topic, a time choice, or the current section. It can say why that piece was chosen in one line. Second, an approved recording can have a few editorial variants such as “new here” or “continue”, but must not pretend to know an individual's day. Third, a truly individual spoken response from Jess requires on-tap synthesis from consented context, bounded characters, an account owner and privacy/latency/cost proof. Never insert a person's private diary or health state into a shared MP3, and never generate a flattering fiction *about* her. The named companion is still disclosed as AI.

**Room-specific jobs:** Read's voice opens one complete article with a human observation, then leaves the full folio one tap away. Books can give a short, rights-cleared introduction to the actual chapter and hand her exact place to the reader. Sky can have a lightly mythical bulletin with its moon fact in an optional cited note. Listen can house the same edition ID and keep the full podcast player. Good life can carry a chosen room's one practical action, with “not today” still a valid choice. Yours plays the exact saved edition, never a fresh personal recap. Those are six distinct editorial jobs, not six copies of a voice shelf.

For *The long way home*, the performance map is concrete: brisk irritation at the disappearing bus; an unforced “Right” when the map dies; the laugh occurs only at the locked gate; the sister's reply is read with a small release, then a quiet ending. One bus brake and a brief bag rustle could belong in a **light-scene** mix, with a clean voice-only mix beside it. No automatic cough. For factual Read, no fictional bus sounds or “hmm” before a number. Public-social commentary, if later approved, names the public source and date, distinguishes a reported conversation from a verified event, and never mines DMs or Community posts.
