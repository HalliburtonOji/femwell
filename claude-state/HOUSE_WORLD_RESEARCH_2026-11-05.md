# FemWell House World — research and product exploration

**5 November 2026 · concept for Ideas, not a main-app build.** Halli proposed Jess and a chosen pet living in a house the user builds and customises, beside her custom garden, under a changing sky: a whole-app world with the presence and agency of a life simulation. This supersedes the previous assumption that a small Jess character in page margins is sufficient. The existing FemWell features and approved botanical identity remain the substrate. No new house, pet, voice default, data schema, public sharing or visual reset is approved or implemented by this note.

## The product thought

**A house you shape, a garden you grow, a sky that changes, and two presences with different jobs.** Jess is her chosen-name assistant/friend who can help with real tasks and conversation. The pet is chosen, named and customisable; it has behaviour and a relationship with Jess, but no hunger meter or punishment for absence. The existing *named bloom* stays the garden's floral companion and accumulated history; it is not silently converted into the pet. A person could therefore have three distinct presences: Jess, pet and bloom. Test whether that feels rich or crowded in a scene before deciding the final cast.

The house is not a decorative menu. It is a place to **build, inhabit and remember**. A book pulled from its shelf opens the actual saved book; a desk note opens the exact Planner item; the window and telescope open the current Sky bulletin/chart; the record player resumes a real episode; a garden gate reaches the actual plants and chapters. Every object has an ordinary one-tap route equivalent so nobody must walk across a 3D room to do something useful. The existing pages become beautifully integrated destinations: Read remains readable editorial type, Listen remains a reliable player, Planner remains a practical calendar. The world can recede while those tasks are in focus.

## Close benchmark study: what to take, what to resist

**Original inference from the comparisons:** many products combine a pet with decorating, and games combine spaces with characters. FemWell's distinctive chance is **a user-built, beautiful world whose props and character scenes are connected to real reading, planning, listening, friendships and reflection, with the source always one tap away**. This claim is a design hypothesis to test, not a market exclusivity claim.

## House/world architecture: proposed experience

### 1. An actual buildable house

- **Start with a beautiful inhabited room.** A curated conservatory/library can be moved and re-skinned immediately. Let users choose a starter architecture (glasshouse, townhouse, cottage atelier) and material palette. Choices change geometry, lighting, textiles and flora, not merely wallpaper hue.
- **Two layers of building.** *Furnish* is easy on day one: tap an object, preview it in valid slots, rotate/move, undo. *Architect* later permits adding rooms, moving walls/windows/doors, changing roofline and garden paths. The final vision must genuinely allow building a personal house; a prototype with one furnished room is explicitly a slice, not the promised whole builder.
- **A small object set with meaningful variation.** A desk may host Planner or writing; a shelf stores real reading; a window frames Sky; a radio/gramophone plays Listen. Furniture is freely placed for expression, but app features never depend on owning an item. One room can hold more than one type of life. No rigid Read-room/Listen-room maze.
- **Phone gestures:** tap-to-place, drag as an option, rotate and undo visible, camera reset, large target boundaries, controls clear of iPhone chrome and bottom nav. Save only confirmed layout changes; show an honest cross-device read-back before promising persistence.

### 2. A cast with distinguishable agency

- **Jess:** the assistant/friend with the existing chosen name, persona, memory and privacy controls. She can move to a chair, turn toward the source, hold an object, take a role in a short scene and stop mid-utterance on “Quiet.” Her useful response must name what it actually read, and action changes require confirmation. Most idle motion is authored/client driven; no LLM call to choose a seat.
- **Chosen pet:** species, name, markings, temperament and favourite spots chosen by the user. A cat may sleep on the books; a dog may wait by the garden door; a bird may perch at the window; a more fantastical botanical creature can be a later art option. Behaviours should be selectable rather than hard-coded stereotypes. It may bring a *real* saved object, react to Jess and be petted/played with, but never demand feeding, punish absence or claim sentience.
- **Named bloom:** the already existing garden companion keeps its name/form/personality, tending and chapter history. The bloom may be visible through the window or in the garden; it is neither erased nor folded into pet identity. A cast size and prominence test should show if three presences are too much; the user can quiet/hide motion individually.
- **Optional user presence:** an abstract silhouette or custom avatar could make shared scenes stronger. Photo-real likeness and bodily data are later privacy/representation decisions, not a required onboarding step.

### 3. The outside is a living part of the home

- The garden is physical land adjoining the house: paths, beds, climbers on the conservatory, a pond or seating place. Existing named plants/chapters become selected visual elements; no entire-history render in the first viewport. She can customise beds and furniture while the approved flora system supplies character and meaning.
- Sky light shifts by device time/local date, and the Moon uses the app's existing calculated lunar state. [NASA's moon-phase explanation](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-phases/) supports the astronomical cycle, but the rendering must use the existing verified source/date rather than inventing one. If location is not supplied, the scene can show time-of-day and lunar art without claiming local weather or a precise view. Live weather is a separate opt-in/provider and licence/cost decision: [Met Office DataHub](https://datahub.metoffice.gov.uk/docs/getting-started) has metered APIs; do not poll per open or assume the free allowance scales to many users.
- A seasonal change is scenery, not a lapse judgement. The house and garden can look beautiful after months away. Plants never die because someone stopped opening the app.

### 4. Content and social journeys inside the world

| Immediate need | World moment | One-tap full destination |
|---|---|---|
| “I have five minutes” | Jess puts a *verified playable* Listen item on the record player; pet settles nearby. | Exact player and transcript/source; offline/broken source is honest. |
| “Continue the story” | A bookmark lies in the open book where the user actually stopped. | Full editorial reader at stored article/paragraph or book/chapter; no 3D text wall. |
| “What is today doing?” | Desk calendar and window sky show the real day; Jess offers one contextual choice. | Actual Planner item, Sky bulletin/chart or Today; no invented meeting or birth details. |
| “I want company” | A porch/guestbook signals a **separately opted-in** Community space. | Existing moderated room or book club; private home contents remain private. No auto-DM or surprise visitor. |
| “I want to make this mine” | Build mode opens on the house with a tray of curated furnishings, plants and material swatches. | Saved layout after acknowledged write; undo/redo and reset. |

**Social extension worth exploring:** an invited visitor sees a *curated public postcard version* of the house/garden, not the live private scene. The user deliberately places shareable objects; private books, journal, cycle, health and Planner traces never leak through a room thumbnail. A Community club can host its own shared room rather than entering someone's private house. This is a separate consent and schema proposal, not part of the first slice.

### 5. Game feel without a second job

The rewarding loop is **choose → make → see a small consequence → revisit or change it**. No streaks, XP, plant survival, forced daily reward or paid currency. Possible delightful events: Jess and pet disagree over a chair; a curtain moves when the sky turns; a book you've actually saved reappears on a side table; an authored sky tale happens by the window; the pet finds a chosen garden spot. Each event can be dismissed, and none blocks reading or planning. House building itself can be satisfying even on days when the user wants no self-improvement.

## Visual directions worth prototyping, not merely recolouring

1. **The Botanical Miniature (recommended first):** a truly dimensional, rotatable cutaway house with painterly plaster, dark timber, brass, stained glass, embroidered textiles and living morning-glory/tendril growth. The garden and changing sky are visible in the same camera. Characters have restrained, physical movement and soft contact shadows. Editorial text appears in a clear DOM layer when an item opens. This could satisfy the Sims-world ambition while keeping FemWell's floral identity and phone legibility.
2. **The Glasshouse Stage:** architecture is a connected conservatory of translucent rooms. Curtains, refraction, shadow and weathered glass create atmosphere; doors do not gate content. More dreamlike and architectural, less dollhouse. Risk: high GPU/transparency cost and less domestic warmth.
3. **The House as Living Folio:** a paper/ink miniature unfolds into rooms, with real botanical specimens crossing page and space. It honours the current magazine/brand type system. Risk: may feel like another page illustration rather than a world she inhabits.

**Visual test:** the same 15-second event in all three: the pet crosses the room, Jess picks up a genuine saved book, daylight fades and the current Moon appears through the window. Compare warmth, realism, touch readability, loading/performance and whether each still looks like FemWell. Do not call a static generated image a working environment.

## Technical and economic reality gate

Current repo: React/Vite/Base44, `three` and `framer-motion` installed; existing Jess profile/memory/action code, `CompanionState`, `GardenChapter` and Sky calculation exist. **No house layout or chosen animal-pet state exists.** A real persistent builder will likely require new entities or fields and ownership rules; propose those for review before schema work. The 50-function cap means extend an existing dispatcher only if server action is truly needed. An authoritative house layout should store compact object IDs/transforms and versioned deltas, not a screenshot or a row for every frame. Treat this as an architecture hypothesis until the exact schema and query limits are measured.

Three technical prototypes should be compared on a real iPhone: (a) a small lazy-loaded WebGL house with baked light and on-demand frames; (b) a layered 2.5D scene with rigged characters; (c) a hybrid with 3D house and 2D character performance. [React Three Fiber's performance guide](https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/next/advanced/scaling-performance) describes demand rendering and adaptive resolution; [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Performance/Guides/Animation_performance_and_frame_rate) warns that WebGL/canvas animation can still cost CPU/GPU even when downloads are small. Those are tools, not proof this app will run smoothly. Measure initial bytes, texture memory, load time, FPS, long-frame count, battery/thermal symptoms and return navigation on iPhone, plus an accessible static/low-motion/text-first mode. Keep AI to explicit Jess interactions and authored scene variations; no model calls for ambient behaviour. Share/cache licensed weather by region and freshness if ever added; never assume free provider pricing scales. The [Kinder World developer farewell](https://www.playkinderworld.com/news) is a real warning that a beautiful, well-loved world can still fail economically.

Privacy/relationship gate: house objects must only be made from user-confirmed sources; memory is inspectable, deletable and separate from merely opening a page. [ICO privacy-by-design guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/guide-to-accountability-and-governance/data-protection-by-design-and-by-default) requires privacy from design time. [Longitudinal AI-companionship research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10079) shows attachment develops over weeks; that is a reason to test Jess's continuity and avoid dependence cues, not a reason to make her cold. No guilt after absence, exclusive-friend lines, auto outreach or secret observation.
