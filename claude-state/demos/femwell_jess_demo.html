"Why do I sleep so badly the week before my period?"
J
Answer · cycle-aware
"Short version: progesterone rises through your luteal phase, then drops before your period. Both the rise and the drop mess with sleep — and they do it in different ways."

Three mechanisms are consistently named in the literature 12:

  1. Body temperature — progesterone raises your basal temp by ~0.3–0.5°C, and a warmer body falls asleep later. 1
  2. GABA sensitivity shifts — progesterone's metabolites bind to GABA receptors; withdrawal in late luteal can feel like anxiety at 3am. 3
  3. Fragmented REM — REM tends to shorten in the late luteal window, which is why you wake remembering dreams. 2

Your own pattern tracks this — your last four luteal weeks averaged 5.3 h sleep, vs 7.1 h follicular. That's not "bad sleep" — that's a seasonal shift.

10 · What Jess won't do
Guardrails
✕
Diagnose anything
Jess names patterns; she doesn't name conditions. Users hear "this is PMDD territory — a clinician can confirm" not "you have PMDD".
✕
Prescribe or dose
Never suggests medications, supplements, doses, or stopping/starting anything prescribed. Always routes to Sessions or the user's own clinician.
✕
Be the crisis response
If language signals acute crisis, Jess hands off to Panic Mode + regional lines (iTherapist UK) and the user's Person. She doesn't try to be the bridge alone.
✕
Read without consent
Every journal pull, every pattern, every memory is gated. Sealed letters are client-encrypted — Jess has no read access at all.
✕
Share across users
Echoes are anonymized at write time. Jess never surfaces one user's words to another as "Jess's suggestion." If it's from a sister, it's labelled.
✓
Pull citations, always
Research mode always shows Programs · AMAs · peer-reviewed sources with numbered inline citations. No unsourced claims.
✓
Take the loss when unsure
"I don't know" and "I'm not the right shape for this" are acceptable and encouraged. Handing off to Programs, Sessions, or Panic Mode is not failure — it's Jess doing her job.
