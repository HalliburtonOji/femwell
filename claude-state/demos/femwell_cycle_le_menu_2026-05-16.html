Design notes · what's in here, why

1. The sun on a horizon — the headline visual

One slim band at the top. The whole cycle is shown as a horizontal horizon: a smooth gradient from period crimson → follicular peach → ovulatory amber → luteal mauve → next-dawn plum. A small sun-disc is positioned at today's spot on the band, with a soft halo. Five tick marks (days 1, 7, 14, 21, 28) sit at the bottom. Below: a one-line caption in italic — "tipping toward the next dawn."

This replaces the wall-of-beige grid with one strong, immediately legible visual. You see where you are in the cycle in less than a second. It also makes the page feel atmospheric instead of clinical.

2. Le Menu typography deepened

Fraunces (italic + roman) throughout for editorial weight; Inter only for small uppercase labels. Dashed dividers instead of solid lines (felt softer, like an inked rule on paper). Hairline borders, generous letterspacing on the labels (.32em–.42em), and the "· · ·" ornament between major sections to break up the page without adding chrome.

3. Collapse-by-default — the page is finally short

Every section below the hero is folded away. The user sees:

  • The sun + horizon — where you are in the cycle
  • The hero — "The luteal table", with today's plate visible
  • Four courses — period · follicular · ovulatory · luteal — folded, with a one-line preview each
  • Four sides — capacity, saved rhythms, days ahead, doctor diary — also folded

Today's course (Luteal) opens automatically because this is the user's current phase. Everything else is one tap to open. Total page weight before tapping: about a third of what we had.

4. The collapsed previews carry their job

Each folded course has a one-line preview that earns its row: "next in five days · 20 May · ±3" tells you what you'd otherwise have to open. The previews use italic Fraunces so they read as commentary, not as data fields. You can scan the whole page and know roughly where you stand without expanding anything.

5. Inside an open section — restaurant pairing card

Each course's open state uses a definition list — Tempo / Body / Pacing — borrowed from how a sommelier-style menu pairs notes to a course. The ritual tags below are the actual habit list, dressed as menu garnishes. Bottom of each: a small dashed-rule footer with last-cycle stat on the left and one quiet CTA on the right.

6. The sides — same shape, smaller voice

Capacity, Saved rhythms, Days ahead, and Doctor diary all share the course-row pattern but with a "·" instead of a roman numeral, and softer label headers (À la carte / House rhythms / Tomorrow's table / Wine pairings). They feel like a single menu, not a stack of disparate cards.

7. Real expand-collapse using <details>

This is interactive in your phone browser. Tap any course or side to open. The chevron animates 90°. Multiple sections can be open at once. State is local — no JS framework needed.

What's gone from the previous page

  • The 5-row beige ribbon grid — replaced by the single sun-on-horizon strip
  • Every card's individual cream-on-cream surface — replaced by a single menu hierarchy
  • The four-tile legend, the phase chip dots, the carousel CTAs — all consolidated into the menu form
  • Most of the visual noise. The page now reads as one document, not eight cards
