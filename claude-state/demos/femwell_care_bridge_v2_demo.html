FemWell · Care Bridge v2 · clinician portal + her control

Her clinician reads what she chose to share, for 14 days, on a read-only portal that leaves a trail.

Care Bridge v2 is how her gynaecologist, endocrinologist, or GP sees the signal behind her symptoms — HRT dosing, flash patterns, sleep, mood arc — without pulling her whole app, her journal, her panic log, or her cycle data into a clinical record she can't control. This demo shows both sides. Her side is a 3-phone flow: invite a clinician, decide what to share, revoke or extend. Clinician side is a desktop-only portal at care.femwells.com, 14-day expiring invite, read-only, async-note-back-only, every page-view and note logged to an UK GDPR + DPA 2018 audit trail she can see.

appendix · build spec

How Care Bridge v2 actually works

Core rules

  1. Clinician never has a FemWell app. Desktop web portal only (care.femwells.com) — mobile is blocked to prevent casual screenshots over coffee.
  2. Magic-link + verified GMC/GMC. Clinician enters their regulator ID on first session; FemWell verifies against the live GMC register (or GMC/AMA for diaspora). Unverified clinician = no portal access.
  3. Single-patient per session. Dr Aisha Patel does not see a roster. Each patient invites him with a separate magic link + separate session. He can hold multiple active windows, but they never aggregate into a "my patients" list.
  4. 14-day default, 7/14/21/30 options. Hard-expire on the day. She can extend once; he can request an extension with one-line reason but cannot unilaterally extend.
  5. Async note only — no chat. One note per session day. She reviews before it's visible to her. No photos, no links, no files attached.
  6. No download. No copy. Portal is render-only. PDF export requires her tap to approve — the PDF is watermarked with her name, his GMC, and the session window.
  7. UK GDPR + DPA 2018 audit is hers. Every view is logged with timestamp/device-fingerprint/IP-prefix; she sees it in real time; she can export it; the log is immutable.
  8. Silent revoke. She ends access. He sees "access ended by patient" — no reason, no re-invite request. She chooses whether to tell him why outside the app.

What we refuse to build

  • No chat thread. Clinical chats become liability; one async note is enough.
  • No in-portal prescribing. The portal shows data; prescribing happens at the visit or via her clinic's own Rx system. FemWell is not an EHR.
  • No "clinician can invite patient". Inversion kills the consent arrow. Patient always initiates.
  • No aggregate clinician view. No "I have 12 patients in peri this week" dashboard. If Dr Aisha Patel wants aggregate peri insight he uses the Pro-educator channel, not his patients' portals.
  • No insurance integration at launch. Care Bridge is between her and her doctor. Not between her data and an insurer.
  • No journal text ever. No sealed letters, no panic log, no body photos. These never cross regardless of clinical justification.
  • No clinician-to-clinician sharing. If she needs a second opinion she invites the second clinician directly.
  • No auto-renew, no silent-extend. Expires always means expires.

UK GDPR + DPA 2018 compliance — what we commit

  1. Lawful basis: explicit patient consent on a per-session basis. No "legitimate interest" carveout.
  2. Purpose-binding: the portal's only purpose is the stated clinical consultation. Data used for anything else (e.g. research) requires a separate consent flow.
  3. Data minimisation: tight payload per scope. The clinician sees rendered views, not raw tables. Server never exposes a "get_all_user_data" endpoint to the portal.
  4. Retention: her data stays on FemWell servers; the portal holds render-only session state. At session expiry, all portal-side state is destroyed.
  5. Subject rights: she can request her audit log, export it, and end access — within the app in under 30 seconds.
  6. Breach response: 72h disclosure to the ICO if any portal session leaks beyond the intended clinician. Patient notified simultaneously.
  7. Data localisation: UK users' data on UK-hosted infra (AWS eu-west-2, London). Expat users' data stays in-region of residence.

Portal-side render contract

The portal fetches rendered views, not raw data. Mental model: the server is the kitchen, the portal gets the plate — and can only see what's on the plate.

  • Each scope maps to a single render endpoint: /session/:id/hrt, /flash, /bleed, /mood, /wearable, /pelvic.
  • Endpoints enforce the consent-scope on the server before returning anything — scope mismatch returns 403, logged.
  • No batch endpoint, no search endpoint, no "download JSON" endpoint. Intentionally missing.
  • Right-click, copy, and screen-capture APIs are blocked via CSP + disable handlers. Screenshot protection is best-effort (desktop OS can always override).
  • Portal is a separate build with its own CSP, served from a separate subdomain — not a route in the main app.
