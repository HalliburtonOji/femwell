FemWell · Partner Sync · dual-side design

Let him read her, not read her phone.

Partner Sync is the narrowest window in the product: the partner sees a phase, a mood, and a "what helps" list — nothing more. No logs, no chats, no metrics. She chooses every bit of what he sees; she can revoke in one tap. The partner's side is a PWA (not a login on her app). This demo covers both sides + the mechanics: consent, digest, revocation, and panic-forwarding behaviour.

Her side · inside FemWell
Phones 1–3 · Profile → Connections → Partner Sync control. She controls the tap, the tuning, the tone. Revoke is always one tap.
Partner side · standalone PWA at with.femwells.com
Phones 4–6 · Home · Week digest · Panic alert. No login — magic link + device binding. Read-only. No logs, no searching, no history beyond 4 weeks.

appendix · build spec

How Partner Sync actually works

Core rules

  1. Partner never has a FemWell account. Standalone PWA at with.femwells.com. No app store. Installable on her request only.
  2. Magic-link + device-bind. He gets a one-time link from her phone (Bluetooth pair or SMS). Once used, the device is bound. New device = new invite from her.
  3. Read-only. His side cannot write to her data. He can send one voice note (≤20s) or pick one of the 4 quick-responses per day. Nothing more.
  4. Four-week rolling memory. His side holds 4 weeks of digests + "what helps" snapshots. Older data is wiped locally and on the relay server.
  5. Server-side "tight pipe". Only 3 payloads per user: phase_band, mood_band, helps_list. Plus two optional: flash_direction, panic_forward. Nothing else ever crosses.
  6. Quiet-hours universal. His app never ping-notifies after 22:00 BST or before 07:00, regardless of his settings.
  7. She always sees what he sees. Phone 3 is a live mirror — if she doesn't see it, it isn't there.

What we refuse to build (on purpose)

  • No chat thread. Chats become logs; logs become evidence in abuse. He picks from 4 responses or records one 20-sec voice note.
  • No cycle predictions for him. He never sees "her period starts in 4 days" — that's hers to share if she wants.
  • No map / location / proximity signal. Never. Not a feature.
  • No "likes", "reactions" on her content. He can heart the weekly digest once. That's it.
  • No cross-partner leaderboard. "Most responsive partner" is the kind of metric that gets women hurt.
  • No alerts to him during her period. She opts in — default is silent.
  • No text-search of history. He cannot search "what did she ask last week". He reads this week, nothing else.
  • No panic auto-forward. Panic mode never notifies him unless she taps "reach Sam" inside Panic. Default is off.

Consent grammar

Each data surface has 3 positions. Never a binary toggle.

PositionWhat it means
offHidden entirely. He sees no placeholder. He can't know it existed.
seeHe sees a summary band (e.g. "tender"). No underlying data.
showHe sees Jess's rewritten version of her words. Still not raw data — always filtered.

Rule: she can flip any row to "off" at any time — the change propagates within 30 seconds. His side shows a "this window is narrower today" row rather than disappearing silently, so he doesn't assume she's ignoring him.

Panic-forward · the exact flow

  1. She opens Panic Mode. Jess never auto-forwards.
  2. Inside Panic, the "reach" card has 4 soft destinations: Sam, Mother, Friend, Samaritans 116 123.
  3. Tapping "reach Sam" shows one box: "what one line do you want him to know?" (2 defaults + blank).
  4. One line + one timestamp crosses. Never a panic log. Never severity. Never "how bad".
  5. Sam's phone gets a gentle banner — no alarm sound, no red UI. Reads "she reached you".
  6. He can pick one of 4 responses or send one voice note. Never call auto-routed.
  7. If he taps "I can't right now", Jess silently offers Halliburton a different reach option. She's never left with just his silence.

Abuse-resistance design

  • No location, ever. Partners of abusive men should not fear geotags inside the app.
  • Revocation is silent on his side. He sees "she's resting this window", not "she revoked you". Gives her plausible safety.
  • Ghost-pause. She can leave the window appearing active while actually showing stale 3-day-old data. If he confronts her, the app looks the same.
  • Trusted-friend escrow. She can name a trusted friend; if her account goes inactive 21+ days, Jess messages the friend — not the partner.
  • Impossible to screenshot her logs. Partner's side physically has nothing to screenshot beyond the window.
  • No "she hasn't opened today" notification. Silence isn't a signal anyone should weaponise.
