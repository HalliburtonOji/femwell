# Femwell: Scheduling & Life Planning Feature Research
**Date:** 16 May 2026  
**Scope:** Deep research into planning/scheduling apps, women's cycle-synced planning, life-stage scheduling needs, and capacity-aware architecture — translated into specific Femwell feature recommendations.

---

## Executive Summary

Femwell has strong foundations in cycle tracking, narrative AI, and community. What it lacks is the **planning layer** — the connective tissue between *knowing* your cycle/body and *acting* on that knowledge in daily life. The most impactful gap is that Femwell tells women how they feel but doesn't yet help them *schedule their life accordingly*. The £75B global femtech market (2026) is consolidating around apps that move from reactive tracking to proactive, personalised life management. Femwell is positioned to lead this — if it closes the planning gap now.

## Part 1: Best-in-Class Scheduling/Planning Apps — What Makes Them Genuinely Useful

### 1.1 Sunsama (Wirecutter's Best Scheduling App 2025)
**Core mechanic:** A structured 10–15 minute morning planning ritual and a 5–10 minute evening shutdown ritual.

**What makes it sticky:**
- **Guided morning session:** Pull tasks from Asana, Trello, Notion, email → estimate duration → time-block onto calendar. The app warns you if your plan exceeds daily capacity.
- **Time estimation built-in:** Forces realistic planning, preventing over-scheduling.
- **Daily shutdown ritual:** Reviews completed vs. incomplete tasks; incomplete tasks are explicitly disposed of (carry forward, reschedule, or remove). Ends with a 1–2 sentence reflection. This creates a psychological boundary between work and rest.
- **Weekly review + planning flow:** Defines *objectives* for the week (not task lists). Combines backward review with forward planning in one workflow.
- **Multi-tool integration:** Google Calendar, Outlook, Asana, Todoist, Notion, Jira, GitHub, Slack.
- **ADHD note:** Multiple users with ADHD specifically praise Sunsama for its structured ritual and time estimation features that address time blindness.

**Key insight for Femwell:** The *ritual* is the product. Planning feels overwhelming without scaffolding. The morning planning session + evening reflection structure is the most replicable and highest-value mechanic in the category.

---

### 1.2 Motion (AI Auto-Scheduling)
**Core mechanic:** AI analyses 1,000+ parameters to auto-schedule tasks on your calendar, then reschedules in real time when things change.

**What makes it stick:**
- **Automatic rescheduling:** When a meeting overruns or an urgent item appears, Motion moves everything else to the next optimal slot without user action.
- **Priority surfacing:** Surfaces the single most important task each hour.
- **Deadline risk alerts:** Warns days or weeks ahead if a task is at risk of missing its deadline.
- **Project management at core:** Not just a calendar tool — full project/task hierarchy flows into the calendar.

**Critical gap (relevant to Femwell):** Motion is described by multiple reviewers as "energy-blind." It optimises for *availability* not *performance* — it will fill your open time but does not know whether that open time is your cognitive peak or your post-lunch trough, let alone your luteal phase day 5. This is the gap Femwell can fill.

---

### 1.3 Reclaim.ai
**Core mechanic:** Dynamic time-blocking that auto-adjusts the entire calendar when priorities shift.

**Distinctive features:**
- **Habit scheduling:** Recurring habits (exercise, focus time, lunch) are protected as flexible blocks that auto-move when meetings appear, rather than being deleted.
- **Buffer management:** Auto-schedules travel time and recovery blocks around meetings.
- **Smart meeting scheduling:** Scheduling links that show real availability across multiple constraints.
- **Free tier:** Genuinely usable free plan — accessible to younger/lower-income users.

**Key insight for Femwell:** Reclaim's habit block concept — a recurring protected slot that *flexes* rather than *disappears* — is directly applicable to Femwell's saved rhythms and self-care routines.

### 1.4 Structured (Mobile-First Visual Day Planner)
**Core mechanic:** A vertical timeline of draggable colour-coded blocks representing the full day.

**What makes it distinctive:**
- **Touch-first design:** Drag to resize, swipe to move — works the way your brain thinks about time as a physical space.
- **Visual density:** See the whole day as a stacked timeline; immediately grasp free vs. busy time.
- **Minimal friction:** No project hierarchy, no integration complexity — just your day as blocks.

**Key insight for Femwell:** The visual day timeline is the most spatially intuitive way to represent a day. Femwell's Today tab could adopt this as the canvas on which Jess AI's narrative is anchored.

---

### 1.5 Fantastical
**Core mechanic:** The gold standard calendar client with natural language event entry.

**Distinctive features:**
- "Remind me to call mum on Tuesday at 3pm" → event created instantly.
- Beautiful week/day/month views.
- Travel time auto-detection.
- Zoom/Meet/Teams built in.

**Key insight for Femwell:** Natural language task/event entry is a table-stakes expectation for AI-native apps in 2026. Jess AI should be able to parse "remind me to take magnesium every night at 9pm" and create a tracked habit from it.

---

### 1.6 Todoist / Things 3
**Core mechanic:** Clean task capture with priority flags, projects, and recurring tasks.

**What makes Todoist sticky:**
- **Inbox capture:** Quick-add from anywhere; process later.
- **Priority levels (P1–P4):** Simple triage.
- **Karma system:** Points for task completion — light gamification that increases daily opens.
- **Recurring tasks:** "Every Monday", "Every 28 days" — essential for cycle-related reminders.

**What's missing (relevant to Femwell):** Todoist's calendar view is a "second thought" — its time-blocking is weak. Neither Todoist nor Things 3 has any concept of energy level or capacity matching.

---

### 1.7 Notion Calendar (formerly Cron)
**Core mechanic:** Calendar that syncs directly with Notion databases and Google Calendar.

**Distinctive features:**
- Any Notion database with a date field appears as calendar events.
- Auto-blocking: blocks corresponding time on personal calendar when a work event is added.
- Scheduling links for external booking.
- Free to use.

**Key insight for Femwell:** If Femwell's users keep any life data in Notion (goals, journaling, health logs), this is the model for how to pull that into a unified calendar view.

## Part 2: Women's Cycle-Synced Planning — What Works and What's Pseudoscience

### 2.1 Alisa Vitti / FLO Living — The Cycle Syncing Method®
**What it is:** Alisa Vitti coined "Cycle Syncing" after researching the female infradian rhythm. The four cycle phases — menstrual, follicular, ovulatory, luteal — each produce measurably different effects on energy, mood, cognition, and physical capacity.

**What works (evidence-supported):**
- **Follicular phase:** Rising estrogen → improved executive function, verbal fluency, learning uptake. Good for: starting new projects, creative brainstorming, learning new skills, social meetings.
- **Ovulatory phase:** Estrogen peaks + testosterone surge → highest energy, verbal confidence, social ease. Good for: presentations, negotiations, networking, high-intensity exercise.
- **Luteal phase:** Rising then falling estrogen, high progesterone → initially productive/detail-oriented, then fatigue, brain fog, reduced executive function in late luteal. Good for (early): detail work, admin, editing. Late luteal: reduce cognitive load, increase recovery.
- **Menstrual phase:** Lowest hormones → rest, reflection, intuitive thinking. Good for: reflection, reviews, strategic big-picture thinking (if energy permits), rest.

**What has scientific support:**
- Hormonal effects on executive function and verbal memory are well-documented in peer-reviewed literature.
- Estrogen's role in dopamine modulation is established — this is why ADHD symptoms worsen in late luteal and perimenopause when estrogen drops.
- The Clue/Healthline menstrual cycle-work research explicitly documents how productivity levels vary by phase.

**What's pseudoscience / overclaimed:**
- Rigid prescriptions ("never do X in the luteal phase") ignore enormous individual variation.
- Food prescriptions tied to specific phases are largely unsupported.
- The claim that every woman *should* cycle-sync work/diet/exercise as a corrective is overstated — the science supports *awareness and adaptation*, not rigid protocol.

**MyFLO app (official Cycle Syncing app):** Period tracker + cycle phase guidance + food/exercise suggestions per phase. Patented algorithm predicts fertile window and ovulation. Does not integrate with a calendar or task system.

## Part 3: Life Planning Beyond Tasks — Whole-Life Apps

### 3.1 Reflectly
**Core mechanic:** AI-guided journaling with mood tracking and CBT/positive psychology prompts.

**What works:**
- Mood + activity log → patterns emerge without spreadsheet effort.
- AI prompts guide reflection rather than leaving blank page anxiety.
- Journal entries become trend data: you can see which activities correlate with better mood.
- Uses CBT frameworks embedded in questions (cognitive reframing built into prompts).

**Key insight for Femwell:** Femwell's Journal section could move from blank journalling to guided prompts that are *cycle-phase aware* — different reflection questions for luteal vs. ovulatory vs. menstrual phases.

---

### 3.2 Finch (Self-Care Pet App)
**Core mechanic:** Habit tracking gamified through caring for a virtual bird — nurture your pet by nurturing yourself.

**What works:**
- Low-stakes, emotionally warm interface reduces the shame associated with habit failure.
- Daily journeys: small self-care actions (breathing, reflection, gratitude) that grow the bird.
- Pattern recognition: connecting mood data to actions over time.
- Designed for mental health — not productivity pressure.

**Key insight for Femwell:** The emotional safety of Finch's approach works because it removes the punitive streak model. Femwell's wellness tracking should celebrate self-compassion over perfectionism — especially during menstrual, late luteal, or perimenopausal low phases.

**Key insight for Femwell:** "On This Day" is enormously powerful for cycle-aware pattern spotting — "last November in the luteal phase, you felt this same way. Here's what helped."

### 3.4 Structured App

## Part 5: Life-Stage-Specific Scheduling Needs

### 5.1 Trying to Conceive (TTC)
**Scheduling needs:**
- Fertile window optimisation: knowing the 5-day fertile window and acting on it, not just seeing it.
- Clinic appointment management: IUI/IVF cycles have complex, time-sensitive scheduling with 48–72 hour trigger windows.
- OPK (ovulation predictor kit) testing reminders: daily morning and evening.
- Two-week wait (2WW) support: the post-ovulation period is anxiety-dense; needs emotional scaffolding not just symptom logging.
- Partner sync: the scheduling needs to be shareable with a partner.
- GP/consultant appointment prep: generating symptom summaries, cycle history, and questions list.

**What's missing in current apps:** None integrate with NHS appointment systems. None offer TTC-specific scheduling rituals or partner sync in the planning layer.

---

### 5.2 Pregnancy
**Scheduling needs:**
- Trimester-aware appointment calendar: 8-week scan, 12-week nuchal, 20-week anomaly, 28-week GTT, 34-week review, 36/38/40-week midwife — these are a fixed schedule most women have to manage manually.
- Birth plan preparation tasks: hospital bag checklist, hypnobirthing practice sessions, perineal massage reminders.
- Nesting task management: home preparation tasks with energy-aware scheduling (first trimester fatigue is real; second trimester energy surge is the optimal window for nesting tasks).
- Kick counter with weekly context (when kicks matter, normal variation by week).
- Pelvic floor exercise scheduling — needs to be a protected daily habit.
- Partner/doula/birth team coordination.

**What's missing:** No app integrates a complete trimester appointment schedule with reminders, AND combines this with nesting/preparation tasks, AND adjusts for pregnancy fatigue.

---

### 5.3 Perimenopausal Women (typically 40–55 in UK)
**Scheduling needs:**
- HRT review scheduling: NICE guidelines recommend 3-month review after starting HRT, then annual reviews.
- NHS Health Check integration: menopause screening now part of routine health checks for 40–74.
- Symptom pattern diary for GP: hot flush frequency/severity, sleep disruption, mood, brain fog — formatted for clinical consultation.
- Medication/supplement reminders: HRT patches (twice weekly), progesterone capsules, magnesium, vitamin D.
- Brain fog compensation: when executive function is reduced, the app should simplify the day and surface only 2–3 priorities.
- NHS Online Hospital: launched January 2026 with menopause as priority — digital consultation scheduling.
- Bone health: exercise scheduling (weight-bearing 3x/week is NICE-recommended).

**What's missing:** No app combines symptom diary + GP prep + HRT review scheduling + capacity-adjusted daily planning in one place. mySysters (Newcastle, UK) offers symptom tracking + GP reports but no planning layer.

---

### 5.4 Teenage Girls (13–18)
**Scheduling needs:**
- School schedule integration: knowing a period is due during GCSE exam week matters.
- Cycle length normalisation: irregular cycles in the first 2–3 years are normal; the app should communicate this to reduce anxiety.
- School kit reminders: period products in school bag, particularly for irregular cycles.
- Mood/symptom communication: helping teenagers identify that low mood, cramps, or fatigue are cyclical and normal.
- Safe, age-appropriate interface: no fertility content, no adult health content.
- Parental transparency controls with privacy defaults.

**Best existing apps:** Luna (UK-focused, PSHE-aligned), Teena (animated cycle journey), MagicGirl (first period focus). None integrate with school schedules.

**What's missing in Femwell context:** Femwell could add a "Student" life stage with timetable integration and exam-aware cycle planning.

---

### 5.5 Postpartum (0–12 months post-birth)
**Scheduling needs:**
- Feed/sleep logging (standard) — but integrated with own recovery tracking.
- 6-week GP check and 8-week baby check scheduling.
- Postnatal depression screening check-ins timed to Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale windows.
- Pelvic floor rehabilitation scheduling.
- Return-to-exercise guidance (running not before 12 weeks, per guidance).
- Sleep deprivation acknowledgement in planning: tasks should auto-reduce when sleep log shows <5 hours.

## Part 6: The Capacity-Aware Planning Architecture

### 6.1 The Core Concept
A capacity-aware planner does not just ask "when are you free?" — it asks "when are you *able*?" It uses available data to model the user's likely cognitive, physical, and emotional capacity at different points in time, and uses that model to:
1. Suggest the right type of task for the right window.
2. Warn when a high-stakes event falls in a likely low-capacity window.
3. Automatically simplify the day plan when capacity signals are low.
4. Offer proactive support (breathing, magnesium reminder, "try this" content) rather than just passive tracking.

### 6.2 Data Inputs Available to Femwell
Femwell already captures or could capture:

| Data Layer | Source | Capacity Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Cycle phase | Cycle tab | Predicted cognitive/physical capacity by phase |
| Cycle day | Cycle tab | Fine-grained within-phase variation |
| Symptoms logged | Track tab | Actual vs. predicted capacity (override) |
| Sleep log | Track tab | Acute capacity modifier (poor sleep = -30% executive function) |
| Mood log | Journal/Track | Emotional capacity signal |
| Life stage | Profile | Pregnancy trimester, perimenopause, postpartum |
| Wearable data (future) | Apple Health / Garmin | HRV, resting heart rate, sleep quality |
| Medication/HRT log | Care Bridge / Track | Hormonal stability signal |

### 6.3 Capacity Model by Cycle Phase
Based on peer-reviewed hormonal research:

**Menstrual (Days 1–5):**
- Cognitive: Low-medium. Introspective, big-picture thinking accessible. Detail work harder.
- Physical: Low. Prostaglandins = fatigue, cramps. Not the day for intense exercise or back-to-back meetings.
- Emotional: Inward, reflective. High sensitivity.
- Femwell action: Minimal task load, prioritise rest and reflection tasks, prompt journalling and weekly/monthly review.

**Follicular (Days 6–13):**
- Cognitive: Rising rapidly. Executive function, verbal memory, learning uptake all increase with estrogen.
- Physical: Rising energy. Best for starting new exercise habits, high-intensity sessions.
- Emotional: Optimistic, curious, socially motivated.
- Femwell action: Front-load new projects, difficult conversations, learning goals. Schedule highest-stakes events here.

**Ovulatory (Days 14–16, approximate):**
- Cognitive: Peak verbal fluency, charisma, communication.
- Physical: Peak strength and power.
- Emotional: Most socially confident, empathetic, persuasive.
- Femwell action: Schedule presentations, job interviews, first dates, GP consultations, performance reviews. High output day.

**Early Luteal (Days 17–23):**
- Cognitive: Detail-oriented, methodical. Good for admin, editing, finishing work.
- Physical: Moderate — progesterone begins to calm system.
- Emotional: Calm, nurturing, focused on completion.
- Femwell action: Admin, organising, completing tasks started in follicular phase, financial reviews.

**Late Luteal (Days 24–28):**
- Cognitive: Reduced executive function, brain fog, reduced working memory. Estrogen drops sharply — dopamine follows.
- Physical: Fatigue, bloating, pain sensitivity.
- Emotional: Heightened emotional reactivity, anxiety, PMS symptoms peak.
- Femwell action: Reduce task load significantly. Surface only 2–3 priorities. Do not schedule high-stakes events. Offer self-care prompts. Warn if important events fall here. ADHD users particularly affected — medication may feel less effective.

### 6.4 Life-Stage Overlays on Capacity Model
The cycle phase model is modified by life stage:

**TTC:** Overlay fertile window anxiety and 2WW emotional load.

**Pregnancy:** First trimester = severe fatigue (even if cycle phase would normally show follicular energy). Third trimester = physical discomfort limits any task.

**Perimenopause:** Cycle phase predictions become less reliable. Symptom logging replaces phase prediction as primary signal. Brain fog and sleep disruption are persistent capacity reducers, not cycle-phase-specific.

**Postpartum:** Sleep deprivation is the dominant capacity modifier. Cycle may not have returned.

**ADHD/neurodivergent:** Luteal phase amplifies ADHD symptoms significantly (estrogen modulates dopamine). App should have an ADHD-aware mode that further reduces cognitive load in late luteal.

### 6.5 What the Planner Does with Capacity Data

**"Traffic light" day rating:**
- Green: High-capacity day → Jess AI suggests taking on stretch goals, scheduling difficult tasks.
- Amber: Medium capacity → Stick to planned tasks, reduce meetings if possible.
- Red: Low-capacity day → AI simplifies the day to 2–3 essentials, offers self-care prompts, suggests moving non-urgent tasks.

**Proactive event warnings:**
"Your performance review is in 3 days. Based on your cycle, that's likely to be a late-luteal day for you. Would you like to prepare your key talking points now, while you're in a higher-energy phase?"

**Task type matching:**
- Creative tasks → follicular/ovulatory.
- Detail/admin → early luteal.
- Rest and reflection → menstrual.
- Social/communication → ovulatory.

## Part 7: Habit and Routine Design — What Works

### 7.1 Atomic Habits Principles and App Translation

**The Four Laws of Behaviour Change (James Clear):**

| Law | Principle | App feature translation |
|---|---|---|
| Make it obvious | Implementation intention: "I will [habit] at [time] in [location]" | Habit setup wizard that captures *when*, *where*, *what triggers it* — not just "what" |
| Make it attractive | Pair habit with something enjoyable | Habit stacking builder: "After [existing habit], I will [new habit]" |
| Make it easy | Reduce friction to 2-minute start | Default habit duration = 2 minutes; user can extend |
| Make it satisfying | Immediate reward / streak / evidence of progress | Visual streak, cycle-phase progress rings, Jess AI acknowledgement |

**Implementation intention specificity matters:** Research by Gollwitzer shows people who specify *when*, *where*, and *how* are 2–3x more likely to follow through vs. those who set a vague goal.

**Femwell application:** When a user adds a habit (e.g., "take magnesium"), the app should prompt: "When do you want to take it? After which existing habit? Where will you keep it?" rather than just creating a reminder.

### 7.2 Habit Stacking — Particularly Relevant for Femwell
Habit stacking (BJ Fogg, Tiny Habits) anchors a new habit to an existing behaviour. This is more reliable than time-based cues because the existing behaviour is more reliably noticed.

**Femwell examples:**
- "After I check my cycle this morning, I will log my mood."
- "When I make my morning tea, I will take my vitamin D."
- "After my evening Jess check-in, I will do 2 minutes of pelvic floor exercises."

The app should suggest habit stacks based on existing user behaviours it can observe (cycle check frequency, time-of-day patterns in the app).

### 7.3 Social Accountability
Research data: Social accountability improves habit success from 25% to 65%. Professional coaching: 45% to 78%. Being part of a group: 95% more likely to complete goals (ASTD).

Femwell's Community section is the engine for this. Features to activate it:
- Shared accountability check-ins ("Did you drink 2L of water today? 847 other women in your cycle phase did.")
- Group challenges: "Follicular phase fitness week — 500 women joined."
- Opt-in partner habit sharing (relevant for couples doing TTC together).

### 7.4 What Makes Tracking Fail
- **87% of habit apps fail to achieve sustained engagement** due to simplistic streak counters and insufficient personalisation.
- Punitive streak models (missing one day resets to zero) create shame and abandonment.
- Too many habits simultaneously → success rate drops sharply. Recommendation: maximum 3 active habits; wizard forces prioritisation.
- No connection between tracking and insight → logging without learning → abandonment.

**Femwell antidote:**
- Flexible streaks: "You've completed this habit 18 of the last 21 days — that's a genuine habit."
- Phase-aware habit expectations: missing an exercise habit during menstruation phase is not a failure — it's cycle-appropriate.
- Monthly habit review built into the cycle review ritual.

## Part 8: Top 10 Planning Features Femwell Is Missing (Ranked by Impact)

### Rank 1: Guided Daily Planning Ritual with Capacity Awareness
**What it does:** A structured 5–10 minute morning session (guided by Jess AI) that: shows today's capacity level (based on cycle phase + logged symptoms + sleep), pulls in any tasks or appointments from the day, time-blocks the day onto a simple visual timeline, and sets a single daily intention.

**Which app does it best:** Sunsama (ritual structure) + Motion (capacity-aware auto-scheduling).

**How it works in Femwell:**
- Jess AI opens the ritual: "Good morning. Today is Day 19 of your cycle — you're in early luteal. Your energy for detail work is good but keep your task list manageable. You have 2 appointments and 3 tasks. Want to plan your day?"
- User confirms, moves, or adjusts tasks.
- Time-blocked visual appears (like Structured app's vertical timeline).
- Day begins with a single intention ("Today I'm focusing on: ___").

**Life stages that benefit most:** All stages — but particularly perimenopause (brain fog compensation), TTC (clinic appointment days need careful scheduling), postpartum (sleep deprivation adjustment).

---

### Rank 2: Capacity-Aware Task Scheduler ("Smart Load Balancer")
**What it does:** Femwell's planning engine knows your predicted capacity for each day of the cycle and automatically distributes tasks — surfacing cognitively demanding tasks in follicular/ovulatory phases and reducing the load in late luteal/menstrual.

**Which app does it best:** Motion (AI scheduling) — but without hormonal awareness. The Agenda (cycle overlay on calendar) — but without AI task distribution.

**How it works in Femwell:**
- User captures tasks with type tags (creative, admin, social, physical, medical).
- Femwell's capacity model assigns each task to the optimal phase window.
- User sees a phase-aligned task queue: "These 3 tasks are best done in your follicular phase (5 days away). Here's what to focus on today."
- High-stakes events automatically generate a warning if they fall in a low-capacity phase.

**Life stages that benefit most:** Perimenopausal women (unpredictable capacity), ADHD women (luteal phase amplification), teenagers (exam scheduling).

### Rank 3: Evening Reflection and Shutdown Ritual
**What it does:** A structured 3–5 minute evening ritual that: reviews what was completed, disposes of incomplete tasks (tomorrow / later / remove), offers a cycle-aware reflection prompt, and creates a psychological boundary between active and rest mode.

**Which app does it best:** Sunsama (best shutdown ritual in the category).

**How it works in Femwell:**
- Jess AI at 8pm (user-configurable): "How did today go? You planned 4 tasks. You completed 2."
- Incomplete task disposal: quick tap to carry forward, reschedule, or remove.
- Reflection prompt changes by cycle phase:
  - Menstrual: "What does your body need tonight?"
  - Follicular: "What are you excited to start tomorrow?"
  - Luteal: "What's one thing you can let go of?"
- Optional: quick mood/energy log (1 tap, not a form).
- Journal entry pre-populated with the day's summary (optional to save).

**Life stages that benefit most:** Perimenopausal women (processing brain fog days), postpartum (acknowledging what *did* get done despite exhaustion), TTC (2WW emotional processing).

### Rank 4: Phase-Aware Habit Engine with Habit Stacking
**What it does:** Habit tracking that accounts for cycle-phase capacity rather than demanding uniform daily performance; built on implementation intention setup and habit stacking scaffolding; flexible streaks that don't penalise phase-appropriate rest.

**Which app does it best:** Reclaim.ai (flexible protected blocks) + Finch (compassionate framing) + Atomic Habits principles.

**How it works in Femwell:**
- Habit setup wizard: "What habit? After which existing routine? Where will you be? What will remind you?"
- App suggests habit stacks based on observed behaviour: "You check Femwell every morning at 7:30am — want to stack your magnesium reminder here?"
- Phase-adaptive expectations: high-effort habits (exercise, cold shower) show amber targets during menstrual phase; rest-supporting habits (sleep by 10pm, evening walk) auto-promoted in late luteal.
- Streak = "21 of last 28 days" not "0 days (you missed yesterday)."
- Monthly habit review built into cycle review ritual.

**Life stages that benefit most:** All — but especially teenagers (building first period-aware habits), TTC (OPK testing habit), perimenopausal (medication adherence).

## Part 9: The Capacity-Aware Scheduling Architecture — Technical Spec

### 9.1 Data Model

```
UserCapacityProfile {
  cycle_phase: enum [menstrual, follicular, ovulatory, luteal_early, luteal_late]
  cycle_day: int
  life_stage: enum [general, TTC, pregnant_T1, pregnant_T2, pregnant_T3, postpartum, perimenopause, menopause]
  logged_symptoms: array [fatigue, cramps, brain_fog, mood_low, mood_high, energy_high, energy_low, ...]
  sleep_hours: float (from log)
  medication_logged: bool
  wearable_data: optional { hrv, resting_hr, sleep_score }
  
  computed:
    capacity_score: 0–100 (algorithm below)
    capacity_label: enum [red, amber, green]
    recommended_task_types: array [creative, admin, social, physical, rest, reflection]
    cognitive_load_budget: int (number of tasks recommended)
}
```

### 9.2 Capacity Score Algorithm (v1 — without wearable data)

```
Base score by cycle phase:
  Menstrual: 45
  Follicular: 75
  Ovulatory: 90
  Luteal early: 70
  Luteal late: 40

Modifiers (additive):
  Fatigue logged: -15
  Brain fog logged: -20
  Cramps logged: -10
  Sleep < 5 hours: -25
  Sleep 5–6 hours: -15
  Sleep 7–8 hours: 0
  Sleep > 8 hours: +5
  Mood high logged: +10
  Energy high logged: +10
  Medication logged (HRT, ADHD): +5
  
Pregnancy modifiers:
  T1 (weeks 1–12): -20 (fatigue)
  T2 (weeks 13–27): base
  T3 (weeks 28–40): -10 (discomfort)
  
Life stage modifier:
  Perimenopause (cycles irregular flag): base score uses symptom log primarily, not phase prediction

Final: clamp to [0, 100]
Green: 65–100 | Amber: 35–64 | Red: 0–34
```

### 9.3 Planning Engine Behaviours by Capacity Level

**Green (65–100):**
- Show full task list.
- Suggest stretch goal or optional extra.
- Encourage high-value task scheduling (presentations, difficult conversations, creative work).
- Jess tone: energetic, forward-looking.

**Amber (35–64):**
- Show planned tasks, no additions.
- Gently suggest delegating or moving one non-urgent task.
- Flag if any high-stakes event is today.
- Jess tone: steady, supportive.

**Red (0–34):**
- Reduce to 2–3 essential tasks maximum.
- Auto-suggest moving non-urgent tasks to next green phase.
- Offer self-care prompt (rest, magnesium, gentle movement, warm bath, calling a friend).
- If red day precedes high-stakes event: "Your presentation is tomorrow. Your energy is low today — let's do a 5-minute prep now so you feel ready."
- Jess tone: compassionate, permission-giving.

### 9.4 Proactive Warnings System
The system should scan the calendar 7 days ahead and surface:
- High-stakes events (user-tagged) falling on predicted red days.
- Medical appointments without pre-prepared symptom summary.
- Habit streaks at risk during low-capacity phases.
- Fertility window (TTC users) overlapping with planned busy/travel days.
