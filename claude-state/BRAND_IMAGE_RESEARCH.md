# FemWell — Brand-Image Research Brief (botanical illustrative identity)
> 2026-06-17 · feeds BRAND_IDENTITY.md §4 (botanical system) + §5 (bloom). Sources cited below.

## Why this brief
Scope widened from the bloom to the whole **botanical/illustrative brand language**: leaves & veining, corner treatments & framing, section dividers/flourishes, edge/texture. Goal: a cohesive, crafted, *restrained* system — amazing, never busy.

## What the best brands do (findings)

### 1. Restraint is the craft (Aesop)
Aesop's identity is "restraint with intention" — classical serif wordmark, **minimal ornamentation**, charcoal not pure black, "maximum effect with minimal materials," content that "feels like publishing rather than promotion." **Takeaway for us:** the botanical system must read as *editorial restraint* — hairline, low-opacity, one motif per fold. Ornament earns its place; it never decorates for its own sake. (Sources: Latterly, Brand Vision, Design Anthology.)

### 2. Botanical line as *structure*, not sticker (Art Nouveau / Morris)
Defining features: **organic curving lines derived from plants**, the **whiplash curve** (a sharply accelerating, asymmetric S-shape), **asymmetric composition**, undulating lines "in the form of flower stalks, buds, vine tendrils." Henry van de Velde: **"a line is a force"** — direction + energy, not a boundary. Art Nouveau used **organic growth patterns as structural logic**; William Morris's vines twist and repeat with stylised irises. Critically: **"restraint prevents the motif from feeling theme-park literal."** **Takeaway:** our vines/sprigs use whiplash S-curves and *grow* from a corner/stem; leaves vary by species; nothing is a stiff symmetric arc. (Sources: Wikipedia "Whiplash (decorative art)", Solis Prints Art Nouveau overview, Grokipedia.)

### 3. The fleuron — divide with an ornament, not a slab
The **fleuron** is the oldest typographic ornament: an inline mark used to **divide paragraphs/lists** or fill space "in a highly stylised way." Modern editorial dividers use **hand-drawn vine/leaf separators** with "perfect symmetry and balanced proportions." **Takeaway:** offer a **fleuron divider** (chapter-grade) alongside the everyday leaf rule and a sprig divider — a small ornament reads more crafted than a plain hairline. (Sources: Typography Fall 2018 "Fleuron"; Dreamstime leaf/flourish divider libraries.)

### 4. Corners & frames: botanical corners that "grow"
Ornamental-corner libraries (ivy/vine "twisting botanical corners", scroll/floral corner frames) are a standard editorial device for stationery, labels, certificates, menus. The tasteful versions are **delicate, corner-anchored, and asymmetric** (a sprig growing inward), not a heavy full border. **Takeaway:** a corner **sprig**, an engraved **carved** double-rule, a **tendril** curl, and a four-corner **frame** for hero surfaces only. (Sources: Century Library ornamental-corner/ivy-vine frame sets; Adobe Stock "leafy vine corner flourish".)

### 5. Women's-wellness palette & tone
Category cues: **warm neutrals — blush, sand, warm ivory — signal warmth, emotional safety, inclusivity** ("particularly effective for women's health/maternal wellness"); earth tones read botanical/organic; Clue's **deep purples + sophisticated data-viz** make menstrual health feel "intelligent and empowering." **Takeaway:** our cream-paper + plum world with one gold / one crimson is already on-category; the botanical line-art in gold/sage/plum reinforces "warm, safe, crafted, intelligent" without going literal-floral. (Sources: Sarah Sherr "visual identity for health & wellness brands"; go-go-gaia & Woman&Home period-app comparisons; Alfa Charlie "uncharted visual identity for women's-health startups".)

## Tasteful vs cluttered (the line we hold)
| Tasteful | Cluttered |
|---|---|
| One motif per fold (corner OR divider OR flourish) | A corner + a frame + a divider + a wallpaper all at once |
| Hairline strokes, low opacity, over paper | Filled/3D leaves, drop-shadowed clip-art |
| Whiplash/asymmetric growth, varied species | One leaf shape stamped & mirrored everywhere |
| Frame a *hero*; sprig a *corner* | Frame on every card; motif behind body text |
| Static, GPU-cheap, no blur on motifs | Animated/blurred motifs, per-frame filter cost |

## How it cohered into our system (→ BRAND_IDENTITY.md §4)
Leaf library (6 species + venation, vein-gradient) · corner system (sprig / carved / tendril / frame) · dividers (leaf rule / sprig / fleuron) · header flourish · all hairline, low-opacity, one-per-fold, static, no blur. Bloom (§5) stays the centrepiece.
