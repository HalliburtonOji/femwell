Expert Layer — Governance Framework

Halli asked us to "do the governance calls." This turns the open questions in the scope doc into a decided framework: a recommended answer for every gate, with the rationale, each one tagged as a PRODUCT call we have decided, a COMMERCIAL/CLINICAL call Halli owns, or a LEGAL-RATIFY item that needs a qualified UK lawyer's sign-off before any launch.

We are not lawyers. Everything below is a recommended product position to take to counsel — it is not legal advice and does not bind. The LEGAL-RATIFY items must be signed off by a qualified UK lawyer (with clinical-governance input) before the expert layer carries any real traffic. No code is to be written until the gate checklist at the end is green.

0. What the layer is (unchanged from scope)

A trust + anti-misinformation wedge: verified UK clinicians (GMC / NMC / HCPC) provide general, non-individualised information and misinformation correction in a surface quarantined from peer chat (the Flo model). It is the one monetisable layer — every peer surface stays free. Askers are anonymous (author_hash, crisis-checked + moderated like every other input); only the expert is identified, because accountable authority is the whole point.

1. Clinician verification method PRODUCT LEGAL-RATIFY

"Verified" has to mean something — a fake clinician badge is a serious harm and liability. Verification is three checks, not one:

  • Register check (the credential is real + current). Confirm the registrant exists, the number matches the name, and status is "Registered" with no conditions/suspensions/sanctions, on the live public register: GMC — the online List of Registered Medical Practitioners (LRMP); NMC — the online register search; HCPC — the online register search. All three are free public lookups and are the source of truth.
  • Identity check (the person is that registrant). The register proves a number exists; it does not prove the applicant owns it. Add a one-time identity step: a live video ID check against photo ID, plus a corroborating link to the registration (e.g. an employer / practising-privileges letter, or a GMC "registrant connect" / confirmation in the registrant's own name).
  • Scope + standing. Record their declared scope of practice (a dietitian does not answer about prescribing); confirm good standing at point of check.
Recommendation: manual vetting by a named owner at launch (volume is low; a vendor or register-API automation comes later). Re-verify every 6–12 months and before each AMA — registration status, conditions and sanctions change over time. Store: name, body, number, verified_at, verified_by, status_at_check, scope_of_practice. The public badge shows body + registration number so any member can self-verify on the register.

Decided (product): the three-check process + manual-at-launch + re-verification cadence + what the badge shows. Needs ratify (legal/ICO): whether the identity-proofing standard is sufficient, and the lawful basis + retention policy for storing verification evidence (it is personal data).

2. Medical-advice boundary, scope limits + disclaimers PRODUCT CLINICAL LEGAL-RATIFY

The danger is an answer drifting from general information into individualised advice, which creates a clinician–patient duty of care and professional + platform liability (GMC Good Medical Practice; the Bolam standard). The boundary is enforced by product design, not just copy.

Hard scope limits (enforced, not aspirational)

  • No diagnosis. Experts never tell an individual what they have.
  • No prescription or medication changes. No "start/stop/change your dose."
  • No individualised treatment advice or triage. No "you should…", no interpreting a specific person's results, scans, or symptoms.
  • No emergency handling. Anything acute routes to 999 / NHS 111, never to an expert queue.
  • General framing only. Answers address the theme ("people with PCOS often find…"), never the person.

The disclaimer / interstitial

Every expert answer carries a persistent, non-dismissable line:

This is general information, not personal medical advice. For advice about you, see your GP or call NHS 111. In an emergency, call 999.

Enforcement mechanics

  • Banned-topic routing: questions seeking diagnosis, dosage, "should I stop my meds," or result interpretation are auto-flagged and answered with a templated "we can't advise on your individual care — here's who can" response; they never reach an expert.
  • Pre-publication review (async): a named reviewer with clinical input signs off each answer before it publishes. (This is why async-first — see §7.)
  • Clinician guidance/training: experts get a one-page content policy (general-only, the banned list, the framing) before they answer.

Decided (product): the hard scope limits, general-only framing, the interstitial's presence, banned-topic routing, pre-publication review. Needs ratify (legal + clinical): the exact disclaimer wording and whether it is contractually sufficient; where precisely the duty-of-care line sits (Bolam / GMC GMP); sign-off of the clinician guidance by a qualified clinician.

3. How experts stay quarantined from peer chat PRODUCT

This is the single clearest call, and it is fully a product decision.

Recommendation: experts operate only inside the dedicated "Ask an Expert" / AMA surface — a separate IA node. They do not post in rooms, Circles, the Lounge, comments, Echo Wall, Witness, Phase Twin, games, or Book Club. No expert badge appears in any peer surface. Technically the expert role is scoped to the AMA entities only — expert accounts cannot author CommunityPost / Comment / Echo / etc. The peer wall stays free and peer-only; clinicians never carry medical authority into casual chat (locked principle #11; the Flo separation of peer "Secret Chats" from expert content).

Decided (product): full quarantine, role-scoped to the AMA surface, no expert presence anywhere peer-to-peer. Askers stay anonymous; only the expert is identified.

4. Reporting / escalation + crisis handling PRODUCT LEGAL-RATIFY

  • Crisis intercept on the question input — the same UK-resources crisis pre-check as every other text box, fired before a question can reach an expert or queue. A crisis disclosure never becomes an expert Q. (Reuses the existing spine; must be wired, not assumed.)
  • Question moderation — the same OpenAI-moderation + keyword + health-vocab allowlist as comments, so clinical talk is not wrongly removed but abuse is.
  • Reporting an answer — members can report an expert answer; reports route to the named human reviewer. A serious clinical-safety report temporarily hides the answer pending review.
  • Escalation ladder — member report → human reviewer → (clinical-safety concern) de-list the expert → (a clinician acted unprofessionally) consider a referral to their regulator (GMC/NMC/HCPC). Complaints + appeals owner is named (Halli).
  • Crisis inside a thread — if an asker replies with crisis content, the crisis intercept fires again.

Decided (product): crisis-on-question, moderation reuse, report routing, the escalation ladder, named complaints owner. Needs ratify (legal / OSA): the threshold + duty for referring a clinician to their regulator; appeals-process adequacy under the Online Safety Act.

5. How it sits under UK rules LEGAL-RATIFY

RegimeThe lineOur position (recommended)
CQC (Care Quality Commission) General health information + peer support is not a "regulated activity." The line is crossed only by "treatment of disease, disorder or injury" or "diagnostic and screening procedures" via a clinician–service-user relationship. Design so no regulated activity occurs — the §2 scope limits (no diagnosis / treatment / triage) are exactly what keeps the surface outside CQC registration. Re-test if scope ever expands toward individualised advice. Lawyer confirms the surface as designed is not a regulated activity.
Advertising of health services (ASA/CAP + GMC/NMC social-media + advertising guidance) Health claims must be truthful and not misleading; testimonials for health services can mislead; clinicians must follow their regulator's advertising/social-media rules. No efficacy/cure claims; "general information" framing throughout; experts bound by a content policy reflecting their regulator's guidance; the monetised badge must not imply individualised care. Counsel + each regulator's guidance.
OSA 2023 (Online Safety Act / Ofcom) The asker side is user-generated content on a user-to-user service → illegal-content + moderation + reporting + appeals duties already apply. Expert answers add a provider-content dimension. Fold the expert surface into the same OSA risk assessment + moderation/reporting/appeals already required for Community; the 18+ gate applies. Part of the Community legal floor.
ICO / UK GDPR Questions can contain special-category (health) data → explicit consent + lawful basis. Expert verification data (name, number, ID evidence) is personal data needing a retention policy. Extend the Community DPIA to cover both the asker's health data and the expert verification data; explicit special-category consent on the ask flow; a defined retention policy for verification evidence.

All four are LEGAL-RATIFY. The product positions above are designed to keep the surface in the safest lane (general-info, not a CQC regulated activity, no misleading claims, OSA-covered, DPIA-extended) — but a qualified UK lawyer must confirm each before launch. These overlap the OSA/ICO legal floor already on the needs-Halli list.

6. Named accountable owner PRODUCT CLINICAL

Recommendation: Halli is already the named OSA/ICO accountable person and owns expert vetting, complaints, and de-listing. Add a named clinical reviewer — a registered clinician — to sign off answer-review, because Halli is not a clinician and clinical-safety review needs qualified input. Until that reviewer exists, the layer does not launch.

Decided (product): Halli owns governance/complaints/de-listing. Open (clinical hire): name the clinical reviewer for answer sign-off.

7. Scope at launch — async vs live PRODUCT

Recommendation: async-only first. Every answer is reviewable before it publishes, which is the entire safety model. Live AMA is far harder to moderate in real time and raises real-time crisis + advice-drift risk. Add live AMA only once async is proven and a real-time moderation capability exists.

Decided (product): async-only at launch.

8. Doctor-Export exclusion PRODUCT

Recommendation: the GP/Doctor-Export excludes expert Q&A. It is general information, not the user's clinical record, and bundling it could imply individualised advice. (Already specced as a whole-life rule — verify when built.)

9. Commercial model COMMERCIAL LEGAL-RATIFY

Recommendation: experts are the one monetisable layer (peer surfaces always free). At launch, a small number of vetted clinicians on a paid sessional/honorarium basis or a reputable-organisation partnership (e.g. a menopause clinic). Avoid pure-volunteer (weak accountability/reliability) and avoid pharma-sponsored experts (conflict of interest + advertising-rule exposure).

Halli's call (commercial): paid vs partnership. Needs ratify (legal): the expert contract — duties, indemnity, the experts' own professional cover, and the liability carve-outs (overlaps §2 + the ToS work below).

10. Liability / ToS / indemnity / insurance LEGAL-RATIFY

Who carries the risk when an answer is wrong or read as advice? This is wholly a legal item: platform ToS carve-outs, the "not medical advice" framing as a contractual term, the experts' own professional indemnity, and the platform's insurance. Draft + sign off alongside the OSA/ICO legal floor.

Entirely LEGAL-RATIFY. Nothing here is a product call — it is drafted and signed off by counsel.
