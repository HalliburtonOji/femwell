1 · The one return-worthy idea

"How do you want to move today?" — a feeling-led picker, never a plan

Mirror asks "what to wear to how you feel." Move asks the same of the body: she tells us her energy/mood, and we surface movement that matches it — we never prescribe. The reason this is return-worthy is that it inverts every other fitness product: those tell you what you should do (a plan, a streak, a readiness score); Move meets you where you already are. Low energy is a valid answer that gets a real, joyful response (a 6-minute stretch, a slow walk audio), not a guilt trip.

Picker moods (feeling, not intensity plan): Restless / need to shake it off Heavy / low battery Strong / want to feel powerful Tender / be gentle with me Playful / want fun Foggy / clear my head Each maps to real content in the pool (dance 39 & run 29 for restless; yoga/stretch/mobility ~64 for tender; strength 194 for strong; dance for playful; walk 26 for foggy).

The cycle × exercise science — honest, not a slogan

"Cycle syncing your workouts" is a huge marketing trend. The honest read of the literature is: real, mixed, and mostly weaker than the slogans claim. We label each claim's strength inline so we never launder a fitness slogan as science.

  • WEAK / CONTESTED Strength & the cycle. The best current evidence — an umbrella review and meta-analyses — finds trivial effects of cycle phase on strength and on muscle adaptation (Hedges g < 0.2; effect sizes 0.01–0.14, the largest being early-vs-late follicular at 0.14). ~90% of moderate-to-high-quality studies found no significant difference between phases. The reviewers call it "premature to conclude" hormones meaningfully change performance, and recommend individualising, not phase-programming. So: do NOT tell a woman she's "stronger in her follicular phase" as fact. (Colenso-Semple 2023, Frontiers/PMC10076834; Meta-analysis, PMC10818650)
  • REAL, CONDITIONAL Luteal heat / fatigue. Core temperature is genuinely ~0.3–0.7°C higher in the luteal phase (progesterone's thermogenic effect). In hot, humid conditions this measurably shortens time-to-fatigue in prolonged exercise; in temperate conditions there's no performance difference, and in well-trained women no difference even in heat. So the honest note is narrow: "if you're exercising in heat and feel like you're running warmer in the back half of your cycle, that's real — ease off, hydrate," not "the luteal phase makes you weak." (Janse de Jonge 2012, PubMed 22776870; MC thermoregulation review 2024)
  • MODERATE BUT MIXED Injury / joint laxity. Knee (ACL) laxity is lowest in the follicular phase and rises through ovulation/luteal; a meta-analysis found moderate evidence of higher ACL-injury incidence around the ovulatory phase. But the authors call it "mixed and conflicting," and this matters mainly for high-impact, cutting/pivoting sport — not a walk or a mobility flow. Frame only as gentle body-awareness, never fear. (Herzberg 2017 meta-analysis; Neuromuscular surrogates review, PMC9879429)
  • STRONG Movement for mood. This is the best-evidenced claim on the board and should be the emotional spine. Meta-analyses put exercise's effect on depression and anxiety at a moderate SMD ≈ −0.4 to −0.45, with sessions of 30–40 min, 3–5×/week most effective — but benefits appear even from short bouts. This is our licence to say "move because it changes how you feel today," truthfully. (Exercise & depression/anxiety meta-analysis, PMC11309647)
The frame: "Move with your energy, not against it." Self-directed, never a rule. The one cycle-aware element is an honest editorial note ("many women notice more energy in the first half, more tenderness before their period — but bodies differ, and the science on 'cycle-syncing workouts' is thinner than the internet says"), NOT a filtered feed.

3 · Whole-life + cross-surface roles

Move is a life domain, not a gym. Every surface gets a named role:

  • Today: the feeling picker + one "movement snack" card, tinted (not dictated) by cycle phase & time of day.
  • Journal: "how did moving make you feel?" prompt (mood, not metrics) — feeds the mood evidence loop, never logs calories or weight.
  • Community: a Movement room — "shook it off today", couch-to-anything, dance wins, ability-inclusive; anonymous-safe, celebratory not competitive.
  • Nutrition: gentle bridge to "fuelling for energy" (energy, not restriction) — must inherit the same anti-diet denylist.
  • Lifestyle: Move is a domain board here; its videos live in the existing For You / Listen (walk audios) / Read surfaces.
  • Health: movement's real clinical wins framed as capability — bone density, pelvic floor, perimenopause strength, mood — sourced, never aesthetic.
  • Pulse / Trends: "you tend to move most when you feel restless" — pattern reflection about mood, not a performance dashboard or streak.
  • Planner: book a walk with a friend, block 15 min for a stretch — movement as a plan you keep, life-integrated.
  • Profile: her movement fingerprint — favourite feelings/sub-types — no fitness "level" ranking.
  • Jess: the "I don't want to but…" companion — talks her into the smallest kind thing, never nags; "want a 5-minute one?" This is Move's signature Jess role.
  • Events: local UK classes — dance, walking groups, swimming, women-only sessions.
  • Deals: kit, classes, trainers — inclusive sizing surfaced first; never weight-loss products.

4 · Tone + anti-patterns

Warm smart-friend, joyful, UK English, no emoji, size/age/ability-inclusive. The test for every card: "would she do this even if it changed nothing about her body?" If the copy only makes sense as a means to shrink, it fails.

HARD DENY — these framings never ship:
  • Calorie-burn / "torch" / "burn fat" framing
  • "Bounce back" / postpartum-shrink / "get your body back"
  • Weight loss as motivation or reward
  • "No excuses" / "earn it" / punishment / guilt / streak-shaming
  • Before/after imagery or "transformation"
  • Aesthetic "tone / sculpt / lean / snatched / red-carpet-ready"
  • Thin-ideal or single body type as the visual default
  • Ability-shaming ("if she can, no excuse") or intensity as the only virtue
POSITIVE REFRAMES — the vocabulary we build in: strength & capability · energy & aliveness · mood & head-clearing · pleasure & play · longevity & bone/heart/brain health · "rest is training" · "shake it off" · feeling powerful · being able to do the things you love for longer · movement you'd do even if it changed nothing about your body.

This isn't only ethics — it's the market. The Global Wellness Summit named the "over-optimization backlash" its defining 2026 trend: a shift away from measurement and performance toward joy, connection and embodied care. (GWS trend, 2026)
