Library, Games & User-Created Groups

A heavyweight brainstorm reconciling Halli's new idea — a Library room, a Games room, and member-creatable groups (book clubs hosted by Jess OR members) — with the Circles work already built. Research + thinking only; nothing here is built. Dated 2026-06-11.

Halli's words: "a room called library and another specifically for games, and specific groups users can create and join and have meaningful conversations… book clubs hosted by Jess or others hosted by users and can let others join. So basically groups for different things."

The single hardest question this raises is not "can we build groups" — it's how does a user-created, member-hosted space sit safely inside an 18+, anonymous-first, UK women's-health app under the Online Safety Act, and how does it relate to the curated Circles we already shipped. This doc answers both, then sketches the IA, the safety model, the flows, and a phased path — and ends with the calls only Halli can make. Web research is cited inline; the full brief is in claude-state/product-research/user_created_groups_2026-06-11.md.

1. The reconciliation — two primitives, one spine

The instinct is to make "Circles" and "user-created groups" the same feature. They should not be. They answer different human questions, carry different risk, and need different governance:

Circles (built)Clubs / Groups (new)
AnswersWho you are — identity / experience cohortsWhat you're doing together — an activity or intention, with specific people
OriginCurated, fixed catalogue (like the rooms)Created by a member or by Jess
ExamplesPerimenopause · PCOS · Books & reading"Little Women, together" · "Tuesday morning walkers" · "TTC cycle-6 buddies"
Size / feelLarge, low-commitment, lurk-first belongingSmaller, higher-commitment, ongoing conversation
Safety surfaceSafe by construction (no user creation, no host power)The real new risk surface — member hosting, OSA, abandonment
LifecyclePermanentForms → active → can go dormant → can end

This mirrors how mature platforms split the two: Reddit's fixed-topic subreddits vs small created chats; Facebook's broad interest vs intentional groups. Keeping them distinct means the safe, curated Circles never inherit the user-created-moderation risk — and the new primitive can carry its own, heavier governance without dragging Circles into it.

Recommendation: two distinct user-facing primitives, one shared safety + data spine. Circles stay exactly as built. The new thing is a separate primitive. Both ride the same anonymity (author_hash), the same crisis intercept on every input, the same moderation (OpenAI + keyword + health allowlist), the same reaction/report tools, and the same CommunityPost backbone — a group-scoped post is just CommunityPost.group_id, exactly as a circle-scoped post is CommunityPost.circle today.

A naming call (real UX risk)

"Circles" (cohorts) and "Groups" (user-created) will confuse people — they sound identical. Proposed three-word taxonomy that stays warm and distinct:

Rooms — the fixed houses Jess keeps.  ·  Circles — who you are.  ·  Clubs — what you do together.

"Clubs" is warm, matches the lead use-case ("a book club, a walking club, a creativity club"), and is clearly distinct from Circles. (Open question Q1.) The rest of this doc uses Clubs for the new member/Jess-created primitive.

2. The unified information architecture

Everything — including Library, Games, and Book Club — slots into one coherent map. Library and Games are fixed Rooms (homes); Book Club becomes a Club living in the Library; the Jess games-master lives in the Games room.

Community (18+ gate · crisis intercept on every input) │ ├─ HOME — rooms-as-doors · Question of the Day · Living Wisdom · "Together this week" pool │ ├─ ROOMS (fixed · Jess-presided · never user-created → no abandonment) │ Lounge · Love · Money · Style · The Lighter Side · Health Corner │ ├─ LIBRARY (NEW fixed room) → the BookReader catalogue + Book Clubs │ │ • Jess's flagship Book Club (the Little Women pillar, reframed as a Jess-hosted Club) │ │ • a directory of member-made Book Clubs (Clubs of type "book") │ └─ GAMES (NEW fixed room) → Jess games-master + light social games (+ member game-nights, later) │ ├─ CIRCLES (fixed curated cohorts) —— WHO YOU ARE │ Life stages · Living with (consent-gated) · Shared loves │ └─ CLUBS (NEW · created by a member OR Jess · hosted) —— WHAT YOU DO TOGETHER book clubs · walking/movement clubs · creativity clubs · stage-buddy clubs · … surfaced in a Clubs directory AND inside their related Room (book clubs in Library, etc.)

A Club is the one new primitive. A book club is a Club that surfaces in the Library; a game night is a Club that surfaces in Games. "Book Club" and "Games" stop being one-off features and become instances of Rooms + Clubs — which is what makes the whole thing cohere instead of sprawling.

3. Library & Games as rooms (the low-risk half)

Library

  • The reading home: the existing BookReader catalogue (already live) becomes a Room, not a buried link.
  • Jess's flagship Book Club = today's Book Club pillar (curated pick, Jess intro, trigger warnings, position-gated spoiler-safe checkpoint threads) — reframed as a Jess-hosted Club so it's the same primitive as member book clubs.
  • A directory of member-made Book Clubs (Phase C), each = a Club of type "book": pick a book from the Library, set a cadence, others join, progress-gated discussion. Borrow Fable's spoiler-free chapter rooms + StoryGraph's page/percentage-locked comments (Book Riot; StoryGraph).

Games

  • The play home: today's Jess-as-games-master (timed round, aggregate reveal, no winners/scores) gets a proper room instead of living only in The Lighter Side.
  • Light, kind, async social games — would-you-rather, this-or-that, a daily one-line prompt — all Jess-hosted, aggregate-only, no scoreboards (locked constraint).
  • Member-run "game nights" are a later Club type — lower priority, since Jess-hosted play covers most of the need with none of the member-hosting risk.
Why this half is low-risk: Rooms are fixed and Jess-presided — no user creation, no host powers, no abandonment, no new OSA surface. Library + Games are mostly IA + reusing pieces already built (BookReader, Book Club pillar, games-master). This can move well before member-created Clubs.

4. Member-hosted Clubs — the safety model (the crux)

This is the genuinely new risk, and it is significant. The research is blunt: under the UK Online Safety Act, FemWell — not the volunteer host — is liable for everything posted inside a member-run space. The duties apply "regardless of the service's size and composition," there is no exemption for small or private spaces, and delegating hosting to a member does not transfer the duty (Bristows; Ofcom). So the model keeps central control while giving hosts limited, supervised powers.

4.1 Auto-moderation always applies — centrally, never delegated

Every post and comment inside a Club runs the same pipeline as the open rooms: crisis intercept first, then OpenAI moderation + keyword + the health-vocab allowlist. The host cannot turn this off. The host facilitates; the platform is the moderator-of-record. This is exactly the OSA-required central content-moderation system, and it's the single most important design rule. (Discord's layered model — auto-filters + human mods + central Trust & Safety — is the reference: Discord.)

4.2 Host powers are limited + supervised — not unilateral

A Club host CANA Club host CANNOT
Set the topic, intro, cadence, and promptsSee any member's identity (everyone stays author_hash-anonymous, including to the host)
Pin a message; seed a discussion promptBan or block a person platform-wide
Remove a post from their Club → routed to the central review queue (not a silent delete)Override or disable the central auto-moderation
Close the Club they createdRead DMs or export member data

Deliberately narrower than Facebook (admins can unilaterally ban/delete) or Discord (owners hold sweeping permissions) — because concentrating unvetted unilateral power in one volunteer is the documented host-abuse + single-point-abandonment risk (Northwestern; arXiv 2205.14529).

4.3 Vetting + friction to create (anti-proliferation)

Letting anyone instantly spin up groups produces proliferation + dead groups. Recommend gated at launch: request-to-host, reviewed by Halli (low volume, safest, matches the research's "manual vetting at launch"). Later, open it with guardrails: a host caps (e.g. host at most 1–2 active Clubs), a cooldown, and a one-tap host pledge (agree to the host code of conduct) — never a frictionless "create" button.

4.4 Co-hosts (≥2) for member Clubs

Solo hosting drives burnout and single-point abandonment; moderators "prefer to work in teams to split the workload" (arXiv 2205.14529). Nudge two co-hosts at creation; consider requiring a second host above a size threshold. (Open question Q3.)

4.5 Abandonment & the "dead group" problem — Jess as fallback host

Reddit formalises abandoned/unmoderated communities and reassigns them via r/redditrequest (Reddit Help). FemWell's version, with a unique advantage — Jess is always available as a caretaker host:

forming → active → (no host activity N days) DORMANT → prompt host → still dormant → offer co-host hand-off → no taker → JESS ADOPTS as caretaker host (Clubs never go un-moderated) → zero activity, long window → ARCHIVED (read-only, gracefully)

Jess-as-fallback solves the two hardest problems at once — the dead-group decay and the "no qualified human host" gap. It is the move no big platform can make because they have no trusted always-present host. (Open question Q5.)

4.6 Reporting & escalation

  • Member report inside a Club → the same central human review queue (Halli) and the same de-list / remove / "Removed by Jess" tombstone tools as the open rooms.
  • A host who misuses power (repeatedly removing benign posts, steering toward harm) → host privileges revoked; the Club is reassigned to Jess or closed.
  • Crisis content anywhere in a Club → the crisis intercept fires (UK resources), exactly as everywhere else.

4.7 Anonymity within small Clubs (the k-anonymity trap)

A real, specific danger: a small Club + FemWell's 11 life stages + free-text health detail can re-identify a member — the smaller the cohort, the smaller the anonymity set (k-anonymity). Mitigations:

  • Minimum viable size (k): a Club stays "forming" (not publicly discoverable, or posts held) until it reaches k members, so no one is the identifiable "only person in the PMDD club from Leeds." (What k? — Q6.)
  • Per-Club namespaced pseudonym: keep author_hash stable within a Club but salted per-Club, so a member can't be tracked across Clubs/surfaces — stable identity for moderation, no cross-surface linkage (pseudonymity-as-a-spectrum).
  • On-device scrub warning before posting identifying detail (reuse the Echo scrub pattern); discourage location precision.
  • Identity link held platform-side only, used solely for safety escalation (accountable-anonymity).

4.8 Privacy taxonomy

Borrow Mighty Networks' clean model (Mighty Networks): Open (discoverable, anyone joins, lurkable) · Closed (discoverable, posts hidden until joined, join auto or host-approved) · Secret (invite-only, not discoverable). Recommend Open + Closed at launch only. Secret + anonymity + health data is the highest k-anonymity + hardest-to-moderate combination — defer it. (Q4.)

5. Discovery, join & lifecycle flows

  • Clubs directory (like the Circles directory): browse by type (book · movement · creativity · stage-buddies), each card shows the intro, host, cadence — no member counts (locked constraint). "Join" or "Lurk."
  • Lurk-first everywhere — read before committing, the same gentle on-ramp Circles use.
  • Cross-surfacing: book Clubs also appear in the Library; movement Clubs near the relevant room; Jess can recommend a Club from your stage/interests.
  • Seasons: a Club can run for a season (a book club for the book; a stage-buddy club for a cycle) and sunset gracefully — avoiding infinite zombie groups.
  • Anti-silence design: engagement ≠ membership — a 2,000-member club had ~20 comments (Book Riot). So: small prompted cohorts; Jess seeds a prompt when a Club goes quiet; scheduled book-club checkpoints; progress-gated reveals. Small + nudged beats big + passive.

6. What we reuse (this is mostly assembly)

The Club primitive is not a from-scratch build — it reuses the spine already shipped:

  • Data: CommunityPost.group_id mirrors today's CommunityPost.circle; a Club entity + a ClubMembership entity mirror CircleMembership (RLS-locked). A book Club reuses BookClubPick / ClubCheckpoint / ClubNote.
  • Functions: createClub / joinClub / leaveClub mirror joinCircle / leaveCircle; posting reuses createCommunityPost + addComment (already moderated).
  • Safety: crisis intercept, OpenAI moderation, health allowlist, report→tombstone, reaction-only mode, the on-device scrub — all already built.
  • Host: Jess (the games-master / Book-Club host pattern) is the ready-made flagship + fallback host.

7. A phased path (with the legal gate in the right place)

PhaseWhatNew riskGate

BJess-hosted Clubs beyond books (a Jess walking club, a Jess creativity club). Proves the Club primitive with no member hosting.Low (no member power)—

The hard sequencing rule: member-created Clubs (Phase C+) wait behind the same OSA / ICO legal floor as the public Community launch — illegal-content risk assessment, proactive-moderation disclosure, DPIA + explicit special-category consent, the 18+ age-assurance sign-off, and a named human-review + appeals owner (Halli). User-led spaces materially increase the illegal-content + moderation surface, so they cannot precede the floor. Library + Games + Jess-hosted Clubs (Phases A–B) carry no new user-hosting risk and can move earlier.
