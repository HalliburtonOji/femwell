# §1 — PROBLEM

## 1.1 What exists today (audited from source)
**`src/pages/DoctorExport.jsx`** (read in full from `origin/main`) — titled **"Health Summary"**:
- **Fixed 90-day window** (`cutoff = today − 90d`, not user-selectable).
- Reads, all filtered by `user_id`: `UserProfile`, `DailyCheckins`, `SymptomLogs`, `MedicationLogs`, `MedicationReminders`, `JournalEntries`, `Correlations`.
- **6 hard-coded sections:** (1) Overview (cycle/period length, last period, life stage, conditions); (2) Symptoms 90d — frequency + avg severity for a **hard-coded 5-field set** (`pain, headache, cramps, bloating, breast_tenderness`) drawn from `DailyCheckins`, plus up to 3 `Correlations`; (3) Mood & Energy by month (mood/energy/sleep/stress monthly averages); (4) Medications (from logs + reminders); (5) Exercise (days/week, types); (6) Recent Journal excerpts (**last 5**, truncated to 120 chars).
- A **Jess clinical summary** at the top (`JessPatientSummary`, the Feature-4 "Wing #2" LLM card, wrapped in a quiet error boundary).
- **Output = one button, "Copy as text"** → ASCII-divider plaintext to the clipboard. **There is no PDF.** No print path.
- **No selection, no organise, no timeframe control.** It is a literal dump.
- Footer disclaimer present: _"Generated by FemWell. For personal reference only. Not medical advice."_

**The Planner** has a **second, parallel** doctor export: a `DoctorReadyDiaryCard` ("Doctor-Ready Diary", MP-A1, marked Done) producing a **6-week** pack pulling `DailyCheckins`, `SymptomLogs`, `HabitLogs`, with a planned `DoctorDiaryExports` entity that "reuses Sealed Letters E2E crypto."

**The Journal** (Phase 2) has a **"Bring this to your GP"** editorial CTA that navigates to `/DoctorExport`.

**The Health/Lifestyle** surface exposes the Jess GP-summary Wing (same `JessPatientSummary` component family).

**`/CareBridge`** is a 404→redirect onto `/DoctorExport`.

## 1.2 Why this is a problem
- **Two implementations drift.** A 90-day clipboard dump and a 6-week encrypted diary pack are different feature-sets with different data, different windows, different outputs — maintained separately, neither is the "real" one. Classic *brick-on-bread* (per `feedback_no_brick_on_bread`): duplicated UI for the same job.
- **The output is wrong for the job.** "Copy as text" gives a wall of ASCII a woman pastes into… what? It isn't a document a GP can glance at. There's no PDF — the thing people actually want to hand over or attach.
- **It's a dump, not a brief.** Everything, fixed 90 days, no editing. That is precisely the thing UK clinicians say is *unhelpful* (§2). A 10-minute appointment can't absorb six sections of un-prioritised data.
- **No agency.** The woman can't say "just the last 6 weeks of hot flushes, sleep, and mood, plus the three things I want to ask" — which is the actual use-case for a perimenopause/cycle consult.

## 1.3 Goal
**One dedicated Doctor's Export page**, reachable from the Journal, the Planner, and Health/Lifestyle, that is a **smart builder**: select a timeframe + the feelings/symptoms/write-ups that matter → organise into a clinician-readable order with a drafted "what I want to discuss" → **generate a clean PDF** (plus print + copy fallbacks). It **consolidates** the two existing implementations rather than adding a third.

## 1.4 Non-goals (v1)
- **No clinician "invited-reviewer" link / portal** (the COMMUNITY_BUILD_SPEC "Care Bridge v2" idea: 14-day expiry read-only link + async note). That is a separate Pro+ track with real cross-controller DPA obligations — explicitly out of scope here, flagged in §7/§8.
- **No EHR / NHS App / GP-system integration.** The artefact is a document the woman owns and shares herself.
- **No diagnosis, scoring-as-verdict, or treatment recommendation.** We may *present* an impact picture (Greene-style domains), never a diagnosis.
- **No paywall.** Doctor-Ready export ships **free** (consistent with the locked "Doctor-Ready Diary already ships free" decision in COMMUNITY_BUILD_SPEC and the parked-paywall rule).

# §2 — RESEARCH: what a UK clinician actually finds useful (cited)

## 2.1 The appointment is short and attention is scarce — curate, don't dump
The single most important finding. Systematic and scoping reviews of **patient-generated health data (PGHD)** in primary care report that clinicians' dominant concern is **data overload**: they are "apprehensive about having to distill the vast amount of PGHD of varying validity into clinically actionable information," and PGHD "require substantial recontextualization work before GPs ascribe them evidential value and act on them." Un-curated data is treated as *supplementary noise*, not signal. The literature's recommendation is **summarisation, filtering, and clear visualisation** before it reaches the clinician. ([JMIR systematic review 2024](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e49320); [PGHD information-quality challenges, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5701635/); [PGHD + EHR integration scoping review, JAMIA Open](https://academic.oup.com/jamiaopen/article/3/4/619/5999185))

**Design consequence:** the composer's *job* is curation. Default to **one focused concern + a short timeframe**, make "include everything" the deliberate choice, cap excerpt counts, and lead the document with a human-written "what I want to discuss" rather than raw rows.

## 2.2 What UK menopause/cycle guidance tells women to bring
Consistent across NHS-facing menopause guidance and charities, a good patient-prepared summary contains:
- **A symptom diary**: which symptoms, **how long** you've had them, and **severity rated 0–10**; physical, menstrual, *and* emotional/psychological changes; compared against the previous year.
- **A menstrual-cycle record** (calendar/diary of cycle and changes).
- **Current medications and supplements**, including herbal/OTC.
- **Relevant personal and family medical history** (e.g. cancers, heart disease, clots — directly relevant to HRT risk/benefit).
- **A written, prioritised list of questions/what you want to discuss**, ticked through during the consult — and being honest about **daily-life impact** rather than downplaying it.
- Practical: book a **double appointment** if symptoms are many.
([Specialised Physiotherapy — prepare for your GP menopause appointment](https://www.specialisedphysiotherapy.co.uk/post/menopause-how-to-prepare-for-my-gp-appointment); [The London General Practice — your menopause appointment](https://www.thelondongeneralpractice.com/your-menopause-appointment-with-your-gp/); [The Menopause Charity — how to ask your GP for help](https://themenopausecharity.org/information-and-support/how-to-ask-your-gp-for-help/); [The Lowdown — preparing for your first perimenopause appointment](https://thelowdown.com/blog/preparing-for-first-perimenopause-appointment); [Bupa menopause symptom diary PDF](https://www.bupa.co.uk/~/media/Files/mms/bins-06002.pdf))

## 2.3 A validated structure to borrow: the Greene Climacteric Scale
The **Greene Climacteric Scale** is a 21-item self-report instrument scored across **six domains — Psychological, Anxiety, Depressed mood, Vasomotor, Somatic, Sexuality** — each item rated **0 ("not at all") to 3 ("extremely")**. A score **>12** indicates likely menopausal symptoms, but it is explicitly **impact/severity-focused, not diagnostic on its own**. It is valued precisely because it "puts words and structure around changes" in a short appointment. ([Patient.info — Greene Menopause Scale](https://patient.info/womens-health/greene-menopause-scale); [Greene Climacteric Scale overview, Medium/Dr Bonza](https://drsarahbonza.medium.com/perimenopause-symptoms-an-overview-of-the-greene-climacteric-scale-and-hormone-therapy-191a2f16f054); [Measurement scales for menopause, NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK447620/))

**Design consequence:** FemWell already collects symptom + mood data. Group the symptom section by **Greene-style domains** and show **severity + frequency over the chosen window** (e.g. "Vasomotor: hot flushes logged 22/42 days, avg severity 7/10, worse in luteal"). Do **not** print a "Greene score = X, you have menopause." Present structured impact; leave the verdict to the clinician.

## 2.4 NICE NG23: for women 45+, symptoms are the diagnosis
**NICE NG23** directs clinicians **not** to use FSH/blood tests to diagnose perimenopause/menopause in women **over 45 with typical symptoms** — diagnosis is **clinical, on the symptom picture**. FSH is only considered at 40–45, or under 40 where premature menopause is suspected. Hot flushes and night sweats are the most common symptoms. ([NICE NG23 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/recommendations); [North East London ICB — menopause management in primary care](https://primarycare.northeastlondon.icb.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Menopause-management-guideline_12.2018.pdf); [Menopause Specialists — diagnosing perimenopause](https://www.menopausespecialists.com/post/diagnosing-perimenopause-symptoms-diagnosis-blood-tests))

**Design consequence:** a clean **symptom-impact-over-time** export is not a "nice to have" — for the core 45+ user it is *the clinical substrate of the diagnosis*. This is FemWell's strongest clinical-utility claim, and it must stay evidence-informed (present the pattern; never assert the diagnosis).

## 2.5 What competitors do (and the gap)
**Flo** offers a **"Health Report" you can share with your doctor** behind its subscription; **Clue** leans on data export (often to Apple Health) rather than a purpose-built clinician PDF. Neither is reported to do a **woman-curated, appointment-specific brief with a drafted question list**. General coverage also warns that raw app-data exports are often *not* useful to doctors. ([Flo — how do I export my data](https://help.flo.health/hc/en-us/articles/360054973811-How-do-I-export-my-data); [Clue — export to Apple Health](https://support.helloclue.com/hc/en-us/articles/20199556038813-Can-I-export-my-data-from-Clue-to-Apple-Health-to-Clue); [period-tracker comparison 2026](https://www.go-go-gaia.com/blog/how-to-choose-period-tracker-app.html))

**Moat:** the **curated, appointment-specific, GP-readable PDF with a prioritised "what I want to discuss"** is a differentiator. The dump-style export is the commodity; the *brief* is the product.

## 2.6 Privacy / consent floor (UK GDPR + DPA 2018)
Health data is **special-category** under UK GDPR. Lawful processing needs **both** an Article 6 basis **and** an Article 9 condition. The relevant Article 9 routes here are **explicit consent** (must be *specific*, *opt-in*, *separate*, *clearly stated*, and *withdrawable at any time*) and **"made public / shared by the data subject"** — which is what happens when *the woman herself* generates and hands a document to her clinician. Sharing special-category data between *organisations* as independent controllers triggers Data Sharing Agreements — relevant only to a future clinician-link (v2), **not** to a user-generated file. ([ICO — special category data](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/); [ICO — conditions for processing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-are-the-conditions-for-processing/); [ICO — when is consent appropriate](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/consent/when-is-consent-appropriate/))

**Design consequence (the privacy-clean architecture):** **generate the document entirely on-device; never upload it to a server.** Then FemWell is not transmitting special-category data at all — the woman is the controller of her own file. Pair that with a **point-of-export explicit-consent moment** ("you're about to compile special-category health data into a shareable document; this includes the items you selected"). This is both the right thing and the lowest-liability path.

## 2.7 Top takeaways → direct build rules
1. **Curate over dump** — short window + focused selection is the default; "everything" is opt-in. (§2.1)
2. **Severity 0–10 + duration + frequency** are the units clinicians and guidance ask for. (§2.2)
3. **Group symptoms by Greene-style domains; show impact over time; never print a diagnosis.** (§2.3, §2.4)
4. **Lead with the human brief** — "what I want to discuss" + prioritised questions — then the data. (§2.1, §2.2)
5. **One page where possible; cap excerpts; visual not verbose.** (§2.1)
6. **On-device generation + explicit point-of-export consent.** (§2.6)
7. **For 45+, the symptom-over-time picture *is* the clinical case** — make it the spine of the document. (§2.4)

## 3.1 Canonical route & consolidation map
- **Keep `/DoctorExport` as the single page.** It is already the `/CareBridge` redirect target and the Journal/Lifestyle link target — least churn, no dead links.
- **Rename the page title to "Doctor's Export"** (the user-facing name Halli uses) with sub-line "A summary you build, for your GP." (The old "Health Summary" h1 becomes the export's *document* title.)
- **Retire the two parallel paths into entry points:**

| Surface today | Today | After consolidation |
|---|---|---|
| `/DoctorExport` "Health Summary" (90d copy-text) | the dump | **becomes the composer** (§4) |
| Planner `DoctorReadyDiaryCard` (6-week pack) | parallel generator | **becomes a deep-link card** → `/DoctorExport?preset=diary&weeks=6` (no generation logic of its own) |

- **Presets** are just URL params that pre-configure step 1 (range + which categories are ticked). They never skip the consent or the preview. Examples:

  - `?preset=full` → 90-day window, all categories ticked (mirrors the old dump, for anyone who wants it).
  - `?preset=journal` → 8-week window, reflections ticked + the entry the user came from pinned.
  - no param → the **smart default** (§4.2).

Designed to the research: human brief first, curated data second, one-page-where-possible, evidence-informed.

## 3.4 States
- **Loading** — spinner while entities load (existing pattern).
- **Empty / not-enough-data** — if the chosen window has little/no data: a warm, non-shaming panel ("There's not much logged in the last 6 weeks yet. A clearer picture comes from a couple of weeks of check-ins — here's what's most useful to track before your appointment: symptoms + severity, sleep, mood.") with a shortcut to the relevant logging surfaces. Never block; offer a wider window.
- **Nothing selected** — if every category is toggled off, **Generate is disabled** with an inline hint ("Pick at least one thing to include").
- **Generating** — brief progress state while the PDF builds on-device (pdfmake is fast; show a subtle indicator for large windows).
- **Generated** — preview rendered; Download/Print/Copy/Save enabled; a quiet "regenerate" if they change a selection.
- **Error** — if an entity read fails (each is already `.catch(()=>[])`, so partial data degrades gracefully) or PDF build throws: a quiet boundary, keep the rest usable, offer Copy-as-text fallback.

# §4 — THE COMPOSER (the smart builder)

## 4.1 The model
Three lightweight client-side objects drive everything (no schema needed to *build* an export):

```
ExportSelection = {
  range: { mode: '6w'|'3m'|'6m'|'custom', from, to },   // resolved to dates
  focus: ['vasomotor', ...],                            // 0..n focus chips
  categories: {                                         // include + which fields
    cycle: bool,
    symptoms: { on: bool, fields: [..] },               // subset of available symptoms
    mood: bool, meds: bool, exercise: bool,
    patterns: bool, reflections: { on: bool, pinned: [entryIds] }
  }
}
ExportOrganise = {
  discuss: string,                  // Jess-drafted, editable
  questions: [string],              // ordered, ≤5
  sectionOrder: [sectionKey],       // drag order; off = excluded
  sectionTitles: { key: label },    // rename
  flaggedRows: [rowId],             // concerning rows to mark
  domainLens: bool,                 // Greene-style grouping
  family_history: string|null       // optional free text (§3.3)
}
ExportDoc = derived → { sections:[...], meta:{...} }   // pure function of the two above + loaded data
```

## 4.2 Smart defaults (what an un-touched composer proposes)
- **Range:** Last **6 weeks** (matches the old Planner diary; long enough to show a pattern, short enough to stay curated per §2.1).
- **Categories:** cycle ✓, symptoms ✓ (all logged), mood/energy/sleep ✓, medications ✓, exercise ✓, patterns ✓, **reflections OFF** (privacy-forward; journal content is opt-in).
- **Focus:** none preselected; if the user came from a preset or has a dominant recent symptom, pre-tick it.
- **Discuss/questions:** Jess drafts a starter from the focus + the standout data ("Hot flushes most days for ~6 weeks, worse before my period, affecting sleep — I'd like to talk about options"). Always editable, never auto-final.

## 4.3 Symptom selection detail
Two symptom sources today: the **fixed 5 `DailyCheckins` severity fields** (`pain, headache, cramps, bloating, breast_tenderness`) and the **`SymptomLogs`** entity (free-er logged symptoms). The composer should:
- Enumerate symptoms **actually present in the window** (don't show empty options), with a count + avg severity per symptom.
- Let the user tick the subset that matters for this visit (default: all present).
- Map known symptoms to **Greene domains** for the optional domain lens (vasomotor, psychological, anxiety, depressed-mood, somatic, sexuality); anything unmapped falls under "Other reported."

## 4.4 Jess's role (bounded, on-device-respecting)
- Jess **drafts** the "what I want to discuss" and may **suggest** question phrasings and a sensible section order — reusing the existing `JessPatientSummary`/Wing service.
- Jess output is **always editable** and clearly labelled as a draft. It must **never** introduce a diagnosis, a severity verdict, or a treatment recommendation (guardrail in the prompt; Ms Verify checks the rendered output for clinical-promise language).
- If the LLM call fails, the composer still works fully with templated, phase-aware defaults (existing error-boundary pattern).

# §5 — DATA WIRING + ENTITIES

## 5.1 Reuse — generation needs NO new entity
All data already exists and is already read by the current page:
`UserProfile`, `DailyCheckins`, `SymptomLogs`, `MedicationLogs`, `MedicationReminders`, `JournalEntries`, `Correlations`. The composer simply makes the **window + field selection** dynamic instead of hard-coded 90 days / 5 fields. Each read keeps its `.catch(()=>[])` so partial data degrades gracefully.

## 5.2 The one decision: persist a saved config? (recommended, minimal)
Women prepare across several days before an appointment and return to regenerate. To support "Save this setup" / reopen, persist the **selection + organise** objects (small JSON; the questions/discuss text is mildly sensitive narrative).

Two viable routes — **pick one in §8**:
- **(A) `localStorage` only (v1, zero backend).** Save the last config under `fw_doctor_export_v1`. No entity, no migration, no server. Simplest; lost if storage is cleared; single-device. **Recommended for v1.**
- **(B) A `DoctorExportConfig` entity (repurpose the planned `DoctorDiaryExports`).** Fields: `user_id`, `selection_json`, `organise_json`, `label`, `last_generated_at`. If it ever stores the drafted narrative, **reuse the Sealed-Letters on-device AES-GCM tier** (`journalCrypto.js`) so the narrative is ciphertext at rest — exactly as the Planner plan already anticipated ("reuses Sealed Letters E2E crypto"). Requires the base44 **manual entity-create** step (per `feedback_base44_schema_via_ai_builder` — schema viewer is read-only; create via Data tab / AI builder once).

> **Do NOT persist the generated PDF on a server.** The document is built on-device and handed to the user. Storing rendered special-category documents server-side would re-introduce the controller/consent burden we're avoiding (§2.6).

## 5.3 Tiny optional additions (not blockers)
- `family_history` free text at organise-time → can live in the config JSON (no entity field needed) or, later, a `UserProfile.family_history` string. Flagged, not required for v1.
- Symptom→Greene-domain map is a **static client constant** (like `echoConfig.js`), not data.

# §6 — PRIVACY / CONSENT UX

1. **On-device generation, no upload.** The PDF/text is assembled in the browser and downloaded/printed locally. No export artefact is sent to a FemWell server. (This is the architecture, not just copy.)
2. **Explicit, specific consent at the export moment** (Step 3, before generate) — a clear opt-in, separate from any account-level consent, naming what's included:
   > "You're about to compile your selected health information into a document to share with a clinician. This can include sensitive details — symptoms, mood, cycle, medications{, and journal reflections if you included them}. The document is created on your device and is yours to share. Continue?"
   - Reflects ICO "explicit consent": specific, opt-in, separate, withdrawable. Withdrawal here = simply not generating / deleting the local file; no server state to revoke.
3. **Reflections are opt-in and visibly so** — journal content is OFF by default; turning it on shows a one-line reminder that personal writing will appear in the document.
4. **Minimisation by design** — name/DOB optional; default to initials; the curation model means less data leaves than the old dump.

# §9 — APPENDIX: PDF generation, methods compared (client-side, Base44/React)

All options run **in the browser / on-device**, which is the privacy requirement (§2.6). Ranked for *this* job (a structured, multi-page, text-heavy clinical document that must be legible, small, and accessible).

| Method | What it is | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **pdfmake** | Declarative doc-definition object → vector PDF | **Real selectable text**, tables, automatic **page breaks**, headers/footers, small files, no server, good for structured reports; pure JS | You describe layout in JS (not HTML/CSS); custom fonts need vfs embedding (or use built-in Roboto/standard fonts) | **RECOMMENDED** — best fit for a paginated clinical brief |
| **@react-pdf/renderer** | Author the PDF as React components | Real text, React mental model, flexbox-ish layout, on-device | Larger dep; its own layout engine to learn; font embedding setup | **Strong alternative** if you prefer authoring the doc in JSX |
| **window.print() + print CSS** | Browser's native "Save as PDF" | **Zero dependency**, perfect fonts/text, trivial | User must pick "Save as PDF" in the dialog; pagination/margins vary by browser; inconsistent on mobile/Capacitor | **Keep as the fallback / "Print" action** |
| **jsPDF (text API)** | Programmatic text drawing | Real text, light | You hand-place every line/coordinate; tables/wrapping are manual; painful for variable-length content | Not for this (too low-level) |
| **html2canvas + jsPDF** | Screenshot the DOM into an image PDF | "Looks like the screen" | **Image, not text** → not selectable, not accessible, **large files**, blurry on print, font/emoji rendering bugs, heavy bundle | **Avoid** for a clinical document |

**Recommendation:** **pdfmake** for `Download PDF` (true-text A4, paginated, light, fully on-device). Use the app's **standard serif/sans for the cover/headings** if font-embedding is cheap, otherwise pdfmake's built-in fonts are perfectly legible for a clinical doc (legibility > brand on the printed page). Provide **`window.print()` as "Print"** and keep **"Copy as text"** (the current `buildPlainText()` is already good — reuse it for the focused selection). Note Base44 bundle hygiene: import pdfmake's fonts lazily / code-split the generate step so it doesn't bloat first load.
