1. Where Nutrition is today (honest inventory)

Seven tabs: Today · My Plan · Recipes · AI Plan · Shop · Progress · Insights. Cycle-awareness lives in tips, not in adaptive targets, and the joy / culture / intuitive-eating dimension isn't a first-class part of the UI.

Logging-friction & harm evidence (a hard ethical constraint, not a nicety)

  • >70% find logging burdensome (PMC5537862).
  • Guilt / shame drives discontinuation (Lindgreen 2021, Brain & Behavior).
  • A 2026 systematic review (Wallace et al., SAGE J Health Psychology): tracking may increase disordered-eating risk — ~half reported increased self-consciousness/guilt after a month; ~40% of male MFP users perceived it as contributing to ED symptoms.
What works: recents/favourites + photo/voice/natural-language + "good enough" editable estimates. What to AVOID: daily pass/fail and calorie scoreboards. This is an ethical design line for FemWell, not a preference.

CONTESTED — flag clearly: cycle-syncing macro prescriptions have weak dietitian consensus — position as self-awareness only (the one solid angle is iron during menstruation). ZOE-style "truly personalized" claims overreach the evidence (Which?). Intuitive Eating IS evidence-based (Tribole & Resch; Cedars-Sinai; PubMed 28619668) — it directly validates the no-scoreboard stance.

WHOLE-LIFE / joy evidence (under-used by every competitor)

  • Cooking confidence ↔ lower depression / higher wellbeing (Frontiers).
  • Cooking brings joy to 58% across 142 countries; cooking together +232% joy; eating with someone cuts rejection feelings 23.5% (Gallup).
  • Budget/skills programs (Cooking Matters, Jamie's Ministry of Food) raise veg intake + cut food insecurity.
This is the dimension FemWell can own that no calorie tracker and no period app touches.

3. Deep brainstorm — themed feature superset

(a) Frictionless logging — recents/favourites strip; "repeat yesterday" / one-tap re-log; photo AI log; voice / natural-language entry; quick-add; barcode via Open Food Facts (free API). The friction-reducers the evidence backs, made FemWell-warm.
(b) A real food database — replace the 28-item FOOD_DB. Trade-off: Open Food Facts (free, barcode-rich, crowdsourced/variable, UK-strong) and/or USDA FDC (free, lab-verified, generic foods, US-centric, no barcodes) as a deterministic floor, with the LLM as the fallback / parser for free-text — not the only source. Cache hot lookups.
(c) Whole-life & joy — a "how did it feel" non-macro log (satisfied? rushed? with people?); cooking-confidence prompts; cultural / world-cuisine celebration; a budget mode (cost-aware swaps, batch-cook); cook-together / social; ties into the Community food-room. The differentiator.
(d) Evidence-based women's nutrition done right — iron / protein / fibre / calcium as gentle micronutrient nudges, never scoreboards; menopause / perimenopause / pregnancy / postpartum modes; the honest cycle-aware framing (iron-during-menstruation is the solid one; everything else is self-awareness, clearly labelled).
(e) Intuitive / non-diet framing — replace calorie pass/fail with qualitative reflection; hunger / fullness check-ins; anti-diet-culture copy throughout. Aligns the whole module with the Intuitive Eating evidence and the harm literature.
(f) Smarter insights — fix the legacy-shape calorie bug first; surface mood-food / energy-food patterns from DailyCheckins; a gentle weekly story in Jess's voice, not a report card.
(g) Planning & shopping — unify the two planners (manual + AI into one model); smarter shopping (auto-build from the plan, de-dupe); a pantry / "what's in the cupboard" layer.
(h) Jess as nutrition companion — ask-Jess about food; gentle, opt-in nudges; "what could I make with this" meal ideas. Voice continuity, not a separate chatbot.
(i) Hydration / drinks polish — keep what works; lighten the UI; fold into the daily snapshot rather than a separate chore.

6. Headline recommendation

Be the whole-life, non-scoreboard, women-aware nutrition layer that the calorie apps and the period apps both miss — Flo/Clue have the women + community and no nutrition; MyFitnessPal/ZOE have the tracking and the diet-culture baggage (and, in ZOE's case, over-claimed personalization). Lead with frictionless logging (recents/favourites + photo/voice), a real food DB (Open Food Facts under the LLM), intuitive/whole-life framing (qualitative reflection, joy, budget, cultural cooking), and evidence-based — not faddy — women's nutrition (iron / fibre / protein / calcium as gentle nudges per life stage). Treat cycle-syncing macro prescriptions and microbiome "personalization" as flagged, optional extras — never the spine — because the evidence doesn't support selling them as truth. Do first: fix the legacy-shape calorie bug, derive real targets, ship recents/favourites, and put a real food database under the whole thing.
