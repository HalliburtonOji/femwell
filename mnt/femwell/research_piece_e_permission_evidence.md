# Research — Piece E permission/small-joy cards: evidence vs kindness — 17/07/2026

## Question
Piece E cards open into slip → "why this is good for you" line → one doable thing. Each of 8 real in-app prompts must map to EITHER a genuine checkable citation (author + finding + URL) OR an honest "no strong evidence — frame as kindness" verdict. No invented studies; anything unverifiable today is marked so. Prefer primary sources / reputable reviews.

## Per-item verdicts

### 1 — Slow Sunday / rest / "rest is part of work, not the reward"
- **Finding (plain):** Employees who mentally switch off from work during off-hours ("psychological detachment") report higher life satisfaction, better mood and fewer strain symptoms, and detachment predicts next-day energy.
- **Cite:** Sonnentag & Fritz, J Organizational Behavior 2015 (stressor-detachment model); meta-analysis Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah, Frontiers in Psychology 2017 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5233687/
- **Confidence:** SOLID (recovery/detachment is a large, replicated literature).
- **Caveat:** The catchphrase "rest isn't the reward, it's part of it" is a values statement, not a study result. What is evidenced is that *deliberate detachment/recovery* aids wellbeing — cite the mechanism, keep the slogan as voice.

### 2 — Notice one beautiful thing / awe
- **Finding:** Older adults prompted to take weekly 15-min "awe walks" for 8 weeks reported more compassion, gratitude and joy, and less daily distress, than a control walking group.
- **Cite:** Sturm et al., *Emotion* 2020 — https://www.ucsf.edu/news/2020/09/418551/awe-walks-boost-emotional-well-being (developed with Keltner).
- **Confidence:** SOLID for the direction; MIXED on generalisability.
- **Caveat:** Small sample (n=60, ages 60–90). "Notice one beautiful thing" is a light everyday-awe nudge, not the full walk protocol — legitimate as inspired-by, don't over-claim the effect size for a single glance.

### 3 — Ten minutes outside, no phone / time in nature
- **Finding:** People spending ≥120 minutes/week in nature were significantly more likely to report good health and high wellbeing than those with none; benefit held however the time was split.
- **Cite:** White et al., *Scientific Reports* 2019;9:7730 — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-44097-3
- **Confidence:** SOLID (n≈20,000, England).
- **Caveat:** Cross-sectional/correlational and self-reported — can't prove causation, and the benefit was a *weekly 120-min* threshold; a single 10-minute go doesn't "buy" the outcome. Frame as "counts toward your week," not "10 min = the study result."

### 4 — Text the friend you've been meaning to / connection
- **Finding (a):** Stronger social relationships are associated with ~50% higher odds of survival — an effect comparable to well-established mortality risk factors. **Finding (b):** People who reach out consistently underestimate how much the recipient appreciates it; surprise contact is valued more than initiators expect.
- **Cite:** Holt-Lunstad et al., *PLoS Medicine* 2010;7:e1000316 — https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316 ; Liu, Rim, Min & Min, *J Personality & Social Psychology* 2022 — https://www.psypost.org/2022/08/study-identifies-a-robust-tendency-to-underestimate-how-much-others-appreciate-being-reached-out-to-63627
- **Confidence:** SOLID (both) — strongest evidence base of the eight.
- **Caveat:** Holt-Lunstad is about sustained relationships/mortality, not a one-off text; use it for "connection matters," and use Liu for the specific "reach out — it's valued more than you think" line.

### 5 — A long walk
- **Finding:** Single bouts of low-to-moderate walking reliably lift positive affect and reduce negative affect; a systematic review + meta-analysis links walking to reduced depressive and anxiety symptoms.
- **Cite:** JMIR Public Health & Surveillance 2024;10:e48355 — https://publichealth.jmir.org/2024/1/e48355 ; short-walk affect studies (Ekkekakis/Colenberg) — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1005558025163
- **Confidence:** SOLID for mood; MIXED on "walking specifically vs. general exercise/being outdoors."
- **Caveat:** Effects overlap with general physical activity and nature exposure — the *walk* isn't proven to be the special ingredient. Honest framing: "movement lifts mood, and a walk is the easy dose."

### 6 — Make a proper cup of tea, fully / savouring & single-tasking
- **Finding:** Savouring — deliberately attending to and prolonging a pleasant experience — is a measurable ability associated with higher positive emotion and subjective wellbeing.
- **Cite:** Bryant & Veroff, *Savoring: A New Model of Positive Experience* (2007) — https://www.authentichappiness.sas.upenn.edu/resources/savoring-new-model-positive-experience-fred-b-bryant-and-joseph-veroff
- **Confidence:** MIXED — the *savouring construct* is solid; the *tea ritual / "romance the mundane"* specific is an illustration, not a studied intervention.
- **Caveat:** Do NOT dress the tea ritual as proven. The evidenced part is "giving full attention to a small pleasant moment boosts it." "Single-tasking beats multitasking" is a separate, weaker claim — leave it out or mark as taste.

### 7 — A gentler pace in the luteal/menstrual phase
- **Finding:** There is NO good evidence that pacing life/training to cycle phase improves performance or wellbeing; cycle-based periodisation is explicitly described as evidence-lacking, and cycle phase has trivial/no effect on strength adaptation. Menstrual *symptoms* (pain, fatigue, low mood) are, however, real and can genuinely impair how you feel.
- **Cite:** "No influence of cycle phase on strength adaptations," Frontiers Sports & Active Living 2023 — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2023.1054542/full ; McNulty et al. systematic review, Sports Medicine 2020 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7497427/ ; IMPACT trial protocol ("evidence is lacking whether periodized exercise based on the menstrual cycle is beneficial"), Trials 2024 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10823667/
- **Confidence:** THIN → FRAME AS KINDNESS for the *prescriptive* "slow down because your phase says so."
- **Caveat (important, get this right):** Split it. Do NOT claim science tells you to slow in the luteal phase — it doesn't. DO honour that if you *feel* rough (cramps, fatigue, low mood are real and documented), being gentle with yourself is reasonable. Kindness framing: "If your body's asking for a slower gear today, you're allowed one" — not "science says pace to your phase."

### 8 — Gallery alone / a film and early night / write one line you're proud of
- **Finding:** No direct study backs these specific prompts as wellbeing interventions.
- **Cite:** None. (Adjacent evidence exists — sleep has a vast health literature, arts-engagement and self-compassion have some — but none maps to these exact prompts, so do not borrow it as proof.)
- **Confidence:** NONE → FRAME AS KINDNESS.
- **Caveat:** Keep these purely as warm permission ("you're allowed a slow evening"). The only place you *could* lean on evidence is the "early night" = sleep, but don't cite unless we add a real sleep source; safer to leave as kindness.
