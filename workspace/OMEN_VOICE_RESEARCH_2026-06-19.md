## 1. AUGURY / OMEN — GARDEN-CREATURE + FLORA FOLKLORE (gentle, hopeful)

> The point is a **library of "they say…" omens** we can attach to a rotating creature/plant. Every entry below is gentle/hopeful by design; the darker variants (out-of-season blooms as ill omen; moths as death) are flagged so the engine can avoid them.

### Garden creatures

**Robin at the window / in the garden.** British and Irish folklore: a robin appearing — especially in an unusual place at an unusual time — is widely read as a **passed loved one near / watching over you**, and more broadly as **good news / hope / new beginnings**, "a messenger to remind people they are not alone" (source: [Centre of Excellence — Robin spiritual meaning](https://www.centreofexcellence.com/robin-spiritual-meaning/); [Bell & Walsh — Robin symbolism](https://bellandwalsh.co.uk/robin-symbolism/); [Pure Cremation — Robins Appear](https://www.purecremation.co.uk/robins-appear), all fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A robin in the garden. They say it means someone's thinking of you — and good news is on its way."

**Ladybird landing on you.** In Sussex lore, a ladybird that lands on you brings **"as many happy months as the spots on its back"**; counting spots is a counting-of-fortune ritual; Victorians read a landing as new gloves/a new hat coming; it is "very unlucky to kill one" (source: [Spooky Isles — Ladybird superstitions](https://www.spookyisles.com/ladybirds-folklore/); [Icy Sedgwick — Ladybird folklore](https://www.icysedgwick.com/ladybird-folklore/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A ladybird landed today. Count the spots — that's how many good months they say are coming."

**Butterfly (esp. white) landing on you.** Universally **transformation, rebirth, the soul**; a butterfly landing on you "good fortune will befall you"; a **white** butterfly specifically reads as **important news coming** (a birth, engagement, marriage) and "you are on the right path." Celtic lore: white butterflies carry the souls of children / act as spirit guides (source: [Centre of Excellence — Butterflies](https://www.centreofexcellence.com/spiritual-meaning-of-butterflies/); [Butterfly Insight — white butterfly](https://www.butterflyinsight.com/white-butterfly-color-meaning-and-myths.html), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A white butterfly. The old story is it's news on the wing — and a small sign you're on the right path."

**Bee at the window / in the house.** A bee flying in **means a visitor is coming**; "a single bee entering is a sign of good luck, usually money." The English custom of **"telling the bees"** treats bees as family — you tell them births, weddings, deaths, news — or they'll take offence and leave (source: [Icy Sedgwick — Bees folklore](https://www.icysedgwick.com/bees-folklore/); [Bee Good — Telling the Bees](https://beegood.co.uk/blog/the-tradition-of-telling-the-bees/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell hook:* the "telling the bees" custom is a ready-made **ritual mechanic** — FemWell can invite a user to "tell the garden" a piece of news (see §5).

**Dragonfly.** **Change, adaptability, insight** — exceptional vision makes it a symbol of "seeing beyond illusion," embracing growth and letting go of old patterns. Generally a good omen of change + joy (source: [Icy Sedgwick — Dragonfly folklore](https://www.icysedgwick.com/dragonfly-folklore/); [Spavelous — Dragonfly symbolism](https://spavelous.com/the-spiritual-symbolism-of-dragonflies/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A dragonfly came by. They're said to mean a change you can see clearly now — trust it."

**Moth.** **Quiet, hidden transformation** (the night-version of the butterfly's visible change); a seeker of inner light / truth; "a moth visiting urges you to trust your inner guidance — change is coming." Note: some traditions read moths as souls of the dead — **keep FemWell on the gentle/transformation reading, avoid the death framing** (source: [Centre of Excellence — Moths](https://www.centreofexcellence.com/the-spiritual-meaning-of--moths/); [Memento Morph — Moths, the night's messengers](https://mementomorph.com/blogs/news/moths-symbolism-the-night-s-messengers), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A moth at the lamp. Some changes happen quietly, in the dark — yours is one of them."

**Firefly.** **Hope, inspiration, resilience** — "one tiny spark can show you the way" in complete darkness; European folklore reads a firefly as good luck (source: [Symbol Sage — Firefly folklore](https://symbolsage.com/firefly-symbolism-myths-rituals/); [Spiritual Wayfarer — Firefly symbolism](https://spiritualwayfarer.com/firefly-symbolism/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A firefly. The smallest light still counts as light."

**Spider + dew web in the morning.** The spider is the cross-cultural **weaver of fate** (Greek Arachne, West African Anansi, Navajo/Hopi Spider Woman, Egyptian Neith); **dew on the morning web means a beautiful day ahead**; keeping web in your purse is said to attract wealth; killing a spider is unlucky (source: [Symbol Sage — Are spiders good luck](https://symbolsage.com/spiders-good-luck/); [Chi-Nese — Spider superstitions](https://chi-nese.com/spider-superstitions-hidden-symbolism/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A dew-bright web this morning. They say it promises a fine day — and that you're quietly weaving something good."

**Snail.** **Patience, perseverance, steady progress** — "success can come from making small, steady progress"; European lore prizes its methodical pace; outsmarts faster animals in African folktales (source: [Centre of Excellence — Snails](https://www.centreofexcellence.com/the-spiritual-meaning-of-snails/); [Spiritual Wayfarer — Snail symbolism](https://spiritualwayfarer.com/snail-symbolism/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A snail. Slow is still forward. Today doesn't have to be fast."

**Cricket on the hearth.** **Good luck + a happy home**; chirping "wards off negative energy"; "a cricket on the hearth has always been a symbol of good luck"; kept as lucky pets in China (source: [LoveToKnow — Lucky cricket](https://www.lovetoknow.com/home/design-decor/lucky-cricket-meaning-symbolism); [Good Luck Symbols — Crickets](https://goodlucksymbols.com/crickets/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A cricket's singing. The old saying is it means a happy house — and luck that's settled in to stay."

**Grasshopper.** **A leap forward** — "grasshopper medicine is about leaping forward, never backward"; when life feels slow, a grasshopper is read as an omen of "a significant leap forward" + good fortune (source: [UniGuide — Grasshopper meaning](https://www.uniguide.com/grasshopper-meaning-symbolism); [YourTango — Grasshopper symbolism](https://www.yourtango.com/self/grasshopper-symbolism-spiritual-meanings-grasshopper-spirit-animal), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A grasshopper. They only jump forwards. Maybe that's the nudge."

**Frog.** **Rain, renewal, fresh water, good luck**; Native American + Navajo lore: frogs call down rain to nourish the earth; protectors of water + fertility (source: [Centre of Excellence — Frogs](https://www.centreofexcellence.com/the-spiritual-meaning-of-frogs/); [UniGuide — Frog meaning](https://www.uniguide.com/frog-meaning-symbolism-spirit-animal-guide), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A frog in the green. They say it calls the rain that makes everything grow again."

**Hummingbird.** **Joy, resilience, emotional renewal, hope, presence** — Hopi/Aztec lore: carries prayers to the heavens + brings blessings; South America: brings luck + positive energy (source: [Birdfy — Hummingbird symbolism](https://www.birdfy.com/blogs/blogs/hummingbirds-spiritual-meaning-a-simple-guide-to-their-special-symbolism); [Wild Gratitude — Hummingbird](https://www.wildgratitude.com/hummingbird-symbolism/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A hummingbird. Pure joy with wings — and a little luck for the day."

**Wren + sparrow.** The **wren** is the "king of birds" (won by cunning — it hid in the eagle's feathers to fly highest); Celtic druids read wren flight-patterns as **messages from the otherworld**; symbol of cleverness + adaptation. The **sparrow** = joyfulness, inclusion, resourcefulness; Celtic: honesty, hard work, simplicity (source: [Uncanny Lounge — Wren, king of birds](https://uncannylounge.com/2024/06/18/the-wren-in-folklore-and-mythology/); [RTÉ — How the wren became king](https://www.rte.ie/brainstorm/2025/1221/1185007-wren-st-stephens-day-ireland-folklore-traditions-bad-luck/); [PetHelpful — Bird symbolism](https://pethelpful.com/birds/bird-symbolism-spiritual-meaning), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line (wren):* "A wren — small, but they say it once out-flew the eagle. Don't let your size fool anyone."

### Flora omens

**The first snowdrop.** Canonical **hope + consolation + new beginnings** — assigned "hope" in Kate Greenaway's *Language of Flowers* (1884) and "consolation and hope" in the Victorian language of flowers; rooted in its biology (it flowers through January/February snow) and the Eve-and-the-angel legend (the snowdrop was the original consolation flower) (source: [Hello Fearless — Snowdrop meaning](https://hellofearless.com/blogs/blog/snowdrop-flower-meaning/); [Petal Republic — Snowdrop](https://www.petalrepublic.com/snowdrop-flower-meaning/); [Almanac — Language of flowers](https://www.almanac.com/flower-meanings-language-flowers), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "The first snowdrop. Proof that life keeps going even through the cold — they've meant hope for centuries."

**A flower blooming out of season / a second, unexpected bloom.** Two readings exist — pick the hopeful one. Appalachian/Southern + old European lore reads out-of-season blooms as an **ill omen** (a break in the natural order). But hydrangea + Japanese quince blooming out of season **"symbolize survival and hope for better times,"** and Japanese lore reads an out-of-season morning glory as "a visitor from beyond." The first bloom of spring (crocus, daffodil) = renewal + "good things to come" (source: [Wilson Times — Flowers that bloom out of season = survival, hope](https://www.wilsontimes.com/news/flowers-that-bloom-out-of-season-symbolize-survival-hope-177067); [Hillbilly Slang — Flowers out of season](https://www.hillbillyslang.com/folklore/flowers-which-bloom-out-of-season/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- *FemWell omen line:* "A second bloom, out of season. They say it means survival — that something in you wasn't done yet."
- **Engine rule:** FemWell uses the *survival/hope* reading only; never the death/sickness variant.

**Dew on petals.** Folds into the spider-web-dew reading above: morning dew = "a beautiful day ahead." A gentle, low-stakes daily-open omen.
- *FemWell omen line:* "Dew on the petals this morning. The old gardeners reckoned that means a fine day."

## 2. THE ALMANAC / FOLK-WISDOM VOICE — how to frame omens as lore, not promises

**The almanac voice = wry, knowing, folksy-but-smart, and openly admits it's lore.** The Old Farmer's Almanac says outright it is a **"big fan of weather folklore, proverbs, and sayings"** and notes "much of the traditional weather lore is surprisingly accurate" — i.e. it leans in with affection while keeping a wink (source: [Old Farmer's Almanac — Weather proverbs](https://www.almanac.com/content/weather-proverbs-and-prognostics-rain-and-clouds), fetched 2026-06-19).

**The canonical form is a couplet + a gentle reason.** "Red sky at night, sailor's delight; red sky at morning, sailor take warning" — then the Almanac *explains* the kernel of truth (red at sunset = sun lighting clouds already moved east; red at sunrise = clouds moving in from the west) (source: [Old Farmer's Almanac — Red sky sayings](https://www.almanac.com/weather-sayings-and-their-meanings), fetched 2026-06-19). **The move FemWell should copy:** state the lore, then add a small grounded "why people believed it" — that's what keeps it charming instead of woo.

**Framing devices that license belief without claiming truth.** The tradition attributes forecasts to the people who needed them — "farmers and sailors, people whose livelihoods depended on the weather, relied on lore" — and presents it as inherited observation, not fact (source: [Old Farmer's Almanac — Red sky](https://www.almanac.com/weather-sayings-and-their-meanings), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell uses this:** every omen is voiced as **reported belief**, never instruction or prediction. Approved openers: *"They say…"*, *"The old gardeners reckoned…"*, *"There's an old story that…"*, *"Folk wisdom has it…"*, *"In Sussex they believed…"*. Forbidden: *"This means you will…"*, *"The universe is telling you…"* (that's the hollow-mystic register FemWell must avoid).
- **Structure to steal:** `[omen, plainly stated] → [the "they say" meaning] → [optional one-line grounded why] → [a small, do-able nudge]`. Example: *"A robin in the garden. They say it means good news on the way — robins have always been winter's company, so people read them as hope when little else was about. Worth texting the person you've been meaning to."*

## 3. SOULFUL + FUNNY BRAND VOICE EXEMPLARS — what to steal vs avoid

| **Co–Star** | Cosmic-deadpan, aphoristic, second person | **Brevity + specificity + a flat, oracular delivery.** Lines like *"Nobody cares how many books you've read,"* *"Non-conformity is also a performance,"* *"Try being a plant for a day"* | **Borrow** the short oracular cadence + specificity. **Avoid** the brutal/scolding edge — it's screenshot-bait but reads as a put-down. FemWell is warm, not cutting. |
| **Sanctuary** | Warm, approachable, "astrology for real life" | **Accessibility over erudition** — friendly, colourful, not an intellectual exercise; popular with newcomers precisely because it's not gatekept | **Borrow** the un-gatekept warmth — omens explained like a friend would, never a guru. |
| **Innocent Drinks** | Friendly, playful, real; "witty friend" | **Self-aware micro-jokes in unexpected places** + treating the customer like a mate. *"Stop looking at my bottom"* (bottle base), *"made by crushed fruit and good intentions,"* *"promise we won't put anything weird in here"* | **Borrow** heavily: warmth + small jokes in small places (empty states, card footers). **This is FemWell's closest UK cousin.** |
| **Oatly** | Dry, irreverent, stream-of-consciousness, transparent | **Anti-marketing honesty** — *"Wow, no cow!"*, product tab labelled *"More than you would ever want to know about our products."* Built voice-first (words before design) | **Borrow** the dry honesty + plain-spoken transparency. **Avoid** the loud/contrarian edge — too brash for a nurturing women's app. |
| **Headspace / Calm** | Nurturing-calm, unhurried, second person | Soft pacing, permission-giving ("it's okay to…"), low-stakes | **Borrow** the permission-giving, never-guilt cadence for low/empty states. **Avoid** going so soft it turns saccharine + characterless. |
| **Mailchimp** | The canonical *method* | **Voice is constant; tone flexes to the reader's state of mind.** Their voice = plainspoken, genuine, "translators," **dry humour — "we prefer winking to shouting"** | **Borrow the whole method** (§3 detail below). This is the operating system for FemWell's voice. |
| **Duolingo** | Chaotic-but-self-aware, "mildly menacing" | Humour + persistence; but **its own guidelines say Duo is NOT "negative, threatening, angry, aggressive, belligerent, or mean"** | **Avoid** the guilt-trip/passive-aggressive notifications — users report guilt marketing "turns them off the product entirely." FemWell must never guilt. |

**The Mailchimp method, in full (the canon to adopt):** "You have the same voice all the time, but your tone changes." Voice = **plainspoken** ("value clarity above all"), **genuine** ("familiar, warm, and accessible"), **translators** (demystify, "educate without patronizing"), **dry humour** ("straight-faced, subtle… we prefer winking to shouting"). Rules: active voice, plain English over jargon, positive framing over negative, and **"don't go out of your way to make a joke — it's always more important to be clear than entertaining"** (source: [Mailchimp — Voice and Tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/); [Mailchimp — Writing principles](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/writing-principles/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell uses this:** adopt "voice constant, tone flexes" as the literal spec. FemWell's constant voice = **warm smart-friend + a wink of garden-mystic**. Tone flexes: lighter on a win, softer on a low day, plainer on an error.

## 4. MICROCOPY FOR EMOTIONAL MOMENTS — warm without saccharine

**Empty states are an opportunity, not a void — but they must still do a job.** NN/g: never default to a totally blank empty state (users can't tell if it's loading or broken); an empty state should **communicate system status, teach the feature, and offer a way to start the task** — "explain what could be displayed, and how to populate it," with an action link. NN/g's tone guidance is **practical, brief, contextual — not decorative copy** (source: [NN/g — Empty-state interface design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell uses this:** every empty state = one warm line + one clear action. *"Your garden's quiet today. Plant one thought and watch it grow → [Write a line]."* The omen/charm sits on top of a real next step, never instead of it.

**Ration delight — warmth works because it's a surprise.** "Delight comes from surprise — from warmth in unexpected places. When enthusiasm is the default, nothing stands out; save it for exactly the right moment. Don't put exclamation points on every screen or jokes in every empty state — users stop reading any of it" (source: [Pencil & Paper — Empty state UX](https://www.pencilandpaper.io/articles/empty-states), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell uses this:** the omen engine must be **rare-feeling**, not on every screen. If a flora-omen greets every open, it stops landing. Rotate; let plain days be plain so the bloom-day feels like a gift.

**Errors are where trust is won or lost — be human + specific, not cute.** "Error messages are where most users lose trust, but a well-crafted error can build trust" — the model is conversational + specific: *"That email doesn't look quite right. Did you mean gmail.com instead of gmal.com?"* (source: [Medium — UX writing for empty/error/success states](https://medium.com/@rounakbajoriastar/ux-writing-for-empty-states-errors-and-success-messages-tiny-texts-big-impact-70559a28306d), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell uses this:** on errors, **drop the mystic and get plain + kind**. No "the stars are misaligned" for a 500. Tone flexes to the reader's state of mind (the Mailchimp rule): a broken sync is a frustration moment, so be clear first, warm second.
- **Saccharine test (for Ms Atelier/Mx Storyteller):** if a line could go on a greeting card with no edit, it's too sweet. Anchor warmth to **a specific detail or a do-able action**, not free-floating sentiment. "You've got this!" fails; "Three lines logged this week — that's three more than last. Add today's? →" passes.

## 5. RITUAL / SEASONAL / "REMEMBERS YOU" MECHANICS — soulful vs gimmicky

**"On this day" memory is powerful but emotionally dangerous — soulfulness requires protective rules.** Facebook's On This Day / Timehop resurface past posts; the PM framed it as something that "rewires our relationships… reconnects me in a profound and beautiful way." But after the Year-in-Review backlash (it surfaced painful memories unprompted), Facebook built **protective rules** into the algorithm — it won't surface former romantic partners or deceased friends (source: [TIME — Facebook On This Day](https://time.com/3756975/facebook-on-this-day/); [TechCrunch — On This Day](https://techcrunch.com/2015/03/24/facehop/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell uses this:** a "your garden a year ago" / "on this day you wrote…" feature must have **the same protective gate** — never surface a hard entry (loss, a breakup, a low-mood log) unprompted. Memory resurfacing is opt-in-feeling and gentle, or it backfires. This is non-negotiable for a women's wellness app touching cycle/fertility/loss.

**Seasonal events drive habit when they're themed + time-boxed + rewarding (Finch).** Finch's seasonal events are "limited-time celebrations… with new rewards and exclusive micropets," each with its own theme + themed items, unlocking after 3 days of use (source: [Finch — Seasonal event overview](https://help.finchcare.com/hc/en-us/articles/37780438941965-Seasonal-Event-Overview), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell uses this:** tie the flora-omen engine to **the real almanac calendar** — solstices, the first snowdrop in Feb, new/full moons, saint's-day/seasonal sayings. A genuine seasonal anchor (a snowdrop omen that *only* appears in late winter) feels soulful; a random reward chest feels gimmicky.

**The annual retrospective works when it's framed as a mirror, not a scoreboard (Spotify Wrapped).** Wrapped is "like a mirror showing the moments, moods, and memories that made your listening uniquely yours" — personal, narrative, shareable (source: [Spotify Newsroom — 2025 Wrapped](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-12-03/2025-wrapped-user-experience/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell uses this:** a "Your Garden This Year" retrospective — which blooms appeared, which omens recurred, what the user tended — framed as a **mirror of a life lived across all domains** (relationships, joy, work, health-as-one-room), not a metrics scoreboard. Narrative + gentle, never "you logged 42% fewer symptoms."

**Ready-made ritual to adapt: "telling the bees/garden."** The English custom of telling the bees life news (births, weddings, news) is a perfect, sourced ritual primitive (source: [Bee Good — Telling the Bees](https://beegood.co.uk/blog/the-tradition-of-telling-the-bees/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell uses this:** invite the user to **"tell the garden"** a piece of news — a low-friction, anonymous-safe journaling/ritual entry that the garden then "remembers" and gently echoes back later. Spans all life domains (a new job, a first date, a friend's wedding), not just health.

## RECOMMENDED APPROACH (for Mr Lead Manager)

1. **Build the omen library as data, not copy** — each entry = `{trigger creature/flora, "they say" meaning, optional grounded-why, life-domain tags, hopeful-only flag, season window}`. Tag every entry to life domains so the engine surfaces relationship/work/joy omens, not just health (per the whole-life rule).
2. **Voice spec = Mailchimp method + Innocent warmth + almanac framing.** Constant voice: warm smart-friend with a garden-mystic wink. Always "they say…", never "this means you will…". Dry-warm, never brutal (Co-Star), never guilt (Duolingo), never saccharine.
3. **Ration the omens.** Rotate; let plain days stay plain so a bloom-day lands. Omen sits *on top of* a real action, never instead of it.
4. **Gate the memory engine** like Facebook had to — never resurface a hard entry unprompted.
5. **Anchor seasonality to the real almanac** (first snowdrop, solstices, moons) for soulfulness over gimmick.
