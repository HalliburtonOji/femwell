# FemWell — Premium Brand Bible Research — 2026-06-19

> Research-grade, citation-heavy foundation for a premium botanical brand bible. UK English, £, en-GB dates.
> Every claim carries a URL with verification date. Where the existing canon (`BRAND_IDENTITY.md`,
> `BRAND_FLORA.md`, `BRAND_IMAGE_RESEARCH.md`) already covers ground, this report **extends rather than re-derives**

## How to read this
- **"Premium vs crafty/clinical/cheap" is the through-line.** The single biggest risk for a botanical women's
  app is reading as *clip-art craft* or *bubblegum femtech*. Every section ends in a concrete rule that keeps
  FemWell on the premium-editorial side of that line.
- The existing canon already nails: botanical line-art discipline (Aesop restraint, Art Nouveau whiplash,

## 1. BOTANICAL / ILLUSTRATIVE BRAND SYSTEMS — premium vs crafty

> The canon already has the core (Aesop restraint, Art Nouveau whiplash line, Morris, fleuron — see
> `BRAND_IMAGE_RESEARCH.md` §1–4). New, verified findings below sharpen the **premium-vs-clipart** test.

**Aesop — restraint is a positioning decision, not a visual preference.** "Minimalism is not a visual preference
for Aesop but a positioning decision that shapes how people interpret quality… restraint reads as confidence."
The brand "builds demand through design discipline… content that feels like publishing rather than promotion,"
and its power equals "consistency of its personality across product, retail, communication, and service" (source:
[Brand Vision — Aesop marketing strategy](https://www.brandvm.com/post/aesop-marketing-strategy), fetched 2026-06-19).
Founder Dennis Paphitis "taught himself botany, chemistry, and philosophy" and personally chooses every quote on
labels — the botanical credibility is *earned*, not decorative (same source).
- **How FemWell applies this:** the flora system must read as *publishing, not decoration*. Every bloom/leaf
  earns its place by carrying documented meaning (already the canon thesis, `BRAND_FLORA.md` §0). The premium
  signal is **discipline of one hand** across every surface — not how many botanicals appear. Hold the line:
  one motif per fold, hairline, low opacity (canon §4.4).

**What separates premium botanical art from clipart (the diagnostic).** Across the cited corpus the consistent
markers of *premium* botanical work are: (a) **asymmetric, growing composition** (the whiplash S-curve — a line
as "a force," not a boundary), (b) **varied real species** rather than one stamped-and-mirrored shape, (c)
**hairline stroke-only line-art over paper**, never filled/3D/drop-shadowed leaves, and (d) **one motif per
fold** (source: `BRAND_IMAGE_RESEARCH.md` "Tasteful vs cluttered" table, which cites
[Wikipedia — Whiplash (decorative art)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiplash_(decorative_art)) and
[Solis Prints — Art Nouveau](https://www.solisprints.co.uk/pages/movement-art-nouveau)). Clipart fails all four:
symmetric arcs, one repeated leaf, filled shapes, wallpaper density.
- **How FemWell applies this:** this is the **craft gate for Ms Atelier**. Before any botanical ships, run the
  four-point test: growing/asymmetric? varied species? hairline stroke-only? one per fold? A "no" on any = it
  reads crafty. The app's `LeafGlyph`/`CornerSprig`/`RichBloomV2` already encode this; new art must match.

**Paper, tone, and "not pure black."** Aesop uses charcoal not pure black; the premium read comes from warm,
restrained tonal depth over a textured ground rather than flat fills (source:
[Brand Sensemaking — Aesop case study](https://brandsensemaking.com/aesop-brand-strategy-identity-case-study/),
fetched 2026-06-19; corroborated by `BRAND_IMAGE_RESEARCH.md` §1). FemWell already does this: `ink #0B0805`
(near-black, not pure) over `PAPER_BG` paper-grain (canon §2.1, §6.2).
- **How FemWell applies this:** never put botanical line-art on a flat fill. It must sit on `PAPER_BG`. The
  paper grain + warm near-black is *half* of what makes the line-art read as engraving rather than sticker.

## 2. EDITORIAL / FEMININE-BUT-NOT-CLICHÉ

**The femtech trap, named and sourced.** The dominant critique of women's-health design is the "reflexive pink"
default. Concrete guideline set: signal **function not gender** with colour; build **expanded palettes** where
"pink should become one optional choice, not the automatic standard"; avoid "curved forms and soft edges as
universal feminine shorthand" and avoid the **two extremes** — "overly clinical or excessively feminized."
Typography should express "both strength and nuance, clinical precision and emotional intelligence" — "neither
delicate scripts nor overly clinical fonts" (source:
[Winsome — Beyond Pink: design guidelines for femtech](https://winsomemarketing.com/womens-health-marketing/beyond-pink-design-guidelines-for-femtech-brands-that-respect-identity-diversity),
fetched 2026-06-19).
- **Named competitor moves (verified):** **Clue** deliberately chose "a neutral, scientific interface rather than
  cutesy-pink," later "expanded their color system beyond pink to include blues and greens" + a "custom
  iconography system focused on clarity"; **Elvie** uses "a minimal teal-dominant palette that sidesteps
  gender-coding," positioning as "cutting-edge health technology" not "a women's product" (sources: Winsome
  above; [The Observer — the male gaze is failing femtech](https://www.theobserverumd.org/post/quick-the-male-gaze-is-failing-femtech-navigating-design-profit-and-function), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell applies this:** FemWell's escape route is **different from Clue/Elvie**, and that's the
  opportunity. Clue/Elvie flee pink *into* clinical neutrality (teal, geometric, data-first). FemWell flees pink
  *into editorial warmth* — cream/gold/crimson/sage + serif + botanical. The canon's "warm smart-friend, not a
  clinician; health is one room, not the house" (CLAUDE.md) is the exact gap neither Clue nor Elvie fills.
  **Borrow:** Clue's *function-not-gender colour discipline* (FemWell's phase hues are semantic, not decorative —
  canon §2.4). **Reject:** the clinical-neutral aesthetic — FemWell is allowed to be beautiful where they are
  sterile. The "no pink-bubblegum, no cold-clinical" middle path is literally the documented white space.

**The editorial-lifestyle reference set (the warmth without cliché).** The Gentlewoman "celebrates modern women
of style and purpose" across "a diverse range of careers from fashion to politics… made by women, for women" —
femininity expressed through *substance and breadth of life*, not pink (source:
[Apartment Therapy — 14 indie magazines](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/14-indie-magazines-to-read-for-endless-life-inspiration-242890),
fetched 2026-06-19). **Cereal** wins on "a stunning clean aesthetic… literary style," its calm ethereal
photography "a huge part of the magazine's success"; **Kinfolk** is "clean, minimal… a beautiful Danish
aesthetic." Together "the clean, minimal visual style of Kinfolk and Cereal has greatly influenced design,
branding and photography in the last 10 years" (source:
[Apartment Therapy](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/14-indie-magazines-to-read-for-endless-life-inspiration-242890)
and [Scandinavia Standard — magazines we love](https://www.scandinaviastandard.com/scandi-six-magazines-we-love/),
fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell applies this:** lift three things and only three. (1) **Breadth = the femininity.** The
  Gentlewoman is feminine because it shows women across *all of life* (career, politics, culture) — exactly
  FemWell's whole-life thesis (CLAUDE.md). Let life-breadth, not pink, carry the gender signal. (2) **Generous
  whitespace + literary tone** (Cereal/Kinfolk) — but note the canon's TONE DIAL warning: "restraint = cohesion
  and craft, NOT sparseness" (`BRAND_IDENTITY.md` §0). FemWell is *lush* editorial, not minimalist white.
  (3) **Photography/illustration as the soul**, not chrome decoration — for FemWell that is the bloom/garden,
  treated with Cereal-grade care.
- **Avoid (from the same set):** Goop/Glossier-style influencer-gloss and millennial-pink can date fast and skew
  commercial; FemWell's cream-paper editorial world ages better and reads more premium (corroborated by the
  luxury-palette finding in §4: ivory/champagne/burgundy "old-money" reads more premium than pink-and-bright).

## 3. TYPOGRAPHY SYSTEMS & PAIRING

**Role-based, ratio-driven scale (the system, not a list of sizes).** A modular type scale is "a consistent
ratio-based system" so "each step inherits the role-appropriate font, weight, line height, and tracking." Common
ratios: **Minor Third (1.2)** subtle, **Major Third (1.25)** moderate, **Golden (1.618)** high-contrast (source:
[Kalamuna — modular type scaling](https://www.kalamuna.com/blog/modular-type-scaling-frontend-developers) and
[Figr — typography system design](https://figr.design/blog/typography-system-design), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell applies this:** the canon already has a **role table** (`BRAND_IDENTITY.md` §1) — Display /
  Heading / Title / Body / Caption / Eyebrow / Control — which is exactly the recommended model ("every node
  maps to a ROLE, no free-floating fontSize"). The research validates this. The one refinement: FemWell's
  allowed set (12/13/14/15/16/17/18/20 + two clamps) is *tighter than a pure 1.2 ratio* — that's fine and
  arguably better for a small app; keep it. Document it as a *quantised* scale (a hand-tuned subset of ~Minor
  Third) so it reads as deliberate, not arbitrary.

**Mobile legibility floors (why 11px fails).** Body text minimum is **16px**, "typically 16–18px for optimal
readability on screens"; headings must be "significantly larger to create clear distinction" (source:
[Kalamuna](https://www.kalamuna.com/blog/modular-type-scaling-frontend-developers) and
[B13 — UI font size guidelines](https://b13.com/blog/designing-with-type-a-guide-to-ui-font-size-guidelines),
fetched 2026-06-19). Use `clamp()` for desktop→mobile transition (same sources).
- **How FemWell applies this:** the canon already retired everything ≤11px and set floors (chrome ≥12, content
  ≥13, prefer 16+) after the on-device review (`BRAND_IDENTITY.md` §1 v2 note). The research **confirms 16px is
  the body floor** — so FemWell's `Body 16/500` default is correct, and the on-device `size-adjust:150%` on the
  serif (which renders Cormorant larger without changing declared px) is the right fix for Cormorant's small
  x-height. Keep the floors; they're industry-aligned.

**Serif+script+sans pairing principle.** Effective tone comes from "pairing a sharper heading family with a more
relaxed body face"; serifs are "optimized for rich reading experiences," sans-serif "clean and highly legible
for UI" (source: [DeveloperUX — typography best practices](https://developerux.com/2025/02/12/typography-in-ux-best-practices-guide/)
and [Figma — best serif fonts](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/best-serif-fonts/), fetched 2026-06-19).
The femtech guidance (§2) warns against "delicate scripts" as a *body/clinical* voice — a script must be a
**display accent only**, never set long or small (source: Winsome, above).
- **How FemWell applies this:** FemWell's three-voice system is textbook-correct: **Ephesis (script) = display
  page titles only**, **Cormorant Garamond (serif) = headings + reading body**, **system sans = chrome only**
  (`BRAND_IDENTITY.md` §1). The canon's own rule — "`.fw-display` SHORT page titles ONLY, never all-caps/long,
  script tangles" — is exactly the femtech warning. **This is already right; the research ratifies it.**

**Named typefaces worth knowing (for context / future swaps).** Verified editorial-serif options and their
character:
| Typeface | Foundry | Character | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cormorant Garamond** | Christian Thalmann (open-source) | Garamond-inspired *display* serif, large family | "larger counters make it more suitable as a text face" than display Cormorant; pairs with Cabin / Maison Neue / Montserrat (source: [Typewolf — Cormorant](https://www.typewolf.com/cormorant), fetched 2026-06-19). **The current app face — validated.** |
| **GT Sectra** | Grilli Type | Calligraphic broad-nib + "sharpness of the scalpel knife" | built for long-form journalism (*Reportagen*); GT Sectra / Fine / Display subfamilies (source: [Grilli Type — GT Sectra](https://www.grillitype.com/typeface/gt-sectra), fetched 2026-06-19). A premium *paid* alternative if Cormorant ever feels too light. |
| **Canela** | Commercial Type | High-contrast, contemporary luxury serif | "luxury and fashion projects call for high-contrast designs like Canela"; pairs with Graphik (source: [Made Good Designs — modern serif fonts](https://madegooddesigns.com/modern-serif-fonts/), fetched 2026-06-19). |
| **Tiempos / Freight / Austin** | Klim / others | Editorial workhorses | named as "versatile workhorse families" for editorial (source: [Made Good — best serif fonts](https://madegooddesigns.com/best-serif-fonts/), fetched 2026-06-19). |
- **How FemWell applies this:** **do not switch** — Cormorant Garamond is open-source, validated for text use,
  and already deeply wired (remap mechanism, size-adjust). Record GT Sectra and Canela as *known premium
  upgrades* only, so the choice reads as deliberate (chose Cormorant knowing the alternatives), and so a future
  "premium paid type" decision has a shortlist rather than a fresh search.

## 4. COLOUR THEORY & PALETTES — warmth / calm / premium + WCAG

**Why cream/gold/crimson reads premium (the "old-money" finding).** "The old-money aesthetic of ivory,
champagne, and warm taupe can feel just as premium as black and gold." "Luxury brands almost always use ivory
cream or brut champagne plus a warm taupe." Critically: **"Burgundy paired with cream creates a balance of
boldness and warmth"** — named for "classic visual brand identity systems." Pantone's 2025 Colour of the Year is
**Mocha Mousse**, "a warm, grounding brown linked to stability." Warm neutrals "build emotional trust… a sense of
calm and simplicity… wellness and comfort in an increasingly overstimulated digital space" (source:
[Vivid Creative — psychology of colour 2025](https://www.vividcreative.com/2025/07/25/the-psychology-of-colour-in-branding-2025s-mood-driven-palette/)
and [Zoviz — luxury brand colours](https://zoviz.com/blog/luxury-brand-colors-meanings), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell applies this:** **FemWell's palette is, almost exactly, the documented premium-warm formula.**
  `paper/cream #ECE7DA` + `gold #A8893F` + `crimson #BC2E27` ≈ ivory + warm-gold + burgundy — the "boldness and
  warmth" pairing named for classic brand systems. This is strong external validation that the palette is
  premium, not a guess. **Takeaway:** lean into it confidently; resist drift toward bright/pink/clinical, which
  the same sources flag as *less* premium.

**What makes a warm wellness palette premium vs muddy (concrete hex references).** Premium wellness palettes
"blend sophistication with authenticity" and pair a neutral ground with **one organic accent + one grounding
dark**, never "single, generic colours." Verified example palettes with hex:
| Palette | Hex set | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Golden Hour** | champagne `#C9B98F` · rich gold `#B89D62` · sage-taupe `#88856A` · charcoal `#474747` | "trust and stability" + "luxury from metallic undertones" |
| **Earthy Sophistication** | sage `#C9D3CA`/`#929C92` · taupe `#A39384` · charcoal `#161313` | sage adds "a healing, restorative element" |
| **Earthen Elegance** | taupe `#D8CBC2` · sand `#A18267` · olive `#3B3418` · brown `#2D241B` | "stability, timelessness, sophistication" |
(source: [Haute Stock — luxury palettes for wellness brands](https://hautestock.co/luxury-color-palettes-for-wellness-brands/), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **The "muddy" failure mode:** muddiness comes from *too many mid-tone warm browns at similar value with no
  anchoring dark and no clean light*. The premium fix in every cited palette is **a clean light ground + ONE
  saturated accent + ONE deep anchor**. FemWell's structure matches: clean `paperHi #F4EFE3` light → `crimson`
  the single saturated pop → `ink #0B0805` deep anchor (canon §2). **Takeaway:** the canon's "one gold, one
  crimson, one cream; retire the duplicate browns" (§2.3) is *literally* the anti-muddiness rule — the audit
  that retired `#3A2C1A`/`#9B8B7A`/two extra golds removed exactly the mid-tone-brown clutter that makes warm
  palettes read cheap. Keep that retirement strict.

**WCAG AA on a warm low-contrast palette (the hard constraint).** AA needs **4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large
text** (large = 18.66px bold or 24px+). AAA = 7:1 / 4.5:1. Crucially: "when working with warm, neutral palettes
like cream and beige, achieving 4.5:1 can be challenging since these colours are naturally similar in tone…
ratios are calculated from relative luminance, so a tool is far more reliable than the naked eye" (source:
[Recite Me — WCAG 4.5:1](https://reciteme.com/news/wcag-contrast-ratio-4-5-1/) and
[WebAbility — colour contrast 2026](https://www.webability.io/blog/color-contrast-for-accessibility),
fetched 2026-06-19).
- **How FemWell applies this (with the app's own tokens):** the danger zone is **text in `gold #A8893F` or
  `muted #2E261B` on `paper #ECE7DA`**, and any `blush`/`sage` text. Rule for the bible: **body text is `ink`
  or `inkSoft` only** (near-black on cream clears 4.5:1 comfortably); **`gold`/`crimson`/`sage`/`blush` are for
  large display, accents, borders, and icons — NOT for body or small captions**, where they risk failing AA on
  cream. `muted #2E261B` (dark brown) is fine for captions because it's near-black, not a light grey — the canon
  already insists on this ("dark brown, NOT a light grey", §2.1). **Action:** run every text/background token
  pair through a contrast checker and record the ratios in the bible (a small table), because "the naked eye"
  is unreliable on warm palettes. This is a verification task for Ms Accessibility.

## 6. BRAND STORYTELLING / NARRATIVE — the north star

**Archetype as the operational north star.** Mark & Pearson's *The Hero and the Outlaw* (2001) brought Jung's
archetypes to branding; "archetypes are the heartbeat of a brand… customers relate to a product as if it were
alive… they have a relationship with it and care about it." Once chosen, an archetype's **core desire becomes "a
guiding North Star for voice, design, and decision-making consistency"** — messaging, visuals and behaviour stay
aligned "around a single motivational force over time" (source:
[Scott Jeffrey — 12 brand archetype wheel](https://scottjeffrey.com/12-brand-archetype-wheel/) and
[TRUiC — 12 brand archetypes](https://startupsavant.com/startup-marketing/brand-archetypes), fetched 2026-06-19).

**The candidate archetypes for FemWell (verified profiles):**
| Archetype | Motivation | Voice | Example brands |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Caregiver** | helping & protecting others | "warm, nurturing, reassuring" | TOMS, Johnson & Johnson, Volvo |
| **Sage** | truth-seeking, understanding | "wise, assured, authoritative" | Google, TED, BBC |
| **Lover** | intimacy, connection | "sensual, romantic, indulgent" | Godiva, Aesop-adjacent |
| **Creator** | innovation, self-expression | "imaginative, artistic, nonconformist" | Apple, LEGO |
| **Innocent** | happiness, positivity | "optimistic, pure, honest" | Dove, Ben & Jerry's |
(source: [Scott Jeffrey](https://scottjeffrey.com/12-brand-archetype-wheel/), fetched 2026-06-19).

- **How FemWell applies this — the recommended archetype blend.** A women's *whole-life* wellness app that is
  explicitly "a warm smart-friend, not a clinician" maps to a **Caregiver core, tinted by Sage, with a touch of
  the Innocent's joy**. The Caregiver "warm, nurturing, reassuring" voice is the spine; the Sage prevents it from
  becoming saccharine (the app *knows things* — cycle science, real meaning); the Innocent's joy keeps it
  lighthearted-by-default (CLAUDE.md's "lighthearted by default; life-stage gently TINTs, never dominates").
  **Avoid** a pure Caregiver (can read as soft/clinical-maternal) and **avoid** Lover-led (too romance-coded for
  a whole-life app). This blend is the *behavioural* north star: every copy/design/feature decision answers to
  "warm friend who knows things and keeps it joyful."

**The central metaphor / north star (the recommendation).** The strongest brands run on one legible central
metaphor (the canon's flora thesis is already this — `BRAND_FLORA.md` §0 treats the brand as "a system of
sensory signs," meaning *transferred not arbitrary*, citing
[Brandingmag — semiotics of brand building](https://www.brandingmag.com/2015/02/04/the-semiotics-of-brand-building/)).
FemWell's metaphor is already implicit across the canon; this report makes it explicit and articulate:

> **THE NORTH-STAR METAPHOR — "Your life, in bloom: a living garden that grows as you do."**
>
> A woman's life is a garden, not a chart. It has seasons (the cycle — inner winter/spring/summer/autumn), it has
> long arcs (life-stages — from the teen daisy to the elder magnolia), it has many beds, not one (career,
> friendship, love, rest, joy — *health is one room, not the house*), and it is **tended, not optimised**. FemWell
> is the garden that remembers what bloomed where: every flower means something true, the butterfly arrives only
> when you genuinely return or change, and the garden is unmistakably **yours** (the deterministic flora
> fingerprint — `BRAND_FLORA.md` §7). The app doesn't track you; it *grows with you*.

- **Why this metaphor is load-bearing (not decoration):** it (a) **reframes femtech** away from clinical tracking
  toward whole-life tending — the documented white space (§2); (b) **unifies every surface** into one language
  (a sunflower always means radiance, a forget-me-not always means memory — canon §3 "one language"); (c) gives
  the **Caregiver-Sage-Innocent** voice a concrete home (a gardener is nurturing, knowledgeable, and quietly
  joyful); and (d) is **already half-built in code** (the flora system, fingerprint, page-character signatures).
  The bible should open with this single sentence and derive everything from it.

- **Three "sounds-dumb-but-has-a-seed" narrative ideas** (the obvious ones are taken):
  1. **The garden has weather, not streaks.** Reject the streak/scoreboard model (canon: "no scoreboards").
     Instead the garden has *seasons and weather* — a hard week is "a frost the garden survived," not a broken
     streak. Sounds twee; the seed is a genuinely no-guilt engagement model that fits the metaphor and the brand
     ban on scoreboards. (Grounded in the resilience flowers above — hellebore "blooms through the cold.")
  2. **Flowers you can give.** Floriography was a *language for sending messages* (canon §1.1). Let a user send
     another woman a *meant* flower in Community (a borage = "courage," a forget-me-not = "I remember") — the
     pairing-as-a-sentence mechanic (canon §2.5) becomes a social gift. Sounds like a sticker pack; the seed is
     that it's the only social-gift system where the gift *carries documented meaning*, which is uniquely on-brand.
  3. **The orchard = the community.** Many gardens make an orchard (canon §2.7). Sounds abstract; the seed is a
     genuinely novel community-visual (a garden-of-gardens) that makes "we help each other bloom" literal
     (companion planting, canon §2.5) and gives the bee its earned home.

## 7. MOTION / MICRO-INTERACTION — calm, organic, premium

**Concrete, sourced motion specification.** From a current, code-grade synthesis of Emil Kowalski's
*Animations on the Web* (the reference for premium web motion):

- **Easing — default ease-out for entering/exiting; ease-in-out for on-screen movement.** "Linear animations feel
  robotic"; spring/real-world easing "feels familiar and less artificial." Specific curves: ease-out cubic
  `cubic-bezier(0.215, 0.61, 0.355, 1)`, ease-out quint `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`, ease-out expo
  `cubic-bezier(0.19, 1, 0.22, 1)`; ease-in-out quart `cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.175, 1)` (source:
  [Vercel open-agents — web-animation-design SKILL.md](https://github.com/vercel-labs/open-agents/blob/main/.agents/skills/web-animation-design/SKILL.md), fetched 2026-06-19).
- **Duration — keep UI under ~300ms.** Micro-interactions 100–150ms; standard UI (tooltips/dropdowns) 150–250ms;
  modals/drawers 200–300ms; **exit ~20% faster than entrance**; **interactions done 100+ times/day should have
  NO animation** (same source).
- **Spring — subtle only.** Apple-style `{ type: "spring", duration: 0.5, bounce: 0.2 }`; "keep bounce subtle
  (0.1–0.3)"; "avoid bounce in most UI contexts" (same source).
- **Performance — transform & opacity ONLY** ("skip layout and paint, run on the GPU"); `will-change: transform`
  for shaky animations (same source).
- **Accessibility — `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce){ animation:none }` on EVERY animation, no exceptions
  even for opacity/colour** (same source). Corroborated by
  [Hack Design — great animations](https://www.hackdesign.org/lessons/great-animations/) (fetched 2026-06-19).

- **How FemWell applies this — the bible's motion law.** FemWell's motion is **breath, not bounce**. The canon's
  bloom motion is already exactly right and now externally validated: breath (scale 1→1.035), multi-axis sway
  (±1.5°), dew shimmer, one-shot settle, staggered delays, **transform/opacity only, one keyframe block per
  page, reduced-motion-gated, no blur on animated nodes** (`BRAND_IDENTITY.md` §5). Codify these motion tokens
  in the bible:
  - **Easing tokens:** `ease-out` = `cubic-bezier(0.215,0.61,0.355,1)` (enter/exit), `ease-in-out` =
    `cubic-bezier(0.77,0,0.175,1)` (move), plus the existing sheet curve `cubic-bezier(.32,.72,.24,1)` (canon
    §6.4) and slider `cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)` (canon §6.5) — both are ease-out-family, on-spec. Keep them.
  - **Duration tokens:** micro 120ms · standard 200ms · sheet/drawer 300ms · scrim fade 220ms (already canon
    §6.2/§6.4). Exits ~20% faster.
  - **No bounce** anywhere (the brand is calm/premium — bounce reads playful/cheap here; the source says avoid
    bounce in most UI). FemWell's organic feel comes from **slow ease + the bloom's breath/sway**, not springs.
  - **Reduced-motion is mandatory** on every animated node — the canon already does this; the bible states it as
    a hard gate.
  - **The 100+/day rule:** the bottom-nav, card taps, and other high-frequency actions stay **un-animated or
    ≤120ms** — calm comes partly from *not* animating the things you touch constantly.
