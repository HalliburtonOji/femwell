## 1. THE "HERO + SELECTABLE FILMSTRIP" (tap-to-promote) pattern — the headline finding

**Name.** The canonical name is the **image-gallery pattern**, composed of two named parts: a **"Thumbnail overview – Lets users scan the available images quickly"** and a **"Primary viewer – Shows the currently selected image at a larger size"** (source: https://uxpatterns.dev/patterns/media/image-gallery). In e-commerce it's the **product-gallery / thumbnail strip**; the sub-pattern where the strip sits *under* the big image and tapping a thumbnail swaps it in is commonly called **"thumbnail-to-hero" / "selectable filmstrip"** (a "filmstrip of several images that users can click through," navigation "in the form of arrows, dot indicators, thumbnail previews, or text-based tabs" — source: https://www.vev.design/blog/web-carousel-design/).

**The core interaction.** Thumbnails are "a list of all images in the gallery in a horizontal or vertical grid… giving a 'sneak peek' of what's coming up," and **the current image should be highlighted** so the user knows their position (source: https://uxplanet.org/ecommerce-ux-best-practices-product-image-gallery-e0ce6145d270). The selected thumbnail promotes into the primary viewer; a "Navigation controls" element lets users "Move between images without forcing users back to the overview" (source: https://uxpatterns.dev/patterns/media/image-gallery).

**Best practice (measured).** Show enough of the strip that hidden items are obvious: **"For products with up to as many as 10–14 product thumbnails, simply displaying them all… will often be the easiest and 'safest' design solution"** (source: https://baymard.com/blog/truncating-product-gallery-thumbnails). The failure mode is a truncated strip with no cue: **"Truncating the thumbnail carousel without any accompanying visual indicators can lead users to make the false assumption that the set of thumbnails viewable by default represents the total number available"** and **"Users are more likely to miss vital visual product information… if they do not find hidden thumbnails"** (same source). Remedies Baymard names: show all, arrow controls, a "+5" truncation thumbnail, or a **partial (peeking) thumbnail** to signal more.

**The swap animation.** No single spec mandates one, but two safe transitions dominate galleries: a **crossfade** of the hero image (old fades out / new fades in, ~150–250ms) or a **slide** matching the direction of travel. The selected thumbnail should animate its selected state (ring/underline) *in sync* with the hero swap so the two feel bound. Do **not** autoplay the gallery ("Don't autoplay the gallery" — source: https://uxplanet.org/ecommerce-ux-best-practices-product-image-gallery-e0ce6145d270). For a calm app, prefer crossfade over slide (gentler, no motion-direction ambiguity) and keep it under 250ms so it reads as a promotion, not a scene change.

**Pitfalls.** (a) No visible "selected" state → user loses their place (source: https://uxplanet.org — highlight current image). (b) Truncated strip with no peek/"+N" → hidden images missed (Baymard, above). (c) Removing focus styles: **"Do not remove focus styles without a visible replacement"**, and **"Verify that image gallery can be completed using keyboard alone"** (source: https://uxpatterns.dev/patterns/media/image-gallery). (d) Assuming pointer/touch/AT behave alike: **"Do not assume pointer, touch, and assistive technologies will all interact with the pattern the same way"** (same). (e) Treating the media as decoration: **"Treating media as decoration only – Important uploads and playback flows break when the design assumes the media is just visual garnish"** (same).

→ *FemWell adapts:* a **"Featured + filmstrip" card** for Lifestyle / Daily Story / Horoscope and product/deal galleries — ONE full-width botanical hero on top, a **peeking strip of 4–8 thumbnails** beneath (best-first, ~15% peek at the right edge so hidden ones are obvious, "+N" if truncated). Tapping a thumb **crossfades (≤220ms)** it into the hero while a **thin oxblood underline / gold ring** marks the selected thumb in sync. The hero caption (Fraunces hook + one cream/oxblood tag) updates with the swap. No autoplay. Keyboard-reachable, visible focus ring. This is the single new pattern most worth mocking first — it turns a static hero into a browsable editorial "front page."

## 2. EDITORIAL / MAGAZINE cards — premium big-typography story cards

**Editorial systems ship a *named family* of card types, not one card.** A digital-magazine design system names: **hero sections** ("Variants: image-led, video-led, or split layout with headline and image"; "Content: kicker, headline, dek/standfirst, primary CTA, optional category tag"); **feature cards** ("kicker, headline, standfirst, byline, lead image, body copy, pull-quotes, inline media, footnotes"); **pull-quote components** ("Break long stories with pull-quotes and imagery"); **listicle/card grid** ("Cards include image, headline, 1–3 lines of summary, and a clear CTA"); a **cover template** ("Hero, kicker, headline, and primary CTA; room for issue metadata"); and a **Q&A template** ("Alternating Q and A blocks, portrait slot, optional audio embed, and a takeaway box") (source: https://www.3dissue.com/how-to-build-a-digital-magazine-design-system-templates-components-and-governance/).

**Premium = layered type-over-image + consistent scale, tested on real copy.** Apple News lets you **"layer a title and heading in front of an image, with their colors optimized for legibility"** and even create **"an illusion of multiple flat layers by causing the article body to overlap the header as the user scrolls"** (source: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applenews/apple-news-format-tutorials). The magazine system stresses **"consistent type sizes and spacing; scannable subheads and pull-quotes"** and warns **"templates that look great with lorem ipsum can fail with real headlines or captions"** — i.e. premium comes from testing components with authentic copy (source: https://www.3dissue.com/how-to-build-a-digital-magazine-design-system...).

**The kicker/standfirst is the premium signal most apps skip.** Editorial hero and feature cards both lead with a **kicker** (small category eyebrow) above the headline and a **dek/standfirst** below — the small-caps-eyebrow → big-serif-headline → light-standfirst rhythm is what reads as "magazine," not "blog card" (source: https://www.3dissue.com/how-to-build-a-digital-magazine-design-system...).

→ *FemWell adapts:* two named cards. **(a) CoverStory card** — full-bleed botanical image with a **layered kicker + Fraunces headline + one-line standfirst** set *over* the image (legibility-optimised scrim), one oxblood category tag. **(b) PullQuote card** — a single beautiful line in large Cormorant on cream, botanical flourish, attributed, tap-to-share. Both extend our existing "Letter/note" card (prior file) with the missing **kicker → headline → standfirst** editorial ladder. Rule: always mock with real Halli-voice copy, never lorem — that's where premium lives.

## 3. WELLNESS DASHBOARD cards — calm-not-clinical daily surfaces

**The strongest model: "Today = a Top Stories page," dynamic by time of day.** Oura redesigned its Today tab explicitly: **"We wanted to make the Today tab like the 'Top Stories' page of a news app, delivering the most timely, relevant health updates"**; **"Depending on the time of day (or night) you open the Oura App, Oura will surface a health insight relevant to your activities…"**; the tab helps you focus on **"the 'so what?' of your health"** (source: https://ouraring.com/blog/new-oura-app-experience/). Glanceable scores stay pinned: **"The Sleep, Readiness, and Activity Scores you know and love are still at your fingertips, located at the top of the Today Tab"** (same).

**Cards are grouped by life-area, surfaced contextually, not dumped.** Oura groups cards **"around the key health areas: readiness, sleep, activity, stress, women's health, heart health, metabolic health"** and distils features into **"easily digestible insights, ready at your fingertips at any time of day"** (source: https://ouraring.com/blog/new-oura-app-experience/; https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360058599753).

**Daily check-in / mood cards drive personalisation — and should feel like a "quiet exhale," not a chart.** Calm and Headspace use **regular mood check-ins** to refine suggestions, letting users **log mood and tag stressors** (source: https://www.themindfulnessapp.com/articles/best-meditation-apps-features-comparison-2025). Users **"found calm, beautiful design visually pleasing and emotionally uplifting, with supportive tones that felt kind, grounding, and non-judgmental"**, and reach for wellness tools precisely to **avoid "tools that feel overly clinical or diagnostic"**; a wellness surface should feel like **"a quiet exhale"** (source: https://gapsystudio.com/blog/mental-health-app-design/; per the indexed snippet of hollylau.com/designing-with-compassion — ECONNREFUSED on direct fetch this week, so treated as corroborating not primary).

**Gentle-progress, not guilt-streaks.** A Duolingo-style habit tracker was praised for **"a calming streak tracker that helps users show up for their habits without guilt, chaos, or pressure"** (source: https://www.notion.com/templates/duolingo-type-habit-tracker) — the calm inversion of the leaderboard streak.

→ *FemWell adapts:* four calm cards. **(a) "Your day" summary card** — a Top-Stories-style opener that changes by time of day (morning intention / evening reflection), one warm line + one soft CTA, phase-tinted not colour-coded-clinical. **(b) DailyCheckIn card** — a 3–5 tap mood/energy log *inside the card* (see §5), warm chips not a slider grid; feeds personalisation. **(c) GentleProgress card** — the growing-flora streak (BRAND_FLORA) framed as "showing up," never a red-amber-green dashboard, no guilt on a missed day. **(d) CycleWheel card** — a soft phase wheel (phase hues #BC2E27/#8FAF8F/#D4AF37/#8E6E8E) as a glanceable "where am I," warm copy ("Follicular — rising energy"), NOT a fertility-clinic gauge. All grouped by life-area (health · relationships · career · joy), surfaced contextually like Oura, never dumped in one wall.

## 4. LIST / COLLECTION & MASONRY — visual browsing surfaces

**Masonry (Pinterest) exists because uniform grids waste space and flatten variety.** Masonry is **"a grid arrangement method where items stack vertically into columns based on available space. Items fill gaps left by shorter elements rather than aligning to strict horizontal rows"**; **"Traditional grid systems force items into rigid rows, creating gaps when content varies in height. Masonry solves this by letting each item rise up to fill available vertical space,"** giving **"a dense, visually engaging arrangement perfect for image galleries… and card-based interfaces"** (sources: https://freefrontend.com/css-masonry-layouts/; https://blog.scottlogic.com/2025/09/17/intro-to-masonry.html). The variable-height stagger is the whole point — it signals editorial variety and invites browse-not-scan.

**Airbnb's collection model: photo-first cards, soft shape language, a photo-only wishlist variant.** Property cards are a **"photo-first card. 1:1 aspect-ratio image with rounded.md corner clipping, image carousel dots overlay,"** a **"Guest favorite" floating badge top-left** and **a heart icon top-right… Rausch-filled when saved**; the shape language is **"soft… every interactive element is rounded"** (~14px cards, pill search) (source: https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/airbnb/DESIGN.md). Crucially, a **photo-only variant exists**: **"property-card-photo — The photo plate itself, separated as a token because some surfaces (wishlist, search results) reuse just the photo without the meta block"** — and experience cards use a **taller 4:5 aspect** for a different content rhythm (same source).

**Recipe/collection cards: image-first order + a bookmark save + folder box.** NYT Cooking's page **"follows a logical order (a large image first to draw users in, followed by the ingredients… and then finally the cooking steps)"**; **"the high-quality images and clean design make it visually appealing"**; the **save button "is represented with a clear, recognizable bookmark icon"** into a **Recipe Box split into "Recently Saved" and "Your Folders"** (sources: https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/02/design-critique-nyt-cooking-mobile-app/). "Editor-curated collections make it easy to find the right recipe" (source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nyt-cooking-quick-tasty-meals/id911422904).

→ *FemWell adapts:* three cards. **(a) MasonryCollection** — a variable-height botanical pin-board for Lifestyle "For You" / saved items; heights vary by content (a quote card is short, a story card tall) so it browses like Pinterest, cream gutters wider than Pinterest's for calm. **(b) PhotoTile (save)** — an Airbnb-style photo-first card with a **heart top-right that fills oxblood when saved**, one-line title beneath, soft ~14px radius; a **photo-only variant** for dense wishlist/saved grids. **(c) RecipeCard / CollectionCard** — image-first, bookmark save into a "Saved" box with **folders** (our "Recently saved" + named collections), warm editorial framing (works for recipes, reads, rituals, deals). Save = a **bookmark or heart**, one canonical icon app-wide.

## 5. INTERACTION-RICH cards — log/swipe/story/checklist/audio inside the card

**Story-format (tap-through) card.** The Instagram/Snapchat pattern: **"segmented bars across the top, one filling at a time. Only the active segment animates its width 0→100%; segments before it are full, segments after are empty"**; **"Tap the right half = next, left half = previous"**; **"Press-and-hold = pause"** so users can read; per-panel duration **"~4s"** driven by a timer loop (not pure CSS) so **"pause/resume and tap-to-skip"** stay trivial (source: https://dev.to/dev48v/i-rebuilt-instagram-stories-segmented-progress-bars-4bil). This is a full-screen, self-advancing card sequence.

**Inline-input / "log in the card."** Duolingo has made **"cards… quietly become Duolingo's universal interface metaphor"** — **"Lesson rewards: cards. Vocabulary recall… cards. Daily Quest progress: cards"** — with actions performed *in* the card: **"the rewards came at me as a deck of cards. Swipe one. Reveal the next. Tap to claim"** (source: https://medium.com/product-powerhouse/duolingo-just-made-everything-a-card-heres-the-pm-case-study-82694805ca13). The pattern generalises: the log/answer/claim happens on the card face, not on a pushed detail screen.

**Gentle swipe / dismiss with recovery.** Swipe cards are **"a highly interactive, binary decision-making interface"**; make the motion feel right with **spring physics so the card snaps back if the user doesn't pass a threshold**, fade in a **"Like/Nope" overlay** as confirmation, and **"an 'Undo' button… allow users to recover the last dismissed card"** (source: https://medium.com/@phillfarrugia/building-a-tinder-esque-card-interface-5afa63c6d3db; https://www.joshmorony.com/create-tinder-style-swipe-cards-with-ionic-gestures/). (Also see prior file §5: a nudge is dismissible-by-definition with swipe + ✕ + snooze.)

**Audio / now-playing card.** A mini/now-playing card shows **"the currently playing track, album art, and basic playback controls"** in a small persistent surface (source: https://www.tunesmake.com/spotify-music/spotify-mini-player.html); richer variants add **"lyrics, check which songs are coming up next, add a song to a playlist"** (source: https://www.viwizard.com/apple-music-tips/apple-music-mini-player.html). The player lives *in* the card.

→ *FemWell adapts:* four cards. **(a) StoryCard** — tap-through Daily Story / onboarding / "your week in review": segmented cream/gold bars top, tap-right/left, **press-and-hold to pause** (calm reading), timer-driven, botanical full-screen panels, no auto-loop into ads. **(b) LogInCard** — mood/energy/ritual logged *on the card face* (warm chips + one tap), then the card gently confirms ("Logged — rest well"); no push to a form screen. **(c) GentleSwipe** — a soft "keep / not now" for suggested reads/rituals/prompts with **snap-back spring + Undo**, framed warmly ("Save for later" / "Maybe another day"), never a hot-or-not binary. **(d) NowPlaying card** — Listen/meditation player IN the card (botanical thumb, play/scrub, "up next"), our existing media-card spec made persistent. Each keeps the action on the card; deep-link full-screen only for the full article/player.

## 6. AFFIRMATION / QUOTE / MOMENT cards — the single-line beautiful surface

**The "moment" card is deliberately one line, one breath.** The I Am app is **"a clean, minimalistic interface to get exactly what you need—empowering words and a moment of peace—without any distractions,"** and lets users **"choose the design of the font and image of your affirmations page"** (source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/i-am-daily-affirmations/id874656917; https://theiam.app/). The whole card is the message — no chrome competing with the line.

**Calm's Daily Calm quote is a shareable, dated ritual.** Calm surfaces **"words of encouragement whenever you need a spirit boost,"** and past **Daily Calm quote images are shareable content** (source: https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005141734-Daily-Quotes). The pattern: one quote a day, beautifully set, built to be shared out as an image.

**Share-out is a first-class action on these cards.** Both I Am and Calm treat **customisable design + sharing** as core, not an afterthought (source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/i-am-daily-affirmations/id874656917; https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005141734). The card doubles as a social artefact.

→ *FemWell adapts:* a **MomentCard** — one beautiful line (Cormorant/Ephesis) on cream with a single botanical flourish, phase-tinted, no other chrome. A daily affirmation / horoscope pull / "gentle reminder." **Share-out is a first-class action**: tap → render a branded cream-and-flora share image (oxblood mark, no clinical framing). This is the most "screenshot-and-share" surface in the app and the cheapest to build — a pure typographic card. One a day, dated, collectible.

## 7. OTHER distinct modern card TYPES worth a name

**Testimonial / voice card.** A recognised card type: a quote + attribution (avatar, name, role) framed to build trust (sources: https://elements.envato.com/testimonial-card-elements-website-ui-design-4SDEPFA; https://www.stan.vision/journal/ui-card-design-examples-and-best-practices). → *FemWell:* an anonymous **community voice card** — a member's line ("What one of us said this week"), warm, anonymised, phase-tinted rim (ties Community into Today).

**Comparison card.** A card that sets two options/states side by side for a decision (source: https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/ui-card-design/ — card taxonomy incl. comparison). → *FemWell:* a gentle **"then vs now"** card (last cycle vs this cycle; last month's mood vs this) or "two paths" for a choice — celebratory framing, no scoreboard.

**Poll / vote card.** An inline card where the tap *is* the vote and results reveal in place (an inline-input card variant, §5; general card best-practice: "Cards should provide a snapshot… only communicating what one will find" — source: https://www.stan.vision/journal/ui-card-design-examples...). → *FemWell:* a **Community poll / "this or that"** card (lighthearted: "cosy night in or big night out?") — vote on the face, results bloom in, spans life not just health (RULE 1).

**Timeline / agenda card.** A single container holding an ordered sequence of moments (validated in prior file §item-6). → *FemWell:* Planner "today's thread" — botanical node markers down a ritual→event→reflection ribbon; here for completeness, already scoped.

**Map / place card.** A card with a small static map + place meta (Airbnb "distance / dates… muted" meta line pattern — source: https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/airbnb/DESIGN.md). → *FemWell:* an **Events/Deals place card** — small map thumb + venue + one CTA, for local women's events; soft-rounded, photo-first.

**Empty-state card done well.** An empty state should carry **"Image… Title… Explanation text… Call to Action,"** choose a **"text tone… that aligns with your brand,"** and — even on errors — **"include a CTA button that allows users to navigate elsewhere"**; the three types are **first-use, no-results, and error** (source: https://www.mockplus.com/blog/post/empty-state-ui-design). → *FemWell:* every empty surface (no saved items, no journal yet, no events) gets a **botanical illustration + warm line + one soft CTA** ("Nothing saved yet — start a collection"), never a dead grey "No data." A signature calm-brand moment.
