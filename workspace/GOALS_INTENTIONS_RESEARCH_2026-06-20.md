# Research — Daily Intentions, Line of the Day, Life Goals — 20/06/2026

FemWell wants DAILY INTENTIONS, a user-specific LINE OF THE DAY, and LIFE GOALS (long + short term) baked into existing pages (Today, Planner, Journal, Garden, Profile, Pulse) — NO new nav tab. Brand is a living garden with a flora lifecycle (bud→bloom→seed→rest), gentle omens, a warm + funny + "a little witch" voice. This file grounds the brainstorm in real, current evidence: what actually works, what's cargo-cult, and what to AVOID (guilt, vanity metrics, over-clinical). ## 1. INTENTION-SETTING vs GOAL-SETTING

- **The core distinction is tense + locus.** "If goals are rooted in your tomorrow and where you want to go, intentions are rooted in your today and who you're actively becoming. If goals are about a destination, intentions are about a direction." (source: https://www.omaritani.com/blog/goals-vs-one-word-intentions). Goals = future/outcome/measurable; intentions = present/values/process and "more fluid, open to interpretation" (source: https://www.movefuelmind.com/blog/the-difference-between-goals-and-intentions).
- **Sankalpa = the yogic root practice.** From Sanskrit *san* ("connection with the highest truth") + *kalpa* ("vow") — "a solemn vow and commitment we make with ourselves to support our highest truth," traditionally set in a receptive state (meditation / yoga nidra) so it takes root subconsciously, and phrased in the **present tense as if already true** (source: https://theyogacoach.co.uk/2021/01/03/intensions-and-sankalpa-vs-resolutions-and-goals/).
- **Why a daily intention helps: priming + attention.** "Start each day with a quick intention-setting ritual. Ask yourself, 'How can I live out my intentions today?'" — intention acts as a compass that keeps attention on the chosen direction "without getting caught in attachment to outcome" (source: https://punchpass.com/resources/blog/20-unique-yoga-intention-examples-for-transformative-practice/). One-word intentions are favoured precisely because they're easy to recall and re-prime through the day (source: https://www.omaritani.com/blog/goals-vs-one-word-intentions).

**How FemWell uses this:** Separate the two surfaces. The **daily intention** is a present-tense, one-line "who I'm being today" set on Today each morning (a sankalpa, e.g. "I move gently and trust my pace") — NOT a task. **Life goals** are the future/outcome layer living in Planner/Profile. The garden metaphor maps cleanly: intention = the bud opening *today*; goal = the bloom you're tending *toward*.

## 2. GOAL-SETTING SCIENCE — what's evidence vs cargo-cult

- **Locke & Latham (1968→): specific + challenging beats "do your best."** Across ~90% of studies, specific and challenging (but attainable) goals produced higher performance than vague goals; participants with difficult goals performed up to 250%+ higher than those with the easiest goals (source: https://www.mindtools.com/azazlu3/lockes-goal-setting-theory/). Their five principles: clarity, challenge, commitment, feedback, task complexity (source: https://strategicmanagementinsight.com/tools/locke-lathams-five-principle-framework/).
- **SMART is a derivative, not the theory — and is over-applied.** Locke & Latham's work "confirms the usefulness of SMART," but a systematic review in sport found **over a third of studies showed specific goals were NOT superior to vague/do-your-best goals**, and warns "one-size-fits-all" acronyms like SMART "are likely not appropriate/effective for every" person (source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1750984X.2021.1901298). Cargo-cult = bolting "SMART" onto a wish without commitment, feedback, or fit.
- **WOOP / MCII (Oettingen + Gollwitzer): the strongest practical recipe.** Four steps — **W**ish, **O**utcome (imagine + feel it), **O**bstacle (name the *inner* hurdle), **P**lan (one if-then). Plan format: "If [obstacle X arises], then I will [response Y]." Mental contrasting forces an expectancy check that converts feasible wishes into commitment and *releases* infeasible ones cleanly (source: https://positivepsychology.com/mental-contrasting/). Evidence: meta-analysis (k=94) put if-then plans at **d ≈ 0.65** on goal attainment; WOOP users were ~twice as physically active as info-only controls; ~30% diet improvement (sources: https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/woop-mental-contrasting-oettingen-wish-outcome-obstacle-plan/ , https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4106484/). **Caveat to flag:** most studies are by the creator and follow-ups run only a few months (source: https://positivepsychology.com/mental-contrasting/).
- **Self-Determination Theory: motivation needs autonomy + competence + relatedness.** Satisfying these three needs yields self-motivation, satisfaction and well-being; thwarting them diminishes motivation. Autonomous (self-chosen, value-aligned) goals stick; controlled/imposed ones don't (sources: https://www.apa.org/research-practice/conduct-research/self-determination-theory.html , https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2022_RyanDeci_SDT_Encyclopedia.pdf).

**How FemWell uses this:** Use **WOOP as the goal-creation flow** (it's the most buildable, highest-evidence pattern and is inherently warm — it names the *inner* obstacle, not a failure). Make goals **self-authored, never prescribed** (SDT autonomy); let users phrase their own challenge level (SDT competence); surface optional "tend together" community on a goal (SDT relatedness). AVOID forcing every goal into a rigid SMART template — offer it as one lens, not the gate.

## 3. HABIT & BEHAVIOUR DESIGN

- **BJ Fogg Tiny Habits — the recipe is a fixed format.** "After [anchor], I will [tiny behavior], then [celebration]." Anchor = an existing routine (the Prompt); tiny = <30s, ridiculously small (Ability); celebration = an *authentic* immediate positive emotion that wires the reward (Motivation). Underlying model **B = MAP** (Behaviour = Motivation × Ability × Prompt) — any factor at zero = no behaviour (sources: https://goalsandprogress.com/tiny-habits-fogg-behavior-model-explained/ , https://habitpixel.com/blog/the-tiny-habits-formula-the-easiest-way-to-create-a-new-habit). Fogg: fake celebrations don't work — the emotion must be real.
- **James Clear Atomic Habits — identity, stacking, systems.** "Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become." Habit stacking formula: "After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]." "You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems." (source: https://www.nateliason.com/notes/atomic-habits-james-clear).
- **Never miss twice (the gentle re-entry rule).** "Missing once is an accident. Missing twice is the start of a new habit." The goal is not zero misses — it's avoiding the *spiral* after one (source: https://jamesclear.com/quotes/the-first-mistake-is-never-the-one-that-ruins-you-it-is-the-spiral-of-repeated-mistakes-that-follows-missing-once-is-an-accident-missing-twice-is-the-start-of-a-new-habit).
- **The limit of streaks (critique).** Streaks run on loss aversion; once a day is missed they trigger the "abstinence violation effect" and rage-quitting, and the demotivation *grows* with the streak length (losing 500 days can make someone quit entirely). Gamification can "sap the intrinsic enjoyment" and crowd out intrinsic motivation for meaningful behaviours (sources: https://thedecisionlab.com/insights/consumer-insights/streak-creep-the-perils-of-too-much-gamification , https://blog.cohorty.app/the-psychology-of-streaks-why-they-work-and-when-they-backfire/).

**How FemWell uses this:** Frame the daily intention/goal nudge as a **Tiny Habit recipe anchored to an existing FemWell ritual** ("After I log my morning mood, I set one intention"). Use **identity "votes"** as the progress unit instead of a streak counter ("3 votes this week toward *becoming a woman who rests well*"). Replace the streak with **never-miss-twice gentle re-entry** copy. AVOID a big red streak number.

## 4. IDENTITY-BASED & VALUES-BASED goals

- **"Be" goals beat "do" goals for stickiness.** Clear: focus "not on what you want to achieve, but on who you wish to become" — "The goal is not to read a book, the goal is to become a reader… not to run a marathon, to become a runner." Identity-based change outlasts outcome-based change because each action reinforces the self-image (source: https://www.nateliason.com/notes/atomic-habits-james-clear).
- **ACT: values are directions, goals are milestones.** "Values are directions we keep moving in, whereas goals are what we want to achieve along the way… unlike goals, values are never accomplished." Values guide choices according to the kind of person you strive to be (source: https://www.cbttherapy.com/post/values-vs-goals-an-acceptance-commitment-therapy-act-perspective).
- **Values clarification comes first, then committed action.** In ACT you clarify what's personally meaningful *before* setting goals; "committed action" is long-term, values-based goal-setting that flows out of clarified values (sources: https://positivepsychology.com/act-worksheets/ , https://psychwire.com/free-resources/expert-insights/resource-yorj7s/values-versus-goals).

**How FemWell uses this:** Make life goals **identity-framed by default** — store a goal as "Becoming a woman who ___" with concrete do-goals nested under it. Run a light **values pass once** (in Profile/onboarding or a seasonal review) so goals inherit from values, not from a generic template. This is also the bridge to the "a little witch" voice: values clarification reads as self-knowing/ritual, not corporate OKR.

## 5. SEASONAL / CYCLICAL goal-setting & reflection loops

- **12-Week Year: ~90-day sprints beat annual resolutions.** ~80% of resolutions are abandoned by February; the 12-Week Year (Moran & Lennington) treats every 12 weeks as its own "year" to keep urgency, with **weekly progress reviews** and a **retrospective each cycle** before the next sprint (sources: https://thegrio.com/2024/01/02/whats-the-12-week-year-the-realistic-way-to-set-and-achieve-new-years-resolutions/ , https://www.thesimplicityhabit.com/set-the-best-goals-seasonally-a-review-of-the-12-week-year/). It explicitly "aligns with Japanese concepts of cyclical time… working alongside the seasons."
- **Anti-resolution movement: ditch rigid Jan-1 outcomes for adaptive, kinder cycles** (source: https://hardlyhamilton.com/2025/12/26/anti-resolution-toolkit-achieve-goals/).
- **Cycle-syncing goals to the four menstrual "seasons."** Menstrual (inner winter) = rest, reflection, review long-term goals/journal; follicular = "accelerate," ideal for goal-setting, creativity, new projects; ovulatory = peak energy, tackle challenge + relationships; luteal = organise, complete, self-care (sources: https://www.geisinger.org/health-and-wellness/wellness-articles/2026/04/02/17/52/menstrual-cycle-syncing , https://balancedawakening.com/blog/the-four-seasons-of-your-cycle-understanding-how-to-cycle-sync-and-its-impact-on-mental-health). **Honest caveat:** cycle-syncing's productivity claims are largely practitioner-led, not strongly RCT-backed — frame as a gentle invitation, not a rule (source: https://www.theambitionplanner.com/post/the-truth-about-cycle-syncing).

**How FemWell uses this:** This is the **best metaphor fit in the brief.** Map goal cadence to the flora lifecycle AND cycle phases: **bud (follicular) = set/plant new goals; bloom (ovulatory) = push bold goals; seed (luteal) = organise + harvest progress; rest (menstrual) = reflect + review, no new goals demanded.** Run a **seasonal review** (every cycle / quarter) as a Garden + Pulse ritual ("year in review/ahead" in miniature), NOT an annual resolution. Let phase gently *tint* the suggested action; never block.

## 6. NO-GUILT / SELF-COMPASSION progress design

- **Self-compassion drives the *right* motivation.** Neff: self-compassion is **positively** linked to mastery (learn/grow) goals and personal initiative, and **negatively** linked to performance goals, fear of failure, perfectionism and self-criticism. "Self-compassionate people are motivated to achieve, but for intrinsic reasons, not… social approval." (source: https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Neff-2023.pdf).
- **Guilt creates a doom-loop; self-compassion cuts it.** After a mistake, guilt tends to drive *more* mistakes; self-compassion lets you "take a step back and the whole energy shifts" — the exact mechanism behind never-miss-twice (sources: https://www.nosmallendeavor.com/self-compassion-kristin-neff , https://jamesclear.com/second-mistake).
- **Streak/guilt mechanics backfire** (re-cited from §3): loss-aversion streaks cause rage-quitting and crowd out intrinsic enjoyment; **forgiveness mechanics (grace periods, freezes, gentle re-entry) prevent the all-or-nothing collapse** (source: https://thedecisionlab.com/insights/consumer-insights/streak-creep-the-perils-of-too-much-gamification).

**How FemWell uses this:** Show progress as **growth, not a chain.** A goal's plant grows with each "vote"; a missed day means the plant *rests*, never wilts or dies. Copy on re-entry is warm + witty ("The garden waited for you. Plant one seed today."). NO red streak counters, NO guilt push notifications, NO "you broke your streak." Progress framing = mastery/identity ("you're becoming…"), never performance ranking.

## 7. WHERE FEATURES LIVE (embed, don't add a module)

- **Implementation intentions work *because* they're tied to existing routines/cues.** The if-then "when situation X, then Y" is literally a cue-anchored plan — its power is contextual triggering, not a standalone to-do (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4106484/).
- **Tiny Habits + habit stacking both demand an *existing anchor*.** "After [anchor I already do], I will…" — the method is explicitly about grafting onto current behaviour to avoid introducing new prompts (sources: https://goalsandprogress.com/tiny-habits-fogg-behavior-model-explained/ , https://www.nateliason.com/notes/atomic-habits-james-clear). A separate "Goals tab" *is* a new prompt — the friction the research says to avoid.
- **SDT autonomy = low-friction, contextual, opt-in** beats a demanding standalone surface that feels controlling (source: https://www.apa.org/research-practice/conduct-research/self-determination-theory.html).

**How FemWell uses this — surface map (all surfaces named, no new tab):**
- **Today** — set the daily intention + show LINE OF THE DAY; anchor = morning mood log.
- **Planner** — WOOP goal-creation flow + the if-then plans land here as contextual cues against real days/phases.
- **Journal** — seasonal/cycle reflection review (inner-winter prompt: "review your long-term goals").
- **Garden** — visual progress: each goal = a plant in the lifecycle; intentions = today's buds; rest, not wilt.
- **Profile** — values pass + identity goals ("Becoming a woman who…") + long-term goal list.
- **Pulse/Trends** — gentle, kind progress patterns over cycles/seasons (votes over time), never a streak leaderboard.

## The 6–8 most useful, buildable mechanisms (ranked)

1. **WOOP goal-creation flow** (Wish→Outcome→Obstacle→if-then Plan). Highest evidence (d≈0.65), inherently warm, names *inner* obstacles. → Planner.
2. **Sankalpa daily intention** — one present-tense line, set each morning, anchored to mood log. → Today.
3. **Identity "votes" instead of streaks** — "3 votes toward becoming a woman who rests well." Sticks better, no guilt. → Garden + Pulse.
4. **Tiny-Habit recipe nudge** — "After I log my mood, I set one intention," with an authentic celebration micro-moment. → Today.
5. **Seasonal/cyclical review loop** (12-Week Year + cycle phases) — plant in follicular, reflect in menstrual; a recurring Garden/Journal ritual, not an annual resolution. → Journal + Garden.
6. **Never-miss-twice gentle re-entry** — plant rests (never wilts); warm re-entry copy. → Garden.
7. **Values pass → identity goals** ("Becoming a woman who…") so goals inherit from clarified values. → Profile.
8. **LINE OF THE DAY** — user-specific omen/line tinted by phase + flora stage; the "little witch" voice surface. → Today.

## Best metaphor fits onto garden/lifecycle
- **Strongest: seasonal/cyclical goals (§5)** — bud→bloom→seed→rest maps 1:1 onto follicular→ovulatory→luteal→menstrual. This is the spine.
- **Strongest #2: identity-as-growth (§3/§4)** — each vote grows the plant; the plant *is* the becoming-self. Replaces the streak entirely.
- **Strongest #3: no-guilt progress (§6)** — "rest, don't wilt" is a native garden behaviour, so self-compassion is built into the visual, not bolted on.

## AVOID (flagged throughout)
- Red streak counters / "you broke your streak" / loss-aversion guilt pushes (§3, §6).
- Rigid SMART template as the *gate* to creating a goal (§2).
- A standalone Goals nav tab — it's the exact new prompt the research says kills adoption (§7).
- Vanity metrics / leaderboards (crowd out intrinsic motivation, §3).
- Over-clinical, prescriptive framing — goals must be self-authored (SDT autonomy, §2) and lighthearted by default (whole-life rule).
- Treating cycle-syncing as hard rule — evidence is practitioner-led; offer as gentle tint only (§5).
