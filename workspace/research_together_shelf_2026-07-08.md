# Research — The "TOGETHER" shelf: do-it-together formats that build belonging in an anonymous women's community — 08/07/2026

## Question
FemWell is designing the "TOGETHER" shelf of its Community — the full range of things women DO together (events/meetups is ONE pillar, already covered in `research_events_together_2026-07-08.md`). This brief gathers cited, live-verified evidence on shared-activity formats (book clubs, watch-alongs, games, group challenges, shared rituals, learning circles, gentle-movement sessions), what drives vs kills participation, async-vs-live tradeoffs for a small anonymous community, how mature communities present a "things to do" hub without a dead feeling, whole-life breadth, and warmth/anti-vanity — then turns it into a ranked buildable set, a strongest-5-7 pillar recommendation, warm mechanics, and a hub structure that never feels empty.

## 1. SHARED-ACTIVITY FORMATS — what makes each work (cited)

**Book clubs / read-alongs.** The proven ASYNC structure is **one thread per chapter-range** (the YA Allegiance club runs 4–5 threads per book, each subject line naming the chapters it covers) so people discuss as they read without spoiler collisions; the rule is **keep the thread's opening message spoiler-free** because that's what lands in notifications, and label later posts "spoilers ahead" (source: https://bookclubs.com/blog/how-to-run-an-online-book-club-discussion). Open prompts ("plot, characters, favourite quotes, predictions — discuss anything from these chapters") beat quiz-style questions. **Async by nature; live calls optional.** Run it well: fixed-length read (e.g. a chapter-range per week), warm host, prompts that invite emotion not comprehension.

**Watch-alongs / co-watching.** Teleparty proves the mechanic: **auto-synchronised playback + a chat rail beside the video** turns solo streaming into "a personal virtual living room" (source: https://www.teleparty.com/ , https://www.teleparty.com/netflix). But sync requires simultaneous presence and a browser extension — heavy for an anonymous mobile app. **The async-friendly version FemWell should copy is the Reddit "live-reaction thread" pattern:** a per-episode thread that fills during air-time but stays warm for anyone who watches later and drops their reactions in. So: **a live "watch it with us tonight" slot AND a persistent per-episode reaction thread.**

**Games together (warm, no leaderboard).** Wordle is the reference: it fulfils the **"ABC of human needs" — autonomy, belongingness, competence** (psych professor Douglas Gentile) precisely because it is **one puzzle a day, the same for everyone, spoiler-free to share, and has NO official leaderboard** — it shows you only your own performance, so play is simultaneous-yet-independent and comparison-free (source: https://www.cnbc.com/2022/02/15/bite-sized-fun-the-psychology-behind-your-sudden-wordle-obsession.html — snippet-corroborated). **The design lesson:** one shared daily thing + spoiler-safe sharing + no scoreboard = belonging without pressure. Games should span life (a style/"which fit" game, a would-you-rather on dating, a two-truths icebreaker), not be cycle quizzes (whole-life rule).

**Challenges / streaks done together.** The evidence is blunt: **competition excludes the ~80% who "know they won't win," and social comparison works short-term but "eventually people quit using things that make them feel chronically guilty and shameful"** (source: https://medium.com/practical-motivation-science/how-to-motivate-with-leaderboards-da5a461fb2f6 ; https://www.commoninja.com/blog/the-psychology-behind-leaderboards). What works instead: **opt-in participation, a non-competitive structure focused on support over leaderboards, and flexible goals** — plus regular check-ins and *shared* progress rather than *ranked* progress (source: https://blog.cohorty.app/group-habit-challenges-for-teams-workplace-wellness-ideas/). Frame the goal as collective ("**we** read 40 chapters this week," "**the room** logged 200 gentle-movement minutes") not individual ranking. Add a **streak-freeze / forgiveness** so a missed day doesn't nuke progress and trigger shame-quitting (streak-freeze pattern: https://streaksapp.com/ , https://habitstreak.me/).

**Co-working / body-doubling.** Already researched — see `research_events_together_2026-07-08.md` §5 (Focusmate/FLOWN/VIDA-for-women: declare your intention → accountability, cameras optional, "no one's looking because everyone's focusing"). Brief pointer only; carry that pattern into Together as a recurring fixed-slot room.

**Cycle-sync / gentle-movement done together.** Best expressed as a **recurring fixed-slot ritual** (the parkrun/VIDA "free + weekly + same-time + non-competitive" formula, per the events brief) — a weekly gentle-movement or wind-down session where the belonging comes from **synchrony**: sharing a common experience produces spontaneous cardiac/emotional synchrony and "a sense of emotional unity" that bonds a group (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7423264/ ; group-walking fusion study https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjso.12723). Keep it optional and never clinical.

**Shared rituals (weekly wind-down, gratitude, "close the week," collective kindness).** Durkheim's **collective effervescence** — "the feeling of excitement and connection felt when a community participates in a collective or synchronous action" — is the deepest belonging lever here, and it's produced by **doing the same small thing at the same rhythm together**, not by conversation (sources above). Async-friendly: a Sunday "close the week" prompt everyone answers in their own time, then sees the aggregate ("the room's word for this week was: *tired-but-hopeful*"). Collective-kindness rituals (a shared gratitude wall, "leave a kind note for a stranger in the room") extend it to relationships/joy domains.

**Learning circles / skill-swaps / crafting-alongs.** P2PU's model is the gold standard: **free, facilitated (not taught) study groups, 3–14 weeks, weekly, running the loop check-in → coursework → group activity → plus/delta**, where "learning with others deepens understanding and builds relationships rooted in shared interests" and the facilitator "doesn't need to be a teacher or subject-matter expert" (source: https://www.p2pu.org/en/ ; https://provlib.org/propagating-promising-practices/learning-circles/ ; https://docs.p2pu.org/facilitation/facilitation-basics). This makes it **buildable by peers, not experts** — a woman can host a "learn watercolours with us" or "CV clinic" circle. Crafting-alongs are the async cousin: a shared project + a photo thread of everyone's progress.

## 2. WHAT DRIVES vs KILLS participation (cited)

**Drives it — relatedness (SDT).** Environments that support **social relatedness** improve motivation and engagement; relatedness mediates the link between presence and outcomes in online communities (source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-74878-4). Belonging is not a nice-to-have on top of the activity — it is the engine of the activity.

**Drives it — commitment devices / the "I'm in" tap.** A public "I'm in" is an **identity claim**: "walking it back carries a cost to your self-concept," and it works best when **specific** ("I'll join the Tuesday circle") not vague; **written commitments outperform verbal ones by 23%** (writing forces clarity + leaves a record) (source: https://finestreak.com/blog/commitment-devices-that-work). BUT a cited caution: for **identity-based goals (e.g. career)**, going too public can *backfire* — so keep the commitment gentle and to the small group, not broadcast to a scoreboard.

**Drives it — the "we" effect / synchrony.** Shared, synchronous experience produces emotional synchrony and identity-fusion with the group (sources §1). Aggregate "we did X" signals convert a solo action into a communal one.

**Kills it — leaderboards, ranking, competition.** Non-competitive learners are actively *demotivated* by leaderboards; the losing side disengages; comparison drives long-term quitting (sources: CommonNinja; Habitry, above). **No public scoreboards anywhere on the Together shelf.**

**Kills it — flaking shame / all-or-nothing streaks.** Chronic guilt makes people quit the product entirely (Habitry). Fix with forgiveness (streak-freeze), soft RSVP states, and never a "you flaked" surface (carry the events brief's soft-RSVP rule).

**Kills it — dead / empty rooms.** "No one wants to be the only person dancing in an empty ballroom" — the cost of starting a conversation in a dead room feels too high (source: https://cmgr.live/blog/the-ghost-town-myth-why-you-cant-post-your-way-out-of-a-dead-community/). This is the single biggest risk for a small anonymous community and gets its own section (§5).

## 3. ASYNC vs LIVE — making "together" work without guaranteed simultaneous presence

A small anonymous community **cannot promise a crowd is online right now**, so the default must be **async-first with live as a bonus**. Crucially, **shared experience bonds people even without real-time interaction** — Wordle is asynchronous-yet-simultaneous (same puzzle, play any time) and still creates "a unifying force… a sense of community" (sources §1). The Royal Society work frames bonding as driven by **shared emotional intensity**, not necessarily co-presence (source: https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/11/10/240048/92251/ — abstract-corroborated).

**Buildable async "together" mechanics (make it feel communal without live presence):**
- **Shared daily/weekly artefact** — one book chapter, one puzzle, one ritual prompt, same for everyone → "we're all on the same page today."
- **Threaded reactions that stay warm** — per-chapter / per-episode / per-prompt threads that accrue over hours-days, so a latecomer still lands in a lively room (book-club + Reddit-reaction pattern).
- **Aggregate "here's what the room did" signals** — "the room read 40 chapters this week," "37 women joined tonight's wind-down," "the room's word for the week: *hopeful*." Communal proof without demanding you be present at 8pm.
- **Fuzzy "we" counts, never exact/vanity numbers** — "a cosy group did this" / "filling up" (carry the anti-vanity rule from the events brief §4).

**Formats that genuinely NEED live presence:** synced watch-parties (Teleparty-style), live audio/video circles, body-doubling rooms. Ship these as **fixed-slot recurring rituals** (the parkrun formula) so presence is *scheduled and predictable* — the way you manufacture simultaneity in a small community is a **standing appointment**, not hoping people coincide.

## 4. HOW MATURE COMMUNITIES PRESENT A "THINGS TO DO TOGETHER" HUB

- **Mighty Networks** organises everything into **Spaces** (rooms with their own chat/feed/events/course), with **Challenge and Habit-Tracker templates** as first-class space types, a **member activity feed** on login (Facebook-like, so people interact without hunting), **polls & questions** ("more comments and reactions than any other content type"), **livestream** ("the heartbeat"), and **events** ("give members a reason to show up"). Their headline framing is agency, not chores: **"One place to go. 84% member-led."** and a **step-by-step welcome checklist** used by "99% of profitable communities" to convert new members (source: https://www.mightynetworks.com/community). **Lesson: name it by agency ("things to do together"), surface an activity feed, seed with polls/questions, and give newcomers a welcome checklist.**
- **Geneva** = a **"home" landing page + "rooms"**, mixing **5 room types (chat / post / async-feed / audio / video / broadcast)** so a community can be synchronous AND asynchronous in one place, with **Gates** (join questionnaires) controlling who gets in (source: https://www.geneva.com/about ; https://mrhack.io/geneva-group-chat-app-full-overview/). **Lesson: one hub, multiple *modes* of togetherness (not just chat); gate entry for safety.**
- **Reddit / Discord** = persistent topic threads + scheduled live threads (the async-warm + fixed-slot-live combination FemWell should copy for watch-alongs and rituals).
- **Peanut** = live audio rooms by topic for women (precedent already logged in the talk-rooms brief) — reuse that infra for live circles.

**How they avoid a dead feeling:** an **activity feed that aggregates everything** (so even a quiet day shows *something*), **member-led seeding** (84% member-led on Mighty), **welcome checklists**, and **scheduled recurring events** as reliable anchors. But note the strongest counter-lesson (§5): more channels ≠ more life.

## 5. HOW TO STRUCTURE THE TOGETHER HUB SO IT NEVER FEELS EMPTY

The hard truth: **"you cannot fix a structural collapse with better marketing… we confused infrastructure with intimacy… digital mansions too big to clean and too empty to feel cozy"** — silence is a *structure* problem, not a *content* problem (source: https://cmgr.live/blog/the-ghost-town-myth-why-you-cant-post-your-way-out-of-a-dead-community/). Concrete rules for FemWell:

1. **Start small, not sprawling.** Do NOT launch 30 rooms for 10 members. Ship a **tight set of 5–7 pillars** (below), each with a live pulse, before adding more. "You can't manufacture the intimate energy of a small, tight-knit group once you've lost it" (source: https://communityroundtable.com/best-practices/seeding-content-in-your-community/).
2. **Aggregate feed = the hub's front door.** One "what the room is doing together" feed (Mighty's activity-feed lesson) so a quiet moment still shows the book-club thread, tonight's wind-down, the weekly ritual result — never an empty grid of rooms.
3. **Seed relentlessly early.** 3–5 prompts/week, recruit champion hosts, keep time-to-first-response low (sources above). FemWell can seed via editorial + Jess-authored gentle prompts so no room ever shows zero.
4. **Async warmth as the floor.** Because you can't guarantee live presence, every pillar must have an **always-on async thread** that stays warm, plus **aggregate "we" signals**, so a solo visitor at 2am still feels the room is inhabited (§3).
5. **Fixed-slot rituals as the heartbeat.** A predictable weekly appointment (wind-down, co-working hour, book-club chapter) gives the hub a reliable pulse (parkrun formula).
6. **Never show emptiness as a number.** No "0 online," no "1 going." Use fuzzy warmth ("a small group forming") — an honest zero is a self-fulfilling ghost town.
7. **A central "Jump to" switcher** (app-wide multi-layer rule) so any pillar is one tap from anywhere in Together.

## 6. WHOLE-LIFE BREADTH (not clinical)

Every pillar must span life domains, health-tint optional:
- **Book club / read-along** — fiction, memoir, career, romance — not "menopause reading list" only.
- **Watch-along** — a rom-com, a reality show, a documentary; live-reaction gossip = the "community/venting" domain.
- **Games** — style/"which fit," dating would-you-rather, two-truths, trivia — joy/fun, relationships, fashion.
- **Challenges** — "read 20 mins a day," "one creative thing a week," "text a friend you miss," "no-spend week" (money), "learn 5 words of a language" — career, money, friendship, creativity, hobbies.
- **Rituals** — Sunday "close the week," gratitude, collective-kindness notes — identity, joy, relationships.
- **Learning circles / skill-swaps** — watercolours, CV clinic, budgeting, sourdough, a language — career, money, hobbies, creativity.
- **Gentle-movement / wind-down** — the one health-tinted pillar; keep it optional and warm.

## 8. RECOMMENDED STRONGEST SET (6 pillars) for FemWell's Together shelf

1. **Weekly Rituals** — "close the week," gratitude, collective-kindness. *Why: deepest belonging (collective effervescence), lowest build, pure async, zero creds — the reliable heartbeat.*
2. **Read-Along (async book/read club)** — per-chapter threads, spoiler-safe. *Why: proven async structure, spans life beyond health, reuses thread infra.*
3. **Together Challenges** — collective-goal, no leaderboard, streak-freeze, gentle "I'm in." *Why: motivating without shame; the "we did X" aggregate is pure belonging fuel.*
4. **Daily Game** — one shared warm prompt/puzzle a day, spoiler-safe, no scoreboard. *Why: Wordle-proven habit + belonging; whole-life prompts keep it non-clinical.*
5. **Watch-Along** — persistent per-episode reaction threads (+ optional live slot). *Why: gossip/joy/entertainment domain; async-warm so it never needs a crowd online.*
6. **Live Circles** — fixed-slot recurring rooms for gentle-movement/wind-down AND learning/skill-swap. *Why: scheduled presence = manufactured simultaneity; synchrony bonding; covers wellness AND career/hobbies.*

(Events/meetups is the 7th pillar — already fully specced in `research_events_together_2026-07-08.md`. The Together shelf = these 6 + Events.)

## 9. WARM MECHANICS (8, whole-life, anti-vanity)

1. **Gentle "I'm in" commitment** — one warm tap that's a small identity claim ("I'll join Tuesday's circle"), specific not vague, visible only to the small group, never a scoreboard (commitment-device evidence; source: https://finestreak.com/blog/commitment-devices-that-work).
2. **Aggregate "we" signal, never a rank** — "the room read 40 chapters this week," "37 joined tonight's wind-down," "the room's word: *hopeful*." Belonging without comparison.
3. **Streak-freeze / forgiveness** — a missed day never breaks the shared streak or surfaces shame; "life happens" copy (streak-freeze pattern; source: https://streaksapp.com/).
4. **Fuzzy warmth counts** — "a cosy group forming," "first-timers welcome, you won't be the only new one" — never "0 online" or exact vanity numbers (anti-ghost-town; §5).
5. **Spoiler-safe sharing** — book/game/watch shares carry no spoilers by default (Wordle + book-club rule), so joining late is safe and pressure-free.
6. **First-timer welcome + welcome checklist** — Mighty's step-by-step onboarding; a "your first together thing" nudge so a newcomer has one easy door (source: https://www.mightynetworks.com/community).
7. **"Dip a toe" async door on every live thing** — nervous users can drop an async reaction to a ritual/watch-along without showing up live (two-ways-in, carried from events brief).
8. **No-flake, soft states everywhere** — "I'm in / maybe / can't this time," changeable, penalty-free; no "you missed it" surface (carry soft-RSVP from events brief §4).
